# MCP tools

Generated by `node scripts/mcp-tools-doc.mjs` from the server's own tool list; the same list is in the app under Settings › Connect an AI assistant (MCP).

61 tools. All read only, except those marked **(changes projects)**, which are listed only when project changes are allowed (the shared access token, or a sign-in that ticked "Also let it change projects"). `site_plan` saves to a project only when given `project_id`.

## Property, site and area data (21)

- **`list_datasets`** — List datasets. Every dataset this server reads: what it covers, how fresh it is, where it comes from (with a link) and which tools use it. Call it first to see what can be answered where.
- **`find_property`** — Find a property. Resolve a location (address, APN + county, coordinates, county, box or polygon) to coordinates with the geocoder’s match quality, its county, whether it is inside city limits or near enough to a city to be in its extraterritorial jurisdiction (ETJ, 0.5 mile rule), and the free county appraisal parcel: owner, situs address, APN, land use, acreage, market / land / improvement values, year built, acquisition date, legal description and the parcel outline as GeoJSON. Texas parcels only.
- **`site_facts`** — Site facts. One call for most facts about a Texas property: its parcel, city or ETJ, FEMA flood zone at the point, TxDOT traffic counts on the roads beside it, water / utility district (MUD, WCID), Houston TIRZ, Opportunity Zone, school district, METRO stops, EPA-regulated facilities within ¼ mile, Houston crime within ½ mile, alcohol sales at the address, and the census tract (population, growth, income, home value, rent, vacancy, jobs, spending).
- **`flood`** — Flood and hazard. FEMA flood facts: the zone at the point (with base flood elevation where published), each flood risk class’s share of the parcel or area (high 100-year, moderate 500-year, minimal; floodway), distance to the nearest high-risk zone, NFIP flood insurance claims paid in the surrounding census tracts (by year and by storm such as Harvey), federal disaster declarations for its counties since 2000, and FEMA’s National Risk Index (overall rating, expected annual loss by hazard). Whole US. For a point it uses the parcel there, else a ¼ mile circle.
- **`terrain`** — Terrain. Ground elevations over a parcel or area for site design: minimum, maximum and mean elevation (NAVD 88 ft, bare ground), relief, the share of the site in slope bands (0–2, 2–5, 5–10, 10–15, over 15 %), the general drainage direction and fall, low channels, and the data source and resolution (USGS 3DEP lidar). For a point it uses the parcel there. Sites up to 5 miles across. Planning numbers, not a survey.
- **`environmental`** — Environmental screen. The Environmental Report’s data as a summary: TCEQ Superfund, hazardous-waste and voluntary cleanups, brownfields, dry cleaners, leaking and registered petroleum tanks, landfills, groundwater restrictions; EPA Superfund, toxic release, hazardous waste, air and water permits; Railroad Commission oil and gas wells, orphan wells and pipelines; wetlands; and the soils under the site (drainage, flooding, building ratings). Counts within Phase I style search distances of the site’s edge, what is on the site, and the nearest records. Texas. For a point it uses the parcel there, else a 0.1 mile circle.
- **`utilities`** — Utilities. Utilities near a Texas site: nearest mapped power lines (transmission and distribution, voltage, operator) and substations, who holds the water and sewer service areas (PUC CCNs), water facility lines, and City of Houston water and sewer mains (size, material) with distances from the parcel edge.
- **`traffic_counts`** — Traffic counts. TxDOT annual average daily traffic (vehicles a day, both directions) for an area in Texas: the busiest roads, average and busiest count by road type, and the TxDOT file date. Default ½ mile around a point. Counts only (no live speeds).
- **`demographics`** — Demographics. US Census ACS figures for rings around a place (default 1, 3 and 5 miles), a county or a polygon: population and growth, households, median household income, home value and rent (approx. where tracts are combined), vacancy, median age, households by income band, jobs located there (LEHD LODES) and estimated consumer spending by category. Home counties only.
- **`market_context`** — Market context. County-level market figures (the app’s Market view) for a home county, the county a location is in, or "all" for the region: population and growth, jobs and top industries, unemployment, housing units permitted by year and year to date, new business locations per month and the newest ones, city sales tax collected, modeled consumer spending, Zillow rents, home loans, interest rates, the construction pipeline from TDLR filings and local development news.
- **`businesses`** — Businesses and places. Businesses and places in the home counties (Overture Maps and Foursquare open data): search by name (closest to a location first), the nearest ones to a point (optionally one category), and counts by category in an area.
- **`businesses_at_address`** — Registered businesses at an address. Businesses registered at a Texas street address from the Texas Comptroller’s active sales-tax permits: retail, restaurant and service tenants, their legal owner, suite and the date the location’s permit was issued. Offices and medical practices mostly don’t hold one.
- **`company_lookup`** — Company lookup. Who is behind a company (LLC, corporation, partnership) that owns property. Texas: the Comptroller’s franchise tax record live (status, formation, Secretary of State file number, registered agent, mailing address, officers / managers from its latest Public Information Report). Every state whose registry is loaded (Texas, New York, Colorado, Connecticut, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Florida, DC): the matching entities with status, kind, formation date and state, registered agent and address. Give state to search one registry. Companies only: people’s names are not looked up.
- **`development_activity`** — Development activity. Development activity from the app’s nightly data: building and trade permits (every Harris County permit, and about 240 city and county open-data portals in 37 states: kind, value, contractor where published), and in the home counties also plats and new subdivisions, TxDOT road projects, water districts / MUDs (creation date, tax rate) and public schools (enrollment). Counts by kind and category and the newest rows. Default 1 mile around a point.
- **`construction_filings`** — Construction filings. Texas TDLR TABS construction registrations (every commercial or public project over $50K must register: an early signal of what is being built, by whom, where and when). Filter by area, county, registration dates, value, square feet, use, type and owner / developer / architect / contractor; returns totals (count, value, square feet, units, by use, type and city) and the top rows. Statewide. A point uses a 2-mile circle unless radius_mi is given.
- **`top_companies`** — Top companies. Developers / owners, architects and general contractors ranked by construction activity (TDLR filings: number and estimated value) in an area or county, with each one’s largest project. Takes the same filters as construction_filings.
- **`crime_near`** — Crime near a place (Houston). Crime from Houston Police incident reports (NIBRS) for a radius or area of Houston: the 12 months through the latest month published (about three months behind) vs the 12 before, violent / property / other, incidents per square mile vs the citywide rate, per 1,000 residents, top offenses, premises and monthly counts. City of Houston only (outside it the answer says so; use crime_by_agency). Default ½ mile around a point.
- **`crime_by_agency`** — Crime by police department. Yearly crime for the police department covering a location anywhere in the US (or one named, or by FBI agency ID), from the FBI Crime Data Explorer: violent and property crimes per 100,000 for the newest full year against the state and the US, the change from the year before, the share solved, the offense mix, rates by year, its rank among the state’s departments of the same kind, and the other departments in the county. With rankings: true, the state’s departments ranked by crime rate.
- **`news`** — Local and project news. Local and development news the app has stored: headlines for a home county or one of its busiest towns (searched nightly), and articles found for construction filings that mention a project or company.
- **`airports_near`** — Airports near a place. The nearest public airports to a location (OurAirports data): codes, type, distance, scheduled service and runways (length, surface), and whether the place sits under a runway’s approach or departure path. No live flights.
- **`area_overview`** — Area overview. One call for most of an area: demographics (1 mile around a point, or the area), construction filings in the last 12 months, development activity counts, businesses by category, Houston crime where covered, and the county’s market headline. Use the specific tools for detail.

## Reports (PDF) (9)

- **`report_flood`** — FEMA flood & hazard report (PDF). The website’s FEMA flood & hazard report as a PDF, for a property (its parcel), a radius, a county or a polygon: flood zone shares, floodway and base flood elevation, NFIP claims by year and storm, disaster declarations and FEMA’s National Risk Index, on a street map, in the Real Estate Intelligence report style. Returns a short summary and the PDF as an embedded resource (report://flood/…pdf); include_csv adds the data as CSV. Whole US.
- **`report_environmental`** — Environmental report (PDF). The website’s Environmental screening report as a PDF for a Texas property (its parcel), radius, county or polygon: cleanup and contamination sites, tanks, permitted facilities, oil and gas wells, pipelines, wetlands and soils within Phase I style search distances, on a street map. Returns a short summary and the PDF as an embedded resource (report://environmental/…pdf); include_csv adds every record listed as CSV. A screen of public records, not a Phase I.
- **`report_crime`** — Houston crime report (PDF). The website’s Crime report as a PDF for an area of Houston (default ½ mile around a point): 12 months vs the 12 before, per square mile vs the city, per resident, monthly and time-of-day charts, year by year, top offenses and premises, the incidents on a street map and listed newest first. City of Houston only (outside it the answer says so). Returns a summary and the PDF (report://crime/…pdf); include_csv adds the incidents as CSV.
- **`report_crime_agency`** — City / County Crime Report (PDF). The website’s City / County Crime Report as a PDF for the police department covering a location anywhere in the US (or one FBI agency ID): rates per 100,000 against the state and the US, trends, year by year and the offense mix, from the FBI Crime Data Explorer. Returns a summary and the PDF (report://crime_agency/…pdf); include_csv adds the years as CSV.
- **`report_demographics`** — Demographics report (PDF). A demographics report PDF for a property: 1, 3 and 5-mile rings (or the radii given) with population and growth, households, income, home value, rent, vacancy, age, households by income, jobs located there and estimated consumer spending by category, on a street map with the rings, in the Real Estate Intelligence report style. For a county or a drawn area it reports the area as one. Returns a summary and the PDF (report://demographics/…pdf); include_csv adds the figures as CSV. Home counties only.
- **`report_market`** — Market report (PDF). The Market report as a PDF for a home county (or the county a location is in), several counties, or "all" for the region: what is moving, the construction pipeline (under construction by month, by use, the largest projects, what starts in 90 days), housing permits, new businesses, jobs by industry, unemployment, city sales tax, consumer spending, census housing and incomes, Zillow rents by ZIP, the Houston for-sale market, material prices and rates, Houston crime, the busiest roads (TxDOT) and local news. A static print version of the Market page’s interactive charts. Returns a summary and the PDF (report://market/…pdf); include_csv adds the numbers as CSV.
- **`site_report`** — Site reports for a parcel (PDF, GeoJSON). The one-click reports on a parcel’s card in the website, for the parcel at an address, APN (with county) or point: parcel_map (the parcel to scale with every line’s length, corners, bearings, an area check and the site facts), topography (USGS lidar contours, shaded relief, a 3D view of the ground), site_environmental (the desktop environmental report with a site and constraints map, soils and records), area_report (1, 3, 5 and 10-mile rings: people, income, rents, jobs, spending, construction filed) or parcel_outline (the outline as GeoJSON with its record). Unbranded PDFs. Example: { "address": "1004 Priya Ln, Waller, TX 77484", "kind": "parcel_map" }.
- **`report_filings`** — Construction filing reports (PDF, CSV). The website’s Export › New report files from TDLR construction filings, for an area (a point uses 2 miles unless radius_mi), a county or a company, with the same filters as construction_filings: summary (headline figures, map, by county, type and use, largest filings), filing_list (every filing, landscape), activity (the most active developers, architects and contractors) or area_comparison (areas: two to six locations side by side). PDF; include_csv adds the rows (the website’s Excel).
- **`report_traffic`** — Traffic report (PDF). The website’s Traffic report for an area in Texas (a point uses 1 mile unless radius_mi): the busiest roads and every counted segment on a map (TxDOT vehicles a day), traffic by road type, and live speeds and incidents where the live traffic service is set up. PDF; include_csv adds the segments.

## Site plans and layouts (15)

- **`site_plan`** — Site plan (layout + PDF sheet). A concept site plan for a Texas parcel, several parcels (apns, merged as one site) or a drawn outline (geojson), laid out by the app’s own engine: buildings, parking, drives, detention and landscaping within setbacks, the front and side streets from the roads beside it; inside the City of Waller or its ETJ under its planning guardrails (setbacks, the city’s parking count, detention by volume). request says what to build in words: uses and sizes, building types ("a 120-key hotel and 20,000 sf of retail"; building_types lists them), where things go ("parking behind the buildings", "pool in the center"), angled parking ("60 degree parking"); omit it for the AI’s best-use ideas (idea picks another). effort low (seconds, words read directly), medium (default, 1–2 min: the AI plans, reads and reviews) or high (2–4 min, may revise once). Returns the layouts compared, the pick and why, its quality check (READY, READY WITH NOTES or NOT READY: check it before passing the plan on), each ask as done or not met, a plan_id for the plan_ tools, and the sheet PDF (11 × 17 or 24 × 36). Home communities (tiny, manufactured, RV-style): home_type, pad_size, home_size or home_sf, homes, buffer_ft. Concept only, not for permitting.
- **`layout_run`** — Layout run (reproducible, validated). Lays out a site with the app’s engine from a typed design brief (schema rei.brief/1) or a request with an outline (geojson lon/lat, or ring_ft in feet), deterministically: the same brief, seed and versions always give the same geometry, quantities and ranking. Every option is checked by an independent validator (containment, setbacks, overlaps, access, circulation, parking, the program, locks): pass / fail / unknown with the rule each rests on. Outcome says feasible, needs-review, no-feasible-found (search budget, not a proof), proven-infeasible, unsupported (a form the engine does not draw: see layout_capabilities) or missing-information. Returns the run record (JSON resource) for layout_replay and layout_edit. No AI, no network. Drainage, turning, fire route distance, grading and utilities are reported unknown.
- **`layout_replay`** — Replay a layout run. Re-runs a saved layout_run record from its own brief (no AI, no new data) and says whether the outcome, geometry, quantities, checks and ranking are identical, and what differs if not (e.g. a newer engine version).
- **`layout_edit`** — Edit a layout run. Changes a saved layout run with typed edits (lock / unlock, move, keep-entrance, regenerate, set-quantity, set-form, compare-forms, set-office-share, place) or words ("keep the buildings and move the pool to the center", "keep the northwest entrance and regenerate the rest", "keep 240 units but compare garden apartments with townhomes"). Applied as one transaction and re-validated: locked pieces never move, and an edit that would break a hard check or a lock is not applied and the conflicts are listed. The record keeps the steps, so it replays and undoes exactly.
- **`layout_capabilities`** — Layout capabilities. Which building and site forms the layout engine draws, by family (multifamily, retail, industrial, flex, mixed use), and how far each is supported: implemented, validated, partial (with the simplification) or unsupported.
- **`building_types`** — Building types. The building types the layout engine draws (apartments of every form, townhomes, plexes, retail, restaurants, office and medical, hotels by keys, senior and student housing, daycare, schools, storage, industrial, flex, data centres, car washes, banks…): id, name, what it counts (units, keys, beds, children, students) and where its sizes come from. With id: its shape, defaults and usual size, and how to ask for it in site_plan words or add one with plan_edit (btype).
- **`plan_get`** — Open a saved plan. Opens a saved site plan by plan_id: the site, the request, the layout and its quality check, the numbers (floor area, units and unit mix, parking, coverage, open space, detention), every piece with its id, name, kind, position (feet on the sheet: the street at the bottom) and size, which pieces are locked, the places that can be named in plan_edit, the run’s other layouts (plan_pick) and the plan’s history. include_record adds the whole plan as JSON. Example: { "plan_id": "Ab3xY9_k2LmQ" }.
- **`plan_edit`** — Edit a saved plan. Changes a saved site plan as the website’s Change With AI does and saves it as a NEW plan_id (the plan before is kept). Words: "move the clubhouse to the entry", "make Apt Bldg 3 four stories", "swap Bldg 2 and Bldg 4", "add a playground beside the pool", "lock the pool", "add a hotel with 120 keys at the front", "set parking angle 60", "front setback 40 ft". Or typed edits, e.g. { "action": "move", "target": "Clubhouse", "to": "entrance" }, { "action": "change", "every": "multifamily", "floors": 3 }, { "action": "add", "btype": "hotel-limited", "keys": 120, "to": "front" }, { "action": "lock", "target": "Pool" }. settings (setbacks, parking ratio and angle, entrances, the jurisdiction guardrails) lay the site out again with them, locked pieces kept. Targets are names or ids from plan_get. refit: true lays the rest out again round a change (kept only if nothing is lost). Answers with what was done and not, numbers before → after, the asks met or not, and the quality check.
- **`plan_pick`** — Pick another layout. Makes one of the run’s other layouts (plan_get lists them: 2, 3…) the plan, as picking a layout card on the website does. Saved as a new plan_id. Example: { "plan_id": "Ab3xY9_k2LmQ", "layout": 2 }.
- **`plan_compare`** — Compare plans. Sets saved plans side by side (up to 6): units, floor area, parking needed and drawn, coverage, open space, the quality check, the independent check and a quick value and profit at the app’s market assumptions, with what changed against the first. Give a plan and the plans edited from it to see what an edit did, or one plan with include_alternatives for its run’s layouts. Example: { "plan_ids": ["Ab3xY9_k2LmQ", "Qz7Pd2_aa1Bc"] }.
- **`plan_check`** — Check a plan. The independent check of a saved plan (the website’s Numbers › Independent check): measured from the drawing itself, not the engine’s numbers: inside the property line, setbacks, nothing on easements or keep-outs, no overlaps at the same level, a way in from a street, every drive and parking row connected, parking required against drawn, the size asked for, units against each building; each pass, fail or unknown with what was measured and what it needs. Then the planner’s quality check (READY, READY WITH NOTES or NOT READY: fire lanes, dead ends, second access, homes facing a drive…) and the jurisdiction’s checks. Example: { "plan_id": "Ab3xY9_k2LmQ" }.
- **`plan_numbers`** — Plan numbers and pro forma. A saved plan’s numbers as the website’s Numbers and Pro forma steps show them: site acres, floor area and FAR, leasable area, units and density, the apartment unit mix, parking drawn and required, coverage, open space, detention, buildings by use; and the pro forma at the app’s market assumptions (total cost, NOI, yield on cost, value, profit, levered IRR), land at its default unless land_price is given. For the full feasibility study, save it to a project and run feasibility_study. Example: { "plan_id": "Ab3xY9_k2LmQ", "land_price": 2400000 }.
- **`plan_export`** — Export a plan. A saved plan as files: pdf (the conceptual site plan sheet, 11 × 17 or 24 × 36, as the website’s Site Plan PDF), svg (the drawing), image (a JPEG of the drawing), dxf (CAD, feet, with layers), geojson (lon/lat, every piece with its use and size), json (the plan record) and bim (the BIM model script; IFC too where the BIM tool is installed). Files come back as resources. For a full set of plans use plan_set; for photoreal views site_renderings. Example: { "plan_id": "Ab3xY9_k2LmQ", "formats": ["pdf", "dxf"], "sheet": "24x36" }.
- **`plan_from_project`** — A project option as a plan. Takes a project’s site option (the study’s option unless option_id names another) as a saved plan, with the project’s parcels and site facts, so plan_get, plan_edit, plan_check, plan_numbers, plan_export, plan_set and site_renderings work on it. The project isn’t changed; save_plan_to_project puts an edited plan back as a new option. Example: { "project_id": "m1abc23de", "option_id": "B" }.
- **`save_plan_to_project`** — Save a plan to a project **(changes projects)**. Saves a plan (a plan_id from site_plan, plan_edit, plan_pick or plan_from_project) to a project as its next site option, as the website’s Save as new option does, and by default makes it the option the feasibility study uses. Then feasibility_study and feasibility_report work on it. Example: { "plan_id": "Ab3xY9_k2LmQ", "project_id": "m1abc23de" }. A plan made with plan_from_project goes back to its own project when project_id is left out.

## Plan sets and renderings (3)

- **`plan_set`** — Plan set (schematic design drawings). A full schematic design set of plans (24 × 36, Real Estate Intelligence title block) from a saved site plan: give its plan_id (from site_plan, plan_edit, plan_pick or plan_from_project). As the website’s Make Plan Set: G, C, L, A (per building type: plans, elevations, sections, schedules), S, M, P, E, FP / FA / LS; fuel (F) with a gas station, telecom (T) and renderings (A-9xx) only when asked. Claude writes the notes and designs each building type (checked by code); drawings are code from the plan. A set takes minutes, so this starts a job, works on it up to about four minutes and answers with a job_id and progress; call plan_set_status until it answers with a download link (24 hours) and, when small, the PDF. Preliminary: not for construction, bidding or permit.
- **`plan_set_status`** — Plan set: carry on and check. Carries on a plan set plan_set started (up to about four minutes a call; nothing happens between calls) and says how far it is. When finished: the sheet list, which designs are Claude’s and which rule-based, a download link (24 hours, new each call) and, when small, the PDF. Waits up to wait_seconds when another call is working on the job.
- **`site_renderings`** — Site renderings (photoreal views). Photorealistic renderings of a saved site plan (give its plan_id: from site_plan, plan_edit, plan_pick or plan_from_project): its 3D massing drawn from a camera preset and painted into a photograph by the image model, as the website’s Render does. Up to 4 views a call, about a minute each (default corner). surround none: the site alone like a presentation model. Returns JPEGs (or 24-hour links when too big for the answer), kept with the plan for plan_set’s rendering sheets. Concept illustrations: placement and heights follow the plan, materials and surroundings are invented. Inside the monthly renderings budget.

## Projects and feasibility (11)

- **`upload_project_file`** — Add a reference file to a project **(changes projects)**. Adds a reference file (a survey, plat, floor plan or earlier site plan: PDF, PNG, JPG, DXF or text, up to 3 MB) to a project’s Files tab, in the team’s own storage, and has the AI read it into facts (setbacks, easements, areas…) for a person to confirm there.
- **`list_projects`** — List projects. The team’s projects (the website’s Projects page): name, project_id, status, site and who changed it last, newest change first. Filter by words in the name, client, site, county or notes, and by status.
- **`get_project`** — Get a project. One project in full: status, notes, parcels (APN, owner, acres, land and market value), the site facts gathered for it (city or ETJ, zoning, flood zone, ground, utilities, districts, roads), each site option with its numbers and checks, the feasibility inputs (land price, assumption changes, budget edits, scenarios) and saved report sets, reference files with their confirmed facts, and its link on the website.
- **`feasibility_study`** — Feasibility study. Runs the website’s feasibility study for a project (its Feasibility tab): a CSI budget priced from the layout’s quantities, the monthly development pro forma, returns, break-even, hold analysis, sensitivity, scenarios and risks, every input with its source. what_if tries changes without saving them (set_assumptions saves). Pick sections: summary, assumptions (every key with value, range and source), budget (every line_id), csi, cashflow, hold, sensitivity, scenarios, risks, register. Default summary, assumptions and risks. Feasibility level only: costs and rents are labelled estimates, not bids or comps.
- **`feasibility_report`** — Feasibility report set (PDF, Excel, CSV, JSON). The project’s feasibility report set, as the workspace’s Reports step makes it: the Feasibility report PDF (summary, site and layout drawing, plan set index, budget by CSI division, pro forma, hold, sensitivity, scenarios, risks and the fact & assumption register), the Excel workbook, the budget and register CSVs and the study JSON. files picks what to attach (default the PDF). save: true also stores the whole set for the team (write: needs the server’s access token) and returns download links.
- **`project_files`** — Project files and links. Download links (good for 10 minutes) for a project’s saved report sets, its reference files (surveys, floor plans, earlier site plans) and its renderings and plan set PDFs.
- **`create_project`** — Create a project **(changes projects)**. Starts a project as the website does from a property card: the parcel at an address or point, an APN (with county), several APNs merged as one site, or a drawn outline (geojson). Keeps the parcels (owner, APN, acres, appraised values), lays the site outline down as option A with its front street, and gathers the site facts (city or ETJ, flood zone, ground, utilities, districts, roads; about 30–60 s, gather_facts: false skips them). Returns its project_id; then site_plan with project_id lays out a plan in it.
- **`update_project`** — Update a project **(changes projects)**. Changes a project’s name, status (prospect, review, contract, owned, hold, dead: there is no delete), client, notes (replace, or add_note to append a dated line), the land price for the study, the target use, which site option the feasibility study uses, and its active scenario.
- **`set_assumptions`** — Set feasibility assumptions **(changes projects)**. Saves changes to the study’s assumptions (rents, vacancy, opex, cost factors, fees, rates, loan terms, cap rates, growth, schedule) on the project or on one scenario, or puts them back to their sourced defaults. Answers with the headline before and after. Keys and units: feasibility_study with sections ["assumptions"].
- **`edit_budget`** — Edit budget lines **(changes projects)**. Changes budget lines the way the workspace’s Budget step does: a line’s rate or quantity, leave it out (off) or put it back, or reset it to the library’s figure. line_ids: feasibility_study with sections ["budget"]. Answers with the contract and project cost before and after.
- **`save_scenario`** — Save a scenario **(changes projects)**. Adds or changes a named scenario (e.g. "Rates +1%", "Costs +10%", "More one-bedrooms"): assumption changes on top of the base case and, for apartments, a different unit mix. Can make it the active one, or remove it. Answers with it beside the base case.

## For ChatGPT connectors (2)

- **`search`** — Search projects and properties. Search for a ChatGPT-style connector: finds the team’s projects by words in the name, client, site, county or notes, and resolves an address ("1004 Priya Ln, Waller, TX"), an APN with its county ("APN 123456 Waller") or coordinates ("30.05, -95.92") to a property. Returns {"results":[{"id","title","url"}]}; give an id to fetch for the full text. The other tools answer each question in more detail; prefer them when you can call them.
- **`fetch`** — Fetch a search result. Full text for an id from search: "project:<project_id>" gives the project in full (as get_project), "property:<lat>,<lon>" the site facts for that property (as site_facts). Returns {"id","title","text","url","metadata"}.
