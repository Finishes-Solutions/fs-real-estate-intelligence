# Real Estate Intelligence Platform: Product Guide

A plain-language guide to what the app does and how to use it. It's written for the people who use the app, not the people who build it, and it's updated as new features ship. For a dated list of every change, see `CHANGELOG.md`.

---

## What it is

A map-based research tool for Finishes Solutions, built around four things, in this order:

1. **Property information:** click any parcel or building to see who owns it, what it's worth, its zoning, site risks, utilities and what's around it.
2. **Site plans:** lay out a building, parking and setbacks on a parcel, by hand or with AI, compare options with their numbers and a pro forma, and keep them in **Projects**.
3. **Area data:** the local market, demographics and map layers (parcels, flood zones, demographics, traffic, satellite and more) for any area you pick.
4. **Reports:** flood, environmental, crime, traffic, drive-time and market reports on an area, ready to download or share.

Construction filings (the state's register of commercial building projects) are there too, as a supporting data set: on the map, in the Map panel and on the **Filings** pages.

**Where it works**

| What | Coverage |
|---|---|
| Property, owner and value lookups | Texas |
| Census demographics, jobs, market numbers | Waller County and the six counties around it (Harris, Fort Bend, Montgomery, Austin, Washington, Grimes), plus the next ring of counties for context |
| Construction filings (TDLR) | The same 7 counties, last two years |
| Building permits | Harris County only (every city in it and the unincorporated county), last two years |
| Plats and new subdivisions | City of Houston and its ETJ (with dates); recorded subdivisions in Fort Bend (with dates), Montgomery, Waller, Austin and Washington |
| TxDOT projects, water districts (MUDs), schools | The same 7 counties |
| Crime, street level (each incident) | City of Houston only |
| Crime by city / county (yearly, per police department) | The whole US |
| Flood zones | Anywhere FEMA has mapped (zoom in) |
| Traffic counts | Texas roads (TxDOT) |
| County and state lines | The whole US |
| Weather, drive times, planes, airports | Anywhere in the world |

## Getting started

- Open the site in any modern browser on a computer, tablet or phone.
- **Install it like an app:** on a computer (Chrome or Edge) or an Android phone, open **Settings → App → Install app** when it's offered, or use the install button in the browser's address bar. On an iPhone, use Safari's Share → Add to Home Screen. It opens in its own window and keeps working with a weak signal; data refreshes when you're back online.
- The map opens on the home region. The **house** button on the right always brings you back.
- On your first visit a short **Getting started** tip sits at the top of the Map's left panel. **Got it** hides it; **?** (Data & help) → **Getting started**, or Settings → Help, brings it back.

## The screen at a glance

**The top bar** (computer and tablet) runs across the whole window and is the same on every page:
- On the left: the panel button (shows or hides the side panel; it's lit while the panel shows), the logo, and six places to go, most used first: **Map**, **Projects** (your site plans), **Reports**, **Market**, **Filings** and **My Work**.
- On the right: **Ask AI**, **?** (Data & help: where every number comes from and how fresh it is; while you're on that page the button is lit, and pressing it again takes you back to the page you came from) and the **cog** (Settings: everything you set once, in one place; see **Settings** below).

**The browser's Back and Forward buttons work between pages.** Each page you open is a step in the browser's history, so after Reports then Market, Back returns to Reports and Back again to the Map, and the tab's title names the page (for example "Reports · Real Estate Intelligence Platform"). Changing a filter or opening a card doesn't add a step.

**A reload keeps your place.** Reloading the page, or opening the installed app again, comes back to where you were: the same map view (including tilt and turn), the property card you had open, the site plan you were working on, or the project you were looking at on Projects. Only a first visit flies in to the region. Links you copy and saved searches work as before.

Three of the six hold more than one page. A row of buttons at the top of the page switches between them, and the place remembers the page you used last:
- **Market:** Overview and Compare areas.
- **Filings:** Timeline, Companies (who's building the most) and Changes (what the nightly check found).
- **My Work:** Notes & watchlist, and Saved searches.

Old links and saved reports still open the same page wherever it now sits (a link to Projects opens Projects, a link to Companies opens Filings › Companies).

A small green number means something new needs a look: on **Notes & watchlist**, watched filings that changed (the number also shows on **My Work**); on the **Changes** page button under Filings, the filings that are new or changed since you last opened it (that one stays on the Changes button and isn't repeated on the Filings tab). Plain grey numbers are just counts (your projects, beside **Projects**; the areas in Compare).

**Left panel** (on phones, the **Explore** tab), top to bottom: the area first, then what you can learn and run on it, then the filings. Every block is the same card, so the edges line up:
- **The area bar** stays pinned at the top while the panel scrolls. It names what the panel is about: the area you've picked (with what kind it is: box, shape, radius, county or place) or, with nothing picked, the map view and the nearest town. Under the name: how many filings match and their value (always shown, even with the filing dots off; with nothing picked it reads "filings region-wide", because it counts the whole region and your filters, not just what's on screen), **+ Compare**, and a **⋯** menu with **Save search**, **Copy link**, **Filing alerts (RSS)** and, when two or more counties are picked, **+ Compare Each**. **Save search** and the alerts are dimmed ("Pick an area or set a filter first") until you've set a filter or picked an area. **Copy link** with no area picked also remembers where the map is looking and how far it's zoomed, so the link opens on the same view. The **×** clears the picked area and puts the tool away (so a radius search's box closes too); your filters stay. The bar stays solid while the panel scrolls beneath it. With nothing picked, **Box**, **Shape**, **Radius** and **County** buttons start the same tools as the map's toolbar (on a phone they switch to the Map first).
- **Area at a Glance:** who lives and works in the picked area, or in the area on screen when nothing is picked: population and growth, median income, home value, rent, households and jobs. The area bar above names the area it covers. With nothing picked it updates as you move the map.
- **Reports for this area:** one row per area report (FEMA, Environmental, Crime, Drive-Time, Traffic, Air Traffic) with a line on what it covers. With an area picked they run on it; without one, the rows say **Run on map view** (FEMA and Environmental need a picked area, so they show once you pick one). Crime covers the City of Houston only, so elsewhere its row is greyed out and says **Houston only**. **All reports →** opens the Reports page.
- **Saved searches:** a short list of the searches you saved (the heading counts them), each with a one-line summary of its filters and area and the day you saved it, an **RSS feed** link and a **×** (Delete saved search). Deleting says **Deleted · Undo** for a few seconds. **Manage in My Work →** opens the full list. It only shows once you've saved one.
- **Construction Filings** (last): the header shows the live count, value and area (for example "3,889 filings · $12.08B · Harris County") and the dates the data covers, even while the section is closed. The **On map** switch shows or hides the filing dots and always matches the map. Opening or closing the section never touches the map: only the **On map** switch shows or hides the dots. Inside: the headline metrics (**+ Metrics** picks them), the project list, and a short note on where the filings come from and how far to trust them. It's closed until you open it, and stays the way you left it.
- **The last line** links to **Data sources & freshness** and credits the base maps.

**Top of the map**, top to bottom:
- **Search** (first row).
- **The tool row:** Move, Box (drag a box), Shape (draw a shape), Radius (an address plus a distance), County (click counties), then **Layers**, **Filters** and **Export**. When the map is narrow (a tablet, or with Ask AI open) the buttons drop their names and keep their icons instead of wrapping: Export's name goes first, then the five tools', then Layers' and Filters'. If even the icons don't fit, the five tools fold into one **Select ▾** button that lists them by name. Hover over any icon for its name. While a tool is on, a **Drawing a box · Cancel** chip shows under the layer buttons (on a computer as well as a phone), and **Esc** puts the tool away; the hint under it says so. The search box and these buttons stay on screen while Ask AI is open.
- **Cursors** (mouse or trackpad) show what a click will do: the dark arrow for nothing in particular, a green arrow over anything you can open or select, a small building at street zoom when a click opens that building, four arrows while you drag the map, and a crosshair with a small badge for each drawing tool (a dashed box for Box, a shape for Shape, a circle for Radius, a county for County). Text boxes keep the normal text cursor.
- **Layers** opens the Map layers panel right under it. Its number says how many layers are drawing on the map (see "Map layers" below).
- **Filters** narrow what the map shows, layer by layer (see "Filters" below). Its number counts the filters that are on, and in **Match everything** mode it reads **Filters · Match**.
- **Export** makes a report.
- **Search** finds addresses, places, roads, businesses, projects, owners and developers. Press ⌘K (Mac) or Ctrl+K to jump to it (the shortcut does nothing while Settings is open). **Show All on Map** and picking a company or person narrow the filings and put them on the map. An address gets a pin and a card whose **Property Card** button opens the parcel's owner, value, site and area details. A town, county, neighborhood or ZIP gets an outline and a card with **Area Summary**, **Use as Area** (makes it the picked area, so Compare, Save search and the area bar all use it), **+ Compare** and **Highlight**; a place with no outline of its own shows no filing count. When Google is set up for the site, a **Google** section lists Google's matches too; picking one switches to the Google map and opens a Google place card (address, phone, website, hours, rating and a link to Google Maps). Results show up as they're found: addresses and places in a moment, and businesses as each source answers, so a slow source never holds up the list. Picking a result moves the map straight away; a town's or road's outline fills in when it arrives. Pressing Enter on a street address waits the moment it takes to find it, so you land on the address and not on a filing that happens to match.
- **Quick layer buttons** under the tool row, always on one line, with the layers you turn on most first. To start with (and until you've used them) the order is Parcels, Flood Zones, Demographics, Traffic Counts, Satellite and 3D, then Filings, Planes and Radar. The app counts, on this device, each time you turn a layer on with these buttons or in Map layers, and puts the buttons in that order the next time you open it (never while you're clicking). The row uses all the width the map has. When there are more buttons than fit, a small arrow appears at the end that has more: click it, scroll the row sideways or use the mouse wheel over it. On a phone the row sits just under the search bar.
- **The filters in effect** show under the layer buttons as chips, one per filter: for example **Harris ×**, **Medical ×**, **Violent crime ×**, **Under construction · Oct 2025 ×** or **Match everything ×**, plus the picked area and a searched place's outline. Click a chip's **×** to drop just that one; **Clear all** at the end clears them all. With nothing narrowing the map, the row isn't there.

**Right side of the map:** only buttons that move the map, in two groups, each with its name when you hover over it:
- Zoom in and out.
- **Compass:** back to a flat, north-up view of the same spot.
- **Near me:** the filings within 3 miles of your location (and puts them on the map). Outside the area we cover, it says so instead.
- **Reset view ▾:** **Whole region** (back to the full Houston region) or **Globe**.

Time-lapse is now on the Filings layer and the Filings legend (see "Construction filings"), and Map layers is the **Layers** button in the tool row.

**Legend:** one card in the bottom-left corner lists every layer that's colouring the map. One layer's key is open at a time (the newest one you turned on); click another layer's name to open it instead. The arrow at the top folds the whole card down to its title, and the app remembers that. On phones it starts folded.

**Pages use the whole width.** The left panel belongs to the Map: on every other page (Market, Reports, Notes & watchlist, Companies and the rest) it steps aside so the page has room, and it comes back on the Map just as you left it, at the same width, open or closed. To glance at it from a page, click the panel button at the far left of the top bar; that only affects the page you're on. On a tablet the panel starts closed so the map isn't cut in half; open it with the same button, and the app remembers your choice. When the assistant or a filing card is open beside a page on a computer, the page narrows to make room for it instead of hiding behind it, and the page's buttons move under its title if they no longer fit beside it.

**Every page is laid out the same way.** At the top: the page's name, its title and a short summary on the left, and its main buttons on the right (on phones they sit under the title). Downloads are under one **Export ▾** button that lists what you can save. Pages that follow the filters (Timeline, Companies, Changes, Compare areas and Reports) have a bar under the title with the number of filings and their value, each filter as a chip you can remove with its **×**, **Edit filters** and **Reset**. **Edit filters** opens the filters right there on the page, so you can change them and watch the page update without going to the Map. When a page has nothing to show yet, it says why and offers the next step.

**Back to where you were:** when something on a page takes you to the Map (a company, a change, a saved search, a past report), a **← Back to …** button appears at the top left of the map. It returns you to that page, scrolled to where you were. Its **×** dismisses it, and picking another page clears it.

**Buttons** follow one pattern everywhere: the main action is a solid green button and the other choices are outlined. On touch screens every button, chip and map control is at least a fingertip wide, and close (×) buttons are easy to hit. Buttons, toggles and rows react when you hover over or press them.

**The app is light only.** There is no dark mode.

**Browser Back and Forward** move between the app's pages (Map, Timeline, Market, a project's page…) instead of leaving the app. Your filters and the area you picked stay as they are; only the page changes.

**Keyboard:** every button, link and field shows a clear green outline when you reach it with Tab. Switching pages from the top bar with the keyboard puts you on the new page's title, and screen readers say which page opened. Dialogs such as Export keep Tab inside them until you close them (Esc), then return you to the button that opened them. If your device is set to reduce motion, the app's animations are switched off (busy spinners keep turning slowly).

**Phones:** the map fills the screen. The bottom bar has **Map**, **Explore** (the left panel), **Projects**, **Reports** and **More**. **More** is a short list in groups:
- **Market:** Overview, Compare areas.
- **Filings:** Timeline, Companies, Changes.
- **My Work:** Notes & watchlist, Saved searches.
- **Export:** **Quick export** for a report straight from the map.
- **Map tools:** Layers, Nearby (the area around you) and Pin a note (a site note at your location).
- **Settings:** opens the full Settings page (your name and team, the map, install and the rest). The gear at the top of **Explore** opens it too.
- **Data & help:** data sources and how fresh they are.

The **More** sheet opens above the bottom bar (the tabs stay in reach) and closes with its **×**, by tapping the handle at its top, by tapping the dimmed map, or with Esc.

The tab you're on is lit, and More is lit on its pages (for example Market or Notes & watchlist), with the same row of page buttons at the top as on a computer. A green number on More means a watched filing changed; new filings show on the Changes row inside it.

On the phone map, the five area tools are one **Select ▾** button at the top left. It opens a short list (Move, Box, Shape, Radius, County); pick one and a **Drawing a shape · Cancel** chip shows until you finish or tap Cancel. **Filters** is the funnel button in the search bar, and **Layers** is the stacked-squares button on the right. Both open as sheets from the bottom with a **×** at the top and a big button at the bottom (**Show results (N)** or **Done** for Filters, **Done** for Layers); the search bar and layer buttons stay in view above the Layers sheet.

Cards slide up from the bottom and open part-way, showing the title, the owner and value line and a row of buttons along the bottom (**Ask AI**, **Site plan**, **Watch** and **⋯**). Swipe up for half the screen, then the full screen. Swipe down to step back, down to just the title bar; swiping never closes a card, only the **×** does.

## Settings

Everything you set once lives in one place: the **cog** at the top right on a computer or tablet (or press **Ctrl+,**, **⌘,** on a Mac), and **More → Settings** or the gear at the top of **Explore** on a phone. It slides in from the right on a computer and fills the screen on a phone; **Esc**, the **×** or a click outside closes it. The row of buttons at the top jumps to each part.

- **You & your team:** **Your name** (shown on notes and site plans you add or edit), the **team passcode** if your team uses one (hidden once saved, with **Change**), whether notes and projects are shared with the team or kept on this device, when they last synced, how many changes are waiting, and **Sync now**. Notes & watchlist and Projects show a small line at the top (for example "Shared with the team · Matthew"); click it to come here.
- **Map:** the **Default base map** the map opens with (Default, Streets, Satellite, Topo, ESRI imagery or Free Map). **Map layers & display…** opens the Layers panel with the **Map display** group open and in view (dot grid, county lines, roads, road names), and **Headline metrics…** opens the picker for the numbers above the filing list. **Show sources on cards and panels** adds a source line under the numbers on property cards and panels (exported reports always list their sources). **Panel width: Reset** puts the Map's left panel back to its normal width after you've dragged it.
- **AI model:** which AI does each job. Every AI feature has its own row with a **provider** and a **model**, kept on this device:
  - **Site plans** (Build with AI, Change With AI, Ask about the plan, What Should Go Here?), **Ask AI** (the typed assistant), **Filing briefs** and **Reference files** (reading a project's surveys and PDFs): **OpenAI** or **Anthropic (Claude)**. The model list shows the models the site's key for that provider can use, after **Server default** (which names the model used when you don't pick one; for Claude, Claude Opus 5.5). OpenAI models that can't give the structured answers these need (the "pro" models, the ChatGPT "chat-latest" ones, voice models, GPT-4 and GPT-3.5) aren't listed.
  - **Place search**, **Voice** and **Renderings**: **OpenAI only**. Claude has no live voice and doesn't make images, and place search uses OpenAI's web search. Their model lists show only the models that can do that job: OpenAI's chat models for place search, its live voice models for Voice, its GPT Image models for Renderings.
  - If the key can't use a pick, or a model can't answer a particular request, that feature's default answers instead, so a pick never breaks anything. With Claude picked and no Claude key on the site, OpenAI answers. The line under the rows says which model gave the last answer. If you picked one OpenAI model for everything before, the text features start from it.
  - On Claude, **Ask AI** keeps the conversation on your device and sends it with each question (Claude doesn't keep conversations). A very long conversation has to be started again with **New**. The jurisdiction and best-use web lookups in site plans stay on OpenAI.
  - **Plan sets:** **Claude model** (the models the site's Claude key can use, after **Server default (claude-opus-5-5)**) and **Effort** (Low, Medium, High or Extra high; **Server default** is Medium). Claude writes each set's notes and designs its building types; higher effort thinks harder, costs more and takes longer. Both are kept on this device. The note under them shows this month's plan set spend against its $100 monthly budget, or says Claude isn't set up on the site.
  - **Usage:** under the model, a table of the AI's use for **This month**, **Last 30 days**, **Last 7 days**, **Today** or **This year**: for each model, the requests, the tokens sent (**In**) and the tokens back (**Out**, which includes the model's reasoning; hover to see how much), and the **Cost**, then a total. A line under it splits the cost by feature: site plans, Ask AI, filing briefs, place search and the nightly data. The app counts every AI answer's tokens from October 16, 2026 (voice talks to OpenAI directly, so it isn't counted here).
  - **The cost is an estimate** at OpenAI's and Anthropic's list prices, taken from public price listings on October 15, 2026. Where those listings disagreed the model has a * and its price may be off; a model with no known price shows a dash and a ?. **Edit prices** lets you set any model's price per million tokens (input, cached input, output); it's kept on this device, and **Use list prices** puts them back.
  - **Billed:** if the server has a (read-only) OpenAI admin key, a **Billed** column shows what OpenAI actually charged **for this app's own API key** for each model over the same days, including its voice use, with the total. The OpenAI account's other keys and projects are never included: the app finds its own key among the account's projects, and the note under the table names the key and project. If OpenAI only bills that key's project as a whole and the project has other keys too, the note says so and gives this key's own cost separately (from OpenAI's token counts for the key, at list prices); giving the app's key a project of its own makes Billed exact. If the key can't be found, no billed amounts show and the note says why. Without an admin key, a note says how to add one.
- **Saved on this device:** how many saved searches, Compare areas, site notes, projects and saved report files this browser holds, each with **Open**.
- **App:** **Install app** when your browser offers it (or how to install on an iPhone, or "Installed ✓"), the keyboard shortcuts, and **Reset layout**. Reset layout asks first, then puts the panel (open or closed, and its width), its sections, the assistant's width, headline metrics, timeline options, export choices and the Projects view back to how they started, and reloads. Your notes, projects, saved searches, team sharing and other settings are kept.
- **Help:** **Getting started** (the first-visit tip again), **Data sources & freshness**, and **Save voice log** (what the voice assistant heard and did this visit, for troubleshooting when it doesn't respond).
- **Testing · danger zone** (last, in red): **Delete all projects…**, for clearing out test data. It first says how many projects, options and renderings will go and whether they go **for the whole team** (team sharing on) or **only from this device** (sharing off); type **DELETE** to turn on the final button. Every project, option and rendering picture on this device goes, and the Projects page opens empty. With team sharing on, the projects are removed from the team too, so teammates' devices drop them as well; **Only this device's copies** instead clears this device and leaves the team's (they download again on the next sync, and changes not yet synced are lost). It can't be undone.

When something isn't set up on your site (team sharing, live traffic), the app says so in plain words, such as "Not available on this site", instead of a technical message.

## Looking up a property

Click a building, or at street zoom click open ground, to open its **property card**. On a computer it fills the right side of the map from top to bottom, and the map buttons move to its left, so nothing on the map hides behind it. The owner and value show first, usually within a couple of seconds; the businesses, height and photo fill in after.

**The top of the card** says what it is and where ("Parcel · Harris County"), the address (or "Unaddressed parcel"), and the owner and value under it. Under that is a row of buttons that stays in reach while you scroll:
- **Ask AI** about this property (it's ready as soon as the parcel record arrives).
- **Site plan** (see "Projects and site plans" below).
- **Watch** adds it to your watchlist; it reads **Watching** while it's on the list.
- **⋯** holds the rest: **Street View ↗**, **Add site note**, **Orbit view**, **Select more** (to pick more buildings or parcels), **Copy link** (a link that opens this property) and **Export details (CSV)**.

Scroll down and the top folds to just the title and the buttons. The small arrow beside the **×** minimizes the card to its title bar; the map below and beside it takes clicks again, and the arrow (or a click on the bar) brings the card back.

**With a card open, one click on the next lot opens it.** At street zoom a click on open ground or another building opens that property straight away (no need to close the card first). A click inside the property already showing, or when zoomed far out, just closes the card.

- **Ownership & value:** owner, market, land and improvement value, year built, when it was acquired, acres (when the record has none, the card measures the parcel outline and shows **≈ N acres**), and building size. This comes from the county appraisal district through the state's parcel service. Where the state's data has no parcel (all of Harris and Waller counties), it comes straight from that county's appraisal district: Harris records have the owner, address and values but no year built or acquisition date, and where several records share one outline (condominium units) the card says how many. **More record details** opens the mailing address, the legal description and every field in the appraisal record.
  - **Zoning & last sale: Get from Regrid** looks those up (paid, capped each month; hover it to see that it uses one of your monthly records, and the button says how many are left). The Regrid record shows right under Ownership, and the row then reads **Regrid record below** (click it to jump there) with the remaining count updated.
  - Where neither the state data nor the appraisal district has a record at the spot (usually a street, a lake or a lot line), the section starts with a big **Get owner, value & zoning from Regrid** button instead. Once Regrid answers, the card's title, the line under it, the Overview and Ownership all switch to the Regrid owner, address and value, and say so ("Owner and value from Regrid, below").
- **Company behind the owner:** when the owner is a company (an LLC, partnership, corporation or trust), the card looks it up in the Texas Comptroller's free public records and shows two lines: the name, then whether it's active, when it was formed and its registered agent (nothing is cut off). **Details** opens the rest: where it was formed, its mailing address and the officers, managers or members from its latest public report. If the name doesn't match exactly, similar names are listed to pick from. People's names are never looked up.
- **The building:** footprint, height, floors and estimated floor area. Height comes from USGS lidar where it's believable; a reading that doesn't fit the building (say, hundreds of feet on a one-storey store) is set aside with a note, and the map or appraisal record is used instead.
- **Site:** flood zone, traffic counts on nearby roads, water and utility districts (MUDs), **utilities** (see below), tax-increment zones, Opportunity Zone, school district, bus stops, nearby environmental sites, crime nearby (Houston), and area rents and jobs.
- **Utilities** (in the Site section, filled in a moment after the rest; distances from the parcel's edge):
  - **Power:** how far it is to the nearest transmission line (with its voltage and owner, e.g. "480 ft to a transmission line, 138 kV, CenterPoint Energy"), the nearest distribution line, and the nearest substation. Power lines come from OpenStreetMap: high-voltage lines are mapped well, but street (distribution) lines only in part, so "none mapped" doesn't mean there are none.
  - **Water and sewer:** who holds the state service area (CCN) for water and for sewer here, with the CCN number. "No CCN here" usually means a city or a MUD that doesn't hold one; the Districts row shows the MUD.
  - **Mains:** in Houston, the distance to the nearest public sewer main (with its size and material) and to a public water main where the city publishes one. Elsewhere, the distance to the nearest water line a utility has mapped for its service area, and a reminder that the city or MUD engineer has the mains.
- **Crime:** inside Houston, the incidents reported within a mile. Anywhere in the US, **Crime by city / county**:
  - the local police department: the city's own police inside city limits, otherwise the county police or sheriff;
  - violent and property crimes per 100,000 residents for the newest full year, compared with the state and the US, and the change from the year before;
  - a 10-year trend line against the state and the US, and the share of crimes solved;
  - how it ranks among the same kind of departments in its state, e.g. "violent crime lower than 71% of Texas city police departments";
  - **Other departments** in the county, if you'd rather see a neighboring city or the sheriff (the section stays where it is on screen while the new department loads);
  - **Full report** for the year-by-year table and the mix of offenses (murder, robbery, burglary, theft and others), with PDF and CSV export.
  - These are whole-year, department-wide figures from the FBI: every spot a department covers shows the same numbers. The card says which year they're for, and warns you when a department reported only part of a year or has stopped reporting.
- **Area snapshot:** census numbers for that neighborhood.
- **Businesses:** what's inside or next door, from the Overture Maps and Foursquare open business lists plus OpenStreetMap, and businesses registered for sales tax at the address. Click a business to open its details in front of the property card, with **←** and the property's name at the top to come back.
- **Development here & nearby:** building permits on the parcel and new building or demolition permits close by (Harris County only), plats and new subdivisions within about a quarter mile, the water district (MUD) the property is in with its creation date and bonds outstanding, the nearest elementary, middle and high school with enrollment, and TxDOT work within a mile. Click any line for its card.
- **Construction filings** on the parcel.
- **From here:** distance and drive time (**Drive time from my location**), a drive-time map, weather, news, and site imagery (recent satellite and dated high-resolution photos).
- **Regrid details** (paid, capped each month, from the Ownership section): zoning, zoning limits such as height and density, the last sale price and date where recorded, and land use.

**Section buttons** under the action row (Owner & value, Building, Site, Crime, Area…) jump to that section, with its heading just below the card's header. As you scroll, the button for the section you're reading lights up, and the row fades at an edge when there are more buttons past it.

**Reports and filings opened from the card come back to it:** a Crime, FEMA, Environmental or other area report from the Site section, a business, a filing under Construction filings, a drive-time map or a new site note opens in the card with **← 1200 Main St** (the property's name) at the top. Click it, or press Esc, to return to the property just as you left it: same tabs, same spot in the card, still highlighted on the map. The filing card works the same way for its drive-time map and notes.

**Esc** closes the card (or goes back, as above), but never a site plan (there Esc steps back instead; see Projects and site plans), not while you're typing in a box, and not when it's closing something else first: Esc in the search box closes its results, and Esc with Map layers or a menu open closes just that.

**Big buildings:** clicking any part of a building selects the whole building, even a warehouse or shopping center that the map draws in pieces. If the building sits on more than one parcel, the card lists every parcel with its owner and value, and a total. The Ownership grid is for the first parcel (it says "Parcel 1 of 3 · owner" and the Regrid button and company lookup say "for parcel R1"), while the Overview adds them all up. Click a parcel in the list to open it in its own tab.

**Several things at once (tabs):** Shift-click to add more, up to 20. On a touch screen, choose **⋯ → Select more** on the card, tap the other things, then **Done**. This works for buildings, parcels, construction filings, planes and airports, mixed together. With just one thing picked there are no tabs; they appear as soon as there are two.
- Each one gets a numbered **tab** at the top of the card (the row scrolls sideways and keeps the newest tab in view; **Add** / **Done** sits beside it, never over a tab); the same number marks it on the map, in the middle of the building or parcel. Parcels picked as open ground keep a faint outline while you look at another tab. Tap a tab to see that card, or its **×** to drop it.
- Shift-click something already picked to take it out (for a parcel, anywhere inside it). A parcel is never added twice. A plain click starts over with just that one; closing the card clears them all.
- With two or more buildings or parcels, an **All** tab adds them up: footprint, floor area, market value and acres (each parcel counted once), the construction filings on them, a CSV export and **Zoom to All**.

## Picking an area and getting reports

Choose **Box**, **Shape**, **Radius** or **County** at the top of the map and mark the area (press **Esc** to stop). It becomes the **selected area**, named in the area bar at the top of the Map's left panel. From **Reports for this area** just below it you can run:

- **FEMA report** (also on the **Reports** page, for the selected area or one you draw): flood zones, past flood insurance claims, disaster declarations and FEMA's risk index. Any size of area works, up to a whole county: flood-zone shares are measured at about 900 evenly spaced points for larger areas, and claims are read for every census tract touching the area. If some tracts can't be read in time, the report says how many so you can run it again.
- **Environmental report** (also on the **Reports** page, for the selected area or one you draw): a screen of state and federal environmental records around the area or a parcel (from a property card's **Area reports**, it uses the parcel itself): cleanup and contamination sites, leaking and registered petroleum tanks, landfills, EPA-permitted facilities, oil and gas wells (including orphan wells) and pipelines, wetlands and soils. Each kind is searched out to the distance a Phase I environmental assessment would use (¼ to 1 mile from the edge of the site), nearest first, with distance and direction, and the table shows how many are **on the site** itself. Any size of area works, up to a whole county: every record is counted, and when a kind has more than 1,000 the report lists the on-site and nearest ones while **Export CSV** gathers all of them (it can take a minute for a county). Larger areas show each soil's **share** of the site. If a source doesn't answer in time, its row says so with a **Retry** button that asks that source again. Exports as PDF or CSV. It is a screen of public databases, not a Phase I.
- **Traffic report** (Texas only; elsewhere its row is greyed out): the busiest roads and daily traffic counts, plus live speeds and incidents where available. Works for any area up to several counties. For big areas it lists the busiest roads and maps the 1,000 busiest counted segments; **Export CSV** lists every counted segment. Incidents are only the ones inside the area.
- **Drive-time map:** 10, 20 and 30-minute drive areas, with the people, households and jobs inside each. The bands stay on the map while you go back to the property card or run a report on one of them, and go away when you close the card (× or Esc). **Clear from map** in the card or in the map legend removes them any time.
- **Air traffic report:** how often planes pass over, how low, and which kinds. Any size of area works within the Houston area (about 100 nautical miles around it); elsewhere its row says **Houston area only** and is greyed out.
- **Crime report** (City of Houston): incidents by type, trend, time of day, day of week, and per resident. Any size of area works, from a block to the whole city; only incidents inside Houston are counted. A neighbourhood takes a second or two; an area covering most of the city can take up to about 20 seconds. Outside Houston its row is greyed out.
- **City / County Crime Report** (anywhere in the US, from the **Reports** page): the police department at the middle of the map or of the selected area, with the same figures as the property card's Crime by city / county.
- **Compare:** **+ Compare** in the area bar adds up to 4 areas to see side by side.

Every report exports as a PDF in the Finishes Solutions style, and its data as a spreadsheet. Reports are kept on the **Reports** page.

If a report can't finish, it says why in plain words (for example "The server took too long on this area. Try again…") with a **Retry** button, instead of a technical error.

### Clearing the view

Everything that narrows what you see shows as a chip under the map's layer buttons: each filter, a time period other than the last 12 months, a time-lapse month, the selected area and a searched place's outline. Click a chip's **×** to drop just that one, or **Clear all** at the end of the row to clear everything (and switch Filters back to **Filter each layer**). On phones, the **✕** next to the filter button clears everything. Inside the Filters menu, **Clear All** is at the top whenever there's something to clear.

## Filters

**Filters** works on the map's layers, not just construction filings. It has a section for each kind of data:
- **Demographics:** median household income, population growth, home value, rent, housing vacancy, and jobs located here.
- **Traffic:** the busiest road, in vehicles a day.
- **Risk:** which flood zones show (all of them, or only the high-risk 100-year floodplain), the crime type (Houston), and, when matching, FEMA's flood risk rating. The crime type is the same setting as the one under Crime in Map layers: change it in either place and both follow, the Filters number counts it, and it's kept for your next visit.
- **Construction filings** (last, closed until you open it or set a filing filter): the filing filters (period, county, type, value, use and so on). When matching, this section also offers the number of construction projects in an area.

The switch at the top picks how the filters work (the line under it, which scrolls with the sections, says what the chosen mode does). A minimum above the maximum (value, square feet or dates) is swapped for you, with a note, so the result is never silently empty. The **Dates** row only shows a range you set yourself; the **Filed** chips already show the usual last-12-months period.
- **Filter each layer:** each filter acts on its own layer.
  - Demographic thresholds fade the neighborhoods that don't qualify on the Demographics layer.
  - The traffic threshold hides quieter roads on Traffic Counts.
  - The risk choices change the flood and crime layers.
  - Setting a filter turns its layer on.
- **Match everything:** the app looks for neighborhoods (census tracts) that meet **every** threshold you set. For example: income at least $100K, growth at least 5%, at least 5 construction projects, and low flood risk.
  - Matching areas are outlined in green and the rest of the map fades.
  - The panel says how many areas match, and **Zoom to matches** frames them.

On the Filings pages, Compare areas and Reports, **Edit filters** opens the same panel over the page with just the construction filters, so you can change them without leaving the page. Close it with the **×**, Esc or a click elsewhere.

The summary at the top of the panel lists everything that's on, and **Clear All** resets it (and goes back to **Filter each layer**). With only the usual last-12-months period set, it just says **No filters on**. The chips under the map's layer buttons show the same filters, each removable on its own. On phones the big button at the bottom reads **Show results (N)** while something narrows the filings, and **Done** when nothing does. The busiest-road and flood-rating data for matching is added by the nightly data refresh; until it arrives, those two thresholds are left out of the match, and the panel says so.

Property filters (use, acres, value, year built, owner, zoning) are planned. They need the county appraisal data to be loaded first.

## Market and demographics

- **Area at a Glance** in the left panel shows the census numbers for the selected area, or for what's on screen when nothing is selected. The area bar above it names the area.
- The **Market** page (Market → Overview) shows one county, several counties together, or the whole region. It opens on the area selected on the map (the selected counties, or the county a drawn area sits in); with nothing selected it shows the whole region. The bar under the title names the area; click **Change** to pick a county's button or click the small map, and click it again (or **Whole region**) to go back to all counties. A bar of buttons stays pinned as you scroll: once the area line above has scrolled away it names the area (click **Change** there to pick another), jumps to each section, and holds the **Monthly charts: 12 mo / 24 mo / All** switch. The sections run in this order:
  - **Summary:** headline tiles, people and money first: population, jobs, median household income, typical rent, homes permitted, new businesses, unemployment, then construction under way, starting in the next 90 days and building material prices. Most have a small trend line (it shows finished months only, and the under-construction line ends on today's number). New businesses carries no year-over-year change, because the permit list only keeps locations still open and older months read low. Unemployment uses the same Houston figure as the Markets table (one decimal), or the county's own rate when one county is shown. **What's moving** words the mortgage rate as a monthly average ("averaged 7.34% in October so far") and gives the latest weekly rate beside it. **What's moving** lists the biggest changes in plain words, and **Ask AI for a market brief** has the assistant sum them up for a construction and finishes business.
  - **Growth & demand:** homes permitted per year (single-family, 2–4 units, apartments), new business locations per month, jobs by industry, city sales tax per month and by city, and estimated consumer spending.
  - **Housing & rents:** home values, incomes, rents, vacancy and age from the census, household income bands, Zillow rents by ZIP (ZIPs are placed in a county by their town, so border ZIPs are approximate), and the Houston for-sale market (listing price, homes for sale, days on market, permits, seasonally adjusted). Cards that don't change with the area carry a small tag: **Houston metro** (the for-sale market) or **National** (material prices and interest rates). Small charts are drawn at their real size, so their numbers are easy to read; rents by ZIP say "n/a" where a ZIP has no year-ago figure and "Based on 1 ZIP" when there is only one.
  - **Costs & rates:** 5 years of building material prices (building materials, lumber, steel, ready-mix concrete, copper), interest rates (30-year mortgage, 10-year Treasury, fed funds), today's rates and home lending.
  - **Markets** and the **Correlation explorer** (below).
  - **Construction pipeline:** from the state's construction filings. Value under construction each month (with what's already scheduled for the next 12 months), by use (click a use for its largest projects) and by county or type, the largest projects under construction and those starting in the next 90 days. Click a project to see it on the map. A filing whose value is clearly mis-entered (over $2 billion) is left out of the totals and named. The filing list starts in November 2024, so earlier months undercount. The chart of value by month always shows the full span, whatever the 12 mo / 24 mo / All switch says.
  - **Area & news:** the Houston crime trend, the busiest roads, local development news (only Houston-area stories about building, development and real estate, dated like "Oct 13"), and the newest business locations, 10 at a time (**Show 10 more**). If crime or traffic can't be loaded, the card says so with a **Retry** link, and it is left out of the exported report.
  - Hover over any chart (bars, lines, the small trend lines) to read its numbers; click a bar for its breakdown (it opens under the chart and scrolls into view). The 12 mo / 24 mo / All buttons set how many months the monthly charts show.
  - **Charts fill their boxes:** every chart is drawn at the width of its box, so text reads at the same size everywhere. The headline tiles fill whole rows, the price charts sit three to a row (two on a tablet, one on a phone), and boxes side by side end level. The page opens faster, and new data (crime, traffic, live prices) only redraws its own section, so open lists and your scroll position stay put.
  - **Charts move with the data:** the first time a chart comes into view its bars grow up and its lines draw across (once; coming back to the page doesn't replay it). Switching 12 mo / 24 mo / All, the county, a chart's series or a pinned bar glides the bars and lines to their new values, so you can see what changed. Live market prices refresh every minute without the charts starting over. While a section's data loads you see a plain grey placeholder, never made-up numbers. If your device is set to reduce motion, charts simply appear; printed pages and PDF exports always show the finished charts.
  - **Export ▾ → Market report · PDF** makes a Market report with a street map of the area and every section; **Market data · CSV** saves every number as a spreadsheet.
- **Markets**, on the Market page (Market → Overview), lists prices for the things that move Houston real estate, in groups you pick from the tabs:
  - oil and natural gas, and Houston's big energy and other public companies;
  - homebuilders and real estate funds;
  - building material prices (lumber, steel, concrete, copper);
  - mortgage and Treasury rates, the major stock indexes, and crypto (Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana, XRP);
  - Houston's own monthly numbers: jobs, unemployment, housing permits, listing prices, homes for sale and days on market.
  - Each row has the latest price, today's and the past year's change, and a small chart. Crypto prices are live. Stock prices are delayed and come from an unofficial source, so treat them as a guide, not for trading. This isn't investment advice.
- The **Correlation explorer** below it answers "does this market move with Houston, and which goes first?":
  - Pick a Houston measure and a market series (or click **Correlate** on any row).
  - It compares year-over-year changes (or month-over-month) and tries every lead and lag up to 12 months.
  - It gives a verdict (strong, moderate, weak or not reliable), the lead time, and a chart of both lines. The choices and the verdict sit on the left, the chart on the right; hover over the chart for each month's actual changes, and over the lead/lag bars for each lag's correlation. **How this is measured** explains the method.
  - It is deliberately strict. It discounts for every lag it tries and for months that overlap. A link has to hold in both halves of the period to be called strong. Two unrelated prices that both rose over the years won't look related, and neither will two series that only share one extreme month, such as the spring 2020 crash.
  - Year over year is the safer choice. Most Houston numbers aren't seasonally adjusted, so month-over-month changes mostly show the seasons.
  - **What moves …?** tests your measure against every market series at once and lists the closest matches. With so many comparisons the bar is high, and often nothing passes. That is a real answer: it means no market series reliably tracks that measure.
  - **Explain with AI** asks the assistant to explain the result in plain language: what the numbers show, possible economic reasons, and what they don't prove. Correlation never proves that one thing causes another.
  - Construction filings and new businesses only go back about two years, so try month-over-month changes for them.
- **Demographics** on the map colors each census tract. Hover over a tract for its numbers.
- **Compare** puts up to 4 areas side by side. The table opens with census numbers for each area (size, population and growth, median income, home value and rent, households and jobs, the same figures as Area at a Glance), then the construction filings, which follow your filters (so they can differ from the Market page). A county's filing count matches the number on the area bar. Every area gets the same filters, shown as chips at the top. Two drawn boxes or shapes can both be added: each is named (Box 1, Shape 2, with the nearest town), and the pencil on a chip renames it. The **+ Compare** button reads **In Compare ✓** (click to open Compare) when the area is already there, and **Compare is full (4)** when there's no room. Adding an area shows an **Open Compare** link. **Export ▾** saves the comparison as PDF, Excel or CSV (Excel and CSV hold plain numbers, with a line saying which filters applied). In the quarterly chart a part-quarter at either end is drawn hollow with a dashed line and labelled, and the value-by-use bars have their own colours (not the area colours). With fewer than two areas, **Pick on the Map** takes you to the Map to choose them. With two or more counties selected on the map, **+ Compare Together** adds them as one area and **+ Compare Each** adds every county as its own area.

## Construction filings

Texas requires most commercial and public construction projects to register with the state (TDLR, through its TABS system). The app loads two years of these filings for the 7 counties every night. AI reads each filing for its use, tenant, developer, architect, contractor and housing units.

- **Filing dots** are off by default. Turn them on with **Filings** (above the map) or the **On map** switch on the panel's Construction Filings header. Green is new construction, grey is renovation, and a ring is an addition.
- **Filters → Construction filings:** time period, county, type, value, use, status, size, housing units, company name, exact addresses only, start or registration dates, and recent changes. On phones, Filters opens as a full sheet with **Clear All** at the top and a button at the bottom that closes it.
- **Filing card:** project details, dates, people involved, an **AI project brief** (what it is, timing, who's involved, area context), and nearby filings. Its top row has **Ask AI**, **Property card** (opens the property at that spot, with a way back to the filing), **Watch** and **⋯** (Open property card, TABS record ↗, Street View, Add site note, Select more and Copy link). Under People, the owner is a link just like the developer, architect and contractor: it shows that owner's filings.
- **Timeline** (Filings → Timeline): the monthly pipeline of what's under construction, plus a Gantt chart of every project. Hatched bars are dates estimated because the filer left them blank. Clicking a project opens its card over the page, and the chart and the Gantt redraw to fit the room beside the card (and again when the card closes or folds away). The legend lists the groups biggest first, and clicking one scrolls the Gantt to that group. Dragging across the chart (or tapping a month) turns the filings on on the map and shows what was under construction then; because that replaces the "Filed in the last 12 months" date filter, a note says so with an **Undo**. Tapping the month you already picked clears it. **Export ▾** saves the filing list as Excel, CSV or PDF.
- **Companies** (Filings → Companies): developers, architects and contractors ranked by activity; switch between Developers, Architects and Contractors at the top. Click one to see their projects on the map (the filings turn on), and use **← Back to Companies** to return to the same spot in the list. The list shows the top 150 by value; search to find the others. **Export ▾** saves the Activity report as PDF, Excel or CSV. On phones each company is a two-line row.
- **Changes** (Filings → Changes): new filings and changes found by the nightly refresh. It opens on **Last 7 days**, which counts the same filings as the green number and the "New this week / Any change" filters; each single nightly check is still in the list. A line under the title says how many changed filings match your filters out of the total, with **Show all** to see every one (it is left out when a check found no changes at all). Chips under the title (**New 119 · Status 138 · Value 2 · End date 9**) jump to a section, and each section's heading stays at the top while you scroll its list. Clicking a change opens the filing's card over the page; **Show on map** on the card goes to the map. The green number on Changes counts what's new since you last looked and clears when you open it.
- **Time-lapse:** **▶ Play by month** on the Filings row in Map layers, or on the Filings legend, plays construction month by month. It turns the filings on first. ## Projects and site plans

### The Feasibility workspace (experimental)

Opening a project now starts on **Feasibility**: the whole study of a site in six steps, with the project's numbers always in a summary beside it.

1. **Intake**: who it's for, the target use, the address and ZIP (filled in from the parcels), and the land. The land starts at the appraisal district's *land* value; enter the asking or contract price to use that instead. You can also type in a survey's metes and bounds to add the exact tract.
2. **Site**: pick the site option the study uses, or let the layout engine lay the parcels out as a new option. The site's buildable area, parking, coverage and detention are shown with the plan. **Open in site plan** takes you to the editor to refine it.
3. **Plans**: the schematic plan set's sheet list for that option, and a button to make the set.
4. **Budget**: a construction budget by CSI division, priced from the layout's quantities. Apartments and townhomes use the Finishes Solutions budget workbook's line items. Single-family homes, shops and restaurants, small industrial and flex buildings (up to 40,000 sf) and convenience stores use the team's own 2024–25 budgets: the middle value of the team's past budgets for that kind of building, line by line (foundations, roof, walls, windows, doors, mechanical, plumbing, electrical, sprinklers and, where the owner finishes the inside, the interiors), with the budgets' 10% contractor fee as its own line. A leased shop, restaurant or flex building leaves the interiors to its tenant improvement allowance. Offices, medical offices, hotels, churches, storage, big warehouses and site work use the platform's rates. Every line shows its quantity, rate and where the rate came from (and how many past budgets it rests on). You can change a rate or leave a line out. For a plan of one kind of building the team has budgeted before, a line under the summary compares the budget's building and site work per square foot with the middle of the team's 2024–25 budgets for that kind of building (with their contractor fee); a gap of more than 20% is flagged.
5. **Pro Forma**: every assumption with its source and usual range:
   - **Data**: interest rates from the platform's nightly market data.
   - **Library**: the budget workbook.
   - **House**: a starting point, not verified.
   - **Yours**: a number you changed.
   
   Change one and every number updates. Results open with the three returns the team decides on: **Levered IRR**, **Equity multiple** and **Cash-on-cash** (the average over the 10-year hold), then a row of cash-on-cash for each year of the hold, then cost, NOI, yield on cost, year-one cash-on-cash, unlevered IRR, NPV and debt coverage. Below are the rent needed to break even, what moves the return most, and the hold analysis. **New scenario** saves a set of changes (financing, costs, unit mix) to compare side by side.
6. **Reports**: download the Feasibility report (PDF), the workbook, the assumption register and the budget (spreadsheets), or save the whole set for the team. **Open the report here** shows it in a new tab to print, even without a connection to the report service.

Everything here is feasibility level: the plans are schematic (not for permit or construction), the costs are estimates rather than bids, and rents and cap rates are assumptions, not appraisals. Every screen and report says so.

Test-fit a building, or a whole mixed-use development, on one parcel or up to 20 together, by hand or with AI, and print it as a site plan sheet.

**Starting one**
- Open a property card (click a building, or open ground at street zoom) and click **Site plan** at the top of the card.
- **Several parcels as one site:** pick up to 20 (Shift-click, or **⋯ → Select more** on the card on touch screens), open the **All** tab and choose **Site Plan for All**. Their outlines are merged into one site, and every parcel you picked is in it. Overlaps are taken out, and the gaps the county's outlines leave between parcels that border each other are filled in as part of the site (no slivers or slits between lots). Where the map shows a road in the gap, the road stays in the site as a no-build area called **Road between parcels** (nothing is built or paved on it; delete it if the road is being closed or the land is yours). Build then lays out each side of the road as a site of its own, with the road as its street and its share of the program by area, so both sides are used (drives don't cross the road). Gaps up to 200 ft are joined this way, and the Start card says what was filled in. Only parcels more than 200 ft from the rest are left out, with a note, and a pick with no parcel record is named in a message. With **Parcels** (the parcel lines) on, each pick is the parcel drawn under your click, with its own outline and account, even where the county's statewide record lumps several of them into one big outline. A property whose account is in separate pieces (one owner's lot on both sides of a county line, say) can have each piece picked; they stay separate picks and both go into the site.
- Start a **new project** (named after the address) or **add the parcel to a project** you already have. Your very first plan skips this choice: the project is made for you, named after the address. While you choose, the site's outline is drawn dashed on the map.
- **Coming back to a parcel:** if the parcel is already in a project, the card says so first (**Already in (the project) · Option A · 25,000 sq ft**) with **Open Plan** to carry on where you were, or **New Option** to start another option in that project, and the choice below is set to that project, so the same land doesn't end up in a second project. The property card's button then reads **Open site plan**.
- **Find a Parcel** on a project with no parcels yet: the next parcel you open and click **Site plan** on goes into that project (the card reads **Add a parcel to (the project)**, with it already chosen).
- The plan starts from the parcel's outline from the county appraisal district (in Harris and Waller, from the district's own parcel map). Where there's no outline, use **Get owner, value & zoning from Regrid** in the card's Ownership section first and the plan uses Regrid's outline, or draw the site yourself (click each corner, then the first corner again).
- Planning takes over the map: the left list folds away, the area tools, Filters, Export and the layer buttons step aside (**Layers** stays), and the map zooms so the whole site sits in the open part of the map, clear of the plan panel and the search. The existing buildings are hidden while you plan. Everything comes back as it was when you close the plan, including the left list open or folded as you had it.
- The map refits to the site when the window or the left list changes size, never while you're moving the map yourself. The **Fit plan** button (top of the map buttons, while a plan is open) brings the whole site back into view.

**Build with AI**
- Say what should go there, e.g. "25,000 sf retail strip", "6,000 sf restaurant with drive-thru", "200 apartments, 3 stories", "warehouse with truck court, as big as fits". Press **Enter** (or **Build Layouts**) to build; Shift+Enter starts a new line.
- **Suggestions fit the site:** the three suggestions under the box are sized to the land: a drive-thru pad, a small shop strip or a medical office under 2 acres; shops with pads, apartments or a small warehouse up to 10 acres; apartments, homes or a warehouse with a truck court up to 25; a mixed-use neighbourhood, homes or a big warehouse beyond that. Tapping one **puts it in the box** (and lights up Build Layouts) so you can change it first; nothing is built until you press Enter or Build Layouts.
- **AI effort: Low, Medium, High or Ultra.** Four buttons above Build Layouts set how hard the AI works, each with about how long it takes, and a line under them says what the one you picked does:
  - **Low** (seconds): the AI reads your request quickly and the layouts come straight back, with no planning first and no review.
  - **Medium** (one to two minutes): the AI plans the site before placing anything (below), reads the request with that plan, then looks over the layouts once, scores them and picks the best, without changing the program.
  - **High** (two to three minutes): the AI thinks the site through carefully before placing anything, follows that plan, reviews the layouts against it as a planner would and, when they miss, changes the program once and lays it out again (below).
  - **Ultra** (three to six minutes): the most thorough. Before placing anything the AI works out three different plans for the site at its deepest thinking, checks each against the site and the numbers, and picks one. Then a longer search gives five layouts to start instead of three, the AI reviews up to six of them with their drawings, and it can change the program and lay it out again twice before it picks. Ultra has a warm gold tint and a small spark, and a soft shine sweeps across it when you pick it (not when your device is set to reduce motion). If the AI's thinking runs out of room before it answers, it's asked once more with more room and a little less thinking (as long as there's time), instead of the build falling back to the engine's own layouts.
  - Your choice is remembered on this device (if you used the old Quick switch it starts on Low, otherwise on High). The arrow keys move between the levels. **Leftover land** sits beside the label (**Filled**, **Phase 2** or **Held**): the same setting as **Leftover land** under Site parameters, so changing one changes the other.
- **One build at a time, and you can see it run:** while it works, a strip at the top of the plan panel shows the step ("Step 2 of 4 · Laying out the layouts"), how long it has taken and **Cancel**; on phones it's a small pill just above the sheet. The button you pressed spins, and Build, What Should Go Here?, the suggestions, the ideas and More layouts wait until it's done. Other changes (Trees, settings) don't stop it, but drawing or deleting a piece while it's still laying out cancels the build, so your change isn't replaced.
- **You only see layouts the AI has checked (Medium, High and Ultra):** while the AI is still reviewing, no layout is listed or put on the map. The **Layouts** section shows only the run: each step (done, under way, still to come), the time so far and **Cancel**, with the line **The new layouts show here once the AI has checked, confirmed and rated them**. Then they all come in at once, ranked by the AI, each with its score, verdict, strengths and problems, and the AI's choice goes on the map, marked **Recommended**. Layout numbers don't change when the AI reorders them. A layout that fails a check is never put on the map by itself while another one passes: the best one that passes goes on, and a message offers the AI's choice (**The AI recommends Layout 2, though it doesn't pass every check · Use it**). If you change the plan by hand while the AI is looking, your plan stays on the map and the message offers the AI's choice instead.
- **If the AI's review doesn't come back** (it fails, takes too long, or you cancel it), you're never left with nothing: the engine's layouts come in as the engine ranked them, with a banner **Not reviewed by the AI** saying why and **Review Now** to have the AI look at them. On **Low**, or with the AI off, the layouts appear as soon as they're drawn, as before.
- **When a build finishes, the panel scrolls to the Layouts** so the results sit right under the overview (on phones the sheet comes up to them). If you scrolled while it ran, it only moves when the Layouts are off screen.
- **Cancel** stops the build. Cancelled during the AI review, the layouts the engine drew come in, marked **Not reviewed by the AI** (**AI review cancelled**), its best one on the map, and nothing replaces them later. Closing the plan mid-review keeps them with the option the same way, without changing the map.
- **Planned first (Medium, High and Ultra):** before the layout engine places anything, the AI thinks the site through the way a planner sketches before drawing. It decides what goes at the front, in the middle and at the back and why (shops on the busy street, homes behind, the pond on the low land), where the ways in go, how the parking and the stormwater work, and checks the arithmetic (building, parking and pond against the acres). The run shows it as its first step (**Thinking the site through**, or **Thinking through 3 plans for the site** on Ultra). That plan sets the program and the layout settings the engine uses, and the review then judges each layout by whether it carries the plan out. After the build, a folded **Planned first** box under **Read as** shows the plan: the idea, what goes where, the ways in, parking, stormwater and the numbers, what it set aside and what to check; on Ultra also the other plans it drafted and why it picked this one. **Ask** can explain it ("why is the pond at the back?"). If the planning step doesn't answer, the build carries on and reads your request as before.
- **The AI's plan (shops, restaurants, offices, medical offices, hotels, churches and other commercial buildings; Medium, High and Ultra):** while it plans, the AI also writes down exactly what it wants built: how many buildings and pad sites, each one's use, size and floors, where it sits and which way it faces, whether the parking goes in front, round the buildings, at the sides or behind them, which side the pond goes on, and whether there's a service drive behind. It decides from the lot (its size and shape, the frontage, a corner), the setbacks, each use's parking rule and the stall and aisle sizes, the detention the site needs (on a Waller site, the guardrails' storage and pond size) and any easements, aiming for the most leasable floor area that parks and passes the checks. Floors are left open unless you name them, so an office or medical office can still go to a second floor. The layout engine draws the AI's plan beside its own layouts and ranks it against them by the same measure, the leasable floor area that parks and passes the checks: it's **Recommended** only when it does best, never just because it's the AI's. Its card is marked **AI's plan** and shows **Why · the AI's reasons** (one line for each decision; the rest under **more**) and **Changed by the planner** (what the engine had to change and why, or "Nothing: laid out as the AI asked"). Only a layout that keeps the AI's buildings and pads (as asked, or the nearest arrangement that fits) is marked **AI's plan**. When the engine can't keep them and has to lay out its own program instead, nothing is marked as the AI's: one line under Build says so, for example **The AI's plan (2 buildings, 120,000 sq ft, 4 pads, parking behind) didn't fit: room for 2 of the 4 pad sites; these are the planner's layouts**. On **High** and **Ultra**, when the engine can't do the plan as written (the parking falls short, it doesn't fit, a check fails, or it couldn't keep it at all), the AI gets the engine's reason, its notes, the numbers and the checks and revises its plan once; the revised one is laid out and compared too, marked **AI's plan · revised** with what changed. **Low** never asks the AI for a plan. If the AI doesn't answer, or its plan makes no sense for the lot, the engine's own layouts stand and the note under Build says so in one line; the build never waits on it. For a mix (shops in front of apartments, say) only the pad sites carry over to the shop zone.
- **How a request is read, for shops:** a drive-thru on a building ("a retail building with a drive-thru", "a strip with an endcap drive-thru") is that building's drive-thru, not a restaurant beside it: it stays one building. Separate uses are only laid out as a mix when you name them as separate buildings or areas ("a 10,000 sf strip and a 2,500 sf restaurant pad", "shops with apartments above"), and each part gets its size. A size with a plus or "at least" ("6000+ sf", "at least 6,000 sf", "6,000 sf or more") is a minimum: you get at least that much, and more where the parking and the land allow. "L-shaped" or "U-shaped" sets the building shape.
- **Pad sites from your words:** "with 2 pad sites", "two pads" or "an outparcel" puts that many restaurant or coffee pads with drive-thru lanes along the street, at every effort level, Low too; where the frontage beside the entry is too short, the layout says so.
- **Read as:** after a build, the program that was used shows as small tags under the request, e.g. **Read as: Retail · 25,000 sf · 1 story · 4/1,000 parking**, with what the AI assumed. If your request names a number the program doesn't have, it says so: **Not understood: 2 pads**, or **Not understood: 120 single-family homes** when the count wasn't picked up and the homes were laid out to fill the site instead. The homes' own numbers show too (**Homes 1,600–2,100 sf · 3 floor plans · Lots at least 6,600 sf**), so "1600-2100 SF homes across 3 floorplans, a minimum of 6600 SF per lot" is read as the homes' size, the number of house plans and the smallest lot, never as floor area or floors. **Edit** puts the request back in the box.
- Once there are layouts, the Build section folds to one line, **Built from "25,000 sf retail strip"** with a small tag for the effort it was built at (**High**, or **Ultra** with its spark) · **Edit request** · **New request**, so the layouts stay near the top.
- **What Should Go Here?** asks the AI for ideas before you decide. It reads what the app knows about the site (size and shape, street frontage, flood zone, nearby roads and traffic, utility districts and lines, environmental records), what's nearby (grocery, restaurants, gas, schools, hospitals, hotels and more, with distances) and local rents where known, and suggests three to five uses, best fit first, under the AI's short read of the site. Each shows how well it fits as a bar, why it fits, what to check, and the request it would build (**Will build: "…" · Edit** puts it in the box to change first). **Lay This Out** builds it; afterwards the ideas fold to one line, **Ideas (4) · show**, naming the one in use. They are starting points, not a market study.
- The AI turns your words into a program: the use, floor area, floors, units, number of buildings, parking ratio, setbacks and how stormwater is handled, filling gaps with typical Houston-area numbers and what it knows about the site. It lists what it assumed.
- **The AI then reviews the layouts.** The layout engine draws the options (the AI never draws), and the AI looks at each one, as a picture with its numbers and checks, the way a planner or developer would: access, parking next to the doors, buildings facing the street, wasted land, what was asked for. When the options miss, it changes the program (sizes, units, product, zones, extras, stormwater, curving streets, building shape) and the engine lays it out again; then it compares the old and new options and puts the best first. Each layout shows the AI's score out of 100 (green from 80, amber from 65, red below), and **Why** opens its verdict with what works (✓) and what to fix (!). The AI's first choice is marked **Recommended** with the reason in one line, its other remarks sit above the layouts, and layouts from its revised program are grouped under **AI's revision**, saying what it changed (for example "25,000 → 28,000 sf · why: the parking had room for more"). How far this goes depends on the effort level: on **High** it takes a minute or two and revises once; **Ultra** can revise twice; **Medium** scores the layouts and picks one but doesn't change the program; **Low** skips the review.
- **The whole site is used, as one plan.** The goal is the most leasable and sellable square footage the property holds, so the land what you ask for doesn't need gets more of the program: more homes, longer shop rows, a bigger building, more buildings of the size you asked for. It's all one plan, not a "Phase 2", and there's no "Future development · not in this phase" land unless you ask for it. The notes say what you asked for and what the land holds ("Asked for 4 buildings, 28,000 sq ft; the land holds 9 buildings, 63,000 sq ft with their parking and detention, so the plan builds them all"). **Leftover land** under Site parameters (and beside Build) changes it: **More of the program (fill the site)** (the default), **More of the program, marked Phase 2** (what you asked for is **Phase 1** and the rest is drawn lighter with dashed outlines as **Phase 2**; layouts then lead with Phase 1, "216 units · 8 buildings · as asked", with "+ Phase 2: 31 more buildings, 997 units" under it), or **Hold it for later** (only what you asked for, the rest kept as **Future development**). Your request can ask for it too: "leave the rest for a future phase" or "hold the back for later" holds it, "the rest as phase 2" marks it. What the AI's plan or review suggests never holds land on its own, and a plan an earlier build had set to hold land fills again on its next build unless you chose that yourself.
- **What you ask for is what it lays out, as closely as the site allows.** Ask for "28,000 sf of retail across 4 buildings" (or "4 buildings of 7,000 sf") and you get four buildings sharing the floor area evenly, spread along the rows with walks between them, not one big box. Apartments asked for in so many buildings are split the same way.
- The app then lays out **three layouts** (Layout 1, 2, 3) on the lot, each with its floor area, parking and coverage, and a name that says how it's arranged (for example "Layout 1 · Rows facing the street · pond at the entry" or "Layout 2 · Rows running back · pond at the back"). Click one to put it on the map; **Undo** goes back. The button is **Build Layouts**.
- Every option is laid out the way a site planner draws one: the drives come first, as one connected network (drive aisles joined by curved connector drives into loops), parking rows line the aisles, and the buildings sit between the rows facing their parking across a walk. Extra parking bays run between aisles where the buildings need more parking.
- **Aisles go through.** An aisle with stalls along it doesn't stop at a curb: it runs on until it meets a drive, or a short drive across the ends of the rows joins it to the aisle beside it (taking a few end stalls only when the plan has spaces to spare). Where the land leaves no way through, the last stalls at the end are left out so there's room to turn, if the plan can spare them; otherwise the plan's checks name the aisle and how many spaces are on it. No stall is left past the end of its aisle.
- **Parking stays back from the entrances.** The first stretch in from the street is a clear driveway with no stalls on it: about 30 ft for a small lot, 50 ft for a typical one, 75 ft for a big parking field (for shops, offices and the other commercial uses, 50 ft on a small site, 75 ft from about 120 spaces and 100 ft from about 400). Adding an entrance by hand clears the stalls within 50 ft of the street beside it, and the checks flag any stall left at an entrance's mouth (cars backing out would meet cars turning in).
- **Every option is usable:** it stays inside the setbacks, nothing overlaps, it has enough parking, and every drive and parking row is reached from the entry. The app tries many arrangements and only offers ones that pass; it always shows three.
- **Shops, restaurants, offices, medical offices, hotels and churches are laid out as a parking field,** the way a civil engineer sketches one:
  - the parking is whole rows of stalls on both sides of each aisle (a single row only along an edge), straight and lined up, never a stub of fewer than six stalls;
  - a 26 ft fire lane runs up each side of the field and joins every aisle at both ends, so no aisle dead-ends and every wall of every building is within 150 ft of a fire lane (only where no layout with a drive round both ends of every aisle fits at all, such as a one-acre or very narrow lot, do the aisles stop at one end with room to turn, and the layout says so);
  - the main entry comes straight in and meets the first aisle square on, with no stall beside it for the stacking distance; a corner lot gets a second way in from the side street;
  - shops and restaurants get a service and loading drive behind the building, joined to the field at both ends, with the trash enclosures on it when they're turned on (a lot too small for one says so);
  - planted islands end every row and break long rows about every 13th stall, and the accessible stalls are nearest the building doors;
  - the detention is one pond on the land the field can't use: the back, the far end, a wedge's point.
- **Small commercial lots (about an acre to three):** where a field with fire lanes up both sides doesn't fit, one building goes at the back with its parking in front and a drive round it: the way in comes straight up one side with stalls along it, an aisle crosses the front with a row facing the storefronts, and the drive round the back joins both ends, so every aisle still goes through and the building is in reach of a fire lane. The building is a bar, or an **L** (or a U) when the request, the AI's plan or Site parameters asks for one, its wing toward the parking so the storefronts face it; on a corner lot the L opens toward the corner when both ways fit. A size given as a minimum ("6,000+ sf") is laid out at least that big and grown where the parking allows. When the asked size doesn't park, the layout shows the most that does and says the parking is what binds (how many spaces the full size needs and how many this lot holds).
- **Drive-thrus:** a shop or restaurant with a drive-thru gets a one-way lane round the building, counter-clockwise so the pickup window is on the driver's side, with the order point two cars before the window and at least 8 cars of stacking behind the order point, and a bypass lane alongside so a car can leave the line and deliveries can drive round. On a bigger site the lane wraps the restaurant, or the shop at the end of a row, from the aisle round its back. The plan and the PDF mark the window and the order point and arrow the lane's direction; the checks flag a lane that stacks fewer than 8 cars, or a drive-thru asked for and not drawn, and a layout that has no room for one says so.
- **Detention underground on a small lot:** where a pond would cost the building (an acre or two, or a Waller site whose detention is sized from its volume), the layout puts the stormwater in vaults under the parking and says how many acre-feet; a bigger lot keeps its pond.
- **"As much as fits" says what stopped it:** the buildings grow (deeper, longer, more of them) until the parking, the setbacks and fire lanes, the detention or a zoning limit on coverage or floor area stops them, and the note says which. An office or medical office whose floors you didn't set goes up to two floors before building less ("20,000 sq ft on two floors: on one floor the building and its 100 spaces don't fit"), and an ask that doesn't fit says how much does and why.
- **When nothing fits** (100,000 sf of storage on a third of an acre, say), the Layouts section says so and stays that way: "Nothing fits … on this 0.30-acre site", with what to try (ask for less, add a parcel, or place pieces by hand) and an **Edit request** button.
- **Amenities go where people use them:** one pool per community, always on a deck beside the clubhouse or community center that runs it. Apartments get their clubhouse and pool right by the entry; a commons beside them holds the park, playground, pavilion and lake rather than a second clubhouse and pool (homeowners in a mixed neighbourhood do get their own community center and pool). A commons' amenities line the drive that serves it, with the lake and lawns behind, never out on their own by the pond. The checks flag a pool away from its clubhouse and a second pool for one community of rentals.
- **Apartments** get a clubhouse right by the entry with its pool on a deck beside it, garden-style buildings with staggered fronts, and a walking trail around the pond. **Shops** face their parking with pad sites (with drive-thru lanes) along the street. **Warehouses** get a truck court on the dock side. **Townhomes** sit in two rows per lane, garages on the lane, with guest parking along the lanes; where the rows run out of room for it, the end homes of the longest rows give way to a bay of guest spaces, so a townhome option always meets its own parking.
- Leave the size out and it fits as much as the parking and detention allow; give a size or a number of units and it lays out that much. If it doesn't all fit, the option says how much did ("Fits about 160 of the 200 units asked for"). A fixed program gets the parking it needs plus about a tenth to spare, not every row the site could hold. On a deep site, shops and offices go in several rows (buildings in the lot with parking on both sides), not one row with empty land behind it. With **Hold it for later**, the land left is kept in one piece as a **Future pad site** or **Future development** (with its acreage); when the site is filled and a piece still can't hold another building with its parking, it's marked **Pad site** (room for a pad building) or **Open land** (room to build more), never land held for a later phase. On a big tract, a "compact, room to expand" option builds on just the part of the frontage it needs. Leave the size out and the plan fills the site with as much leasable building as parks, with only the landscape the buffers, islands and stormwater need.
- **Stormwater** is sized from the land actually developed: about 0.55 acre-feet of storage per acre (or the detention rate on the place's rule sheet, see Street rules for the place), in a pond about 5 ft deep (roughly 13% of the developed land). It's a **detention** pond (a dry basin) by default, a **retention** pond (a wet pond with a permanent pool) when you ask for one, **underground vaults** under the parking (no pond) or **none** for a regional facility. Sites under an acre get none unless you choose it. Ponds are clean rectangles (or straight-edged along an L-shaped strip) beside the buildings, at the back, at the entry or at the far end of the rows depending on the option; when it can't hold it all, the option says how much would go underground.
- **Paving is one surface.** Drives, parking rows and truck courts are drawn as one paved area with a single curb line around the outside, the way the asphalt is poured, so there are no lines where a drive meets a parking row (on the map, the sheet and in CAD). Click a piece to see its own outline.
- **Markings:** stall stripes along every row, **accessible (ADA) stalls** in the rows nearest the buildings (as many as the federal table asks for, one in six van-accessible, in blue with a striped access aisle shared by each pair), and at the entry a stop bar for traffic leaving and a double yellow centre line (none where the entry has a median).
- **Trash enclosures** are left out unless you turn them on under Site parameters (or add one with Draw). When on, they're placed by the usual rules: apartments get a double enclosure (trash and recycling) for about every 50 units, or a compactor plus recycling from 200 units; shops and offices one for about every 25,000 sf, none more than about 250 ft from a building; a restaurant its own; a warehouse one per building; homes roll carts to the curb, so none. Each sits beside a drive aisle or in place of a few spare parking stalls, gate facing the drive for the truck, at least 10 ft from a building. If the plan can't find room for all of them, the option says so. Your city's solid waste rules decide in the end.
- Land the layout can't use for buildings becomes lawns, a dog park or a playground on a fill-the-site plan, not unexplained empty space.
- **Clean drawing:** the whole site is drawn as landscape under what's built on it, so there are no lawn shapes with ragged edges; ponds and parking row ends are square by default (rounded and natural curves are still a choice under Look).
- **Trees** are drawn as tree canopies on the sheet and as 3D trees on the map. The **Trees** button in the Draw by hand section turns them on and off (as does Trees under Site parameters › Look).
- **Building shapes:** bars (apartments with stepped fronts), **L-shaped**, **courtyard (U-shaped)** and **towers on a two-floor podium**, chosen for the whole plan under Site parameters (Building shape) or for one building in the Selected box. In 3D, buildings show a parapet round the roof and a line at each floor, and a tower rises from its podium.
- **Restaurant pads** get outdoor seating by the door and a drive-thru lane round the building. **Playgrounds** are laid out with a play structure, slide, swings and a bench, not a circle.
- **Curved pieces:** connector drives turn on arcs, the entry has curb returns and a planted median (a real boulevard where it fits: see Entrances below), parking rows end in rounded planted islands, and ponds, the pool and lawns are free-form shapes.
- **Paving over lawn, never under a park:** plain lawn is drawn under the drives and parking, so a drive is never hidden beneath grass. Parks, lawns with a name and open space are kept clear of the streets; where one does overlap a drive or a building it's outlined in red and the drive shows on top. An area drawn with the area tool (Landscape 1, 2…) counts as plain lawn, such as an island or a planted strip, until you give it a name of its own.
- **Without the AI:** if the AI isn't set up, the app reads your words directly and still lays out three layouts. After its first try it says so in one line, **AI isn't set up on this site**; **What Should Go Here?**, **Ask** and the Medium, High and Ultra effort levels are greyed out (Low is used), the button reads **Build Layouts · From your words (AI off)**, and only suggestions the app can read fully from the words are offered (no "with 2 pads", which needs the AI). It tries the AI again after about five minutes.
- **Townhomes and duplexes on a court:** when the homes asked for won't fit on lots of their own along a street ("24 duplex units" on an acre and a half), the plan lays them out the way a rental community on one tract is built: two-story rows of six townhomes facing a shared parking court, a turnaround at the end, a fenced patio behind each home and the unit lines drawn in, and it says why. Ask for a "pull-through drive" and a second drive runs between the buildings out to the side street.
- **Mixed use:** describe several uses ("37,700 sf retail with 2 pads, a commons with a lake, 120 apartments, 18 townhomes and 12 duplex units") and the site is laid out in zones from the street back, on one connected drive network:
  - shops at the front facing their parking, with pad sites along the street and a service drive behind;
  - a commons with an amenity lake that holds the site's detention, a pavilion, pickleball courts, a playground and restrooms;
  - apartment buildings with a clubhouse and pool, townhome rows on private lanes, and duplexes on a street at the back;
  - on a wide site, a main boulevard runs from the entry through every zone.
  - On a lot too shallow for every zone one behind the other, smaller zones share a row side by side (townhomes beside duplexes, the commons beside the apartments). A small program on a big tract is built along the part of the frontage it needs, and the rest is kept for a future phase only when you hold land for later; otherwise shops and offices grow along their rows (beside homes too, when the homes have no set number: the planner weighs a longer shop row against a row of homes by the square footage each adds). Each option notes anything that didn't fully fit.
  - **Homes and a pond on a shallow site:** when the shops, a street of homes and a detention pond across the back don't fit one behind the other in the site's depth (a long, shallow tract on a highway, say), the pond goes beside the homes instead: a strip at one end of the rows, half at each end, or in the land the homes leave open, with shallower shop buildings (70 ft) where that's what it takes. Or the pond goes **beside the shops**, on the stretch of frontage they don't need (and down that end of the site for what doesn't fit there), so the homes get the whole width of the site behind: on a 20-acre tract 630 ft deep under the Waller guardrails that's about 37 homes instead of 32, with fewer homes facing the back of the shops. These layouts are named "· pond beside the shops". Among the layouts there's always the one with the most homes that still keeps the shops' size and the detention. Only if that still doesn't fit does a layout hold less detention, and its notes say how much ("Ponds hold about 75% of the detention the site needs; plan on underground storage or a regional facility for the rest"). A plan that asks for homes never comes back without them unless nothing fits, and then the layout says so first: how deep the site is, what doesn't fit together, and what would make room (underground detention, fewer or smaller shops, or the homes alone). A pods layout, where one can be drawn, still goes ahead of these.
- **Mixed sites in pods:** on a mixed site of 40 acres or more (or a smaller one, from 15 acres, when you say where a use goes or the zones-from-the-street layouts can't fit the program), the planner also lays the site out the way master-planned communities are drawn, and ranks those layouts with the zones-from-the-street ones. A pods layout only goes first when it keeps every use within 3% of what the zones-from-the-street layout it beats gives, counting each use up to the number you asked for (homes beyond the number asked are counted by the square footage they add, not as a use kept). A pods plan with a bigger lake but a fifth fewer homes comes after it, unless the homes had no number and the zones-from-the-street layout misses placements you asked for that the pods plan keeps. The best zones-from-the-street layout always stays in the list, and the best pods layout does when it keeps more of your placements or near as much of the program:
  - a **collector road** goes in first: the main entry boulevard, then a **loop road** round a central commons and lake, a loop road one block in from the lot lines, or a **spine road** with a T at its end; side streets get a connector with their own way in. Greenbelts with a trail run where the loop's corners meet the lot lines;
  - those roads cut the site into **pods**, and each pod gets one use and its own street direction (apartments can face the highway while the homes run another way). Each pod is laid out the way a whole site of that use would be, with straight streets for now; its way in comes off the collector road. Shops get a block on the street about as deep as it is wide. Each use is first sized by a quick trial, so a pod gets about the land its use really takes and the rest goes to the homes; land a pod still doesn't need is left as **open space** (never a blank), and when the lake falls short the planner turns the larger open spaces into simple, rounded ponds clear of the buildings and lots;
  - **say where things go** and the pods follow it: "retail at the corner of FM 362 and Bauer Road", "apartments along the highway" (or "facing", "on the frontage"), "the commons in the middle", "homes at the back", "townhomes as a buffer between the retail and the homes", "about 600 homes around a lake" (the commons then holds the lake and the homes go round it), "shops on the left corner", "apartments along Highway 6" (that road by name). With the AI, it reads the same from any wording; without it, the app reads these phrases itself, with the sizes said beside them ("120 apartments next to 20,000 sf of retail"). A commons said with a lake and other things ("a commons with a lake, pool and playground") keeps its lake among them; a lake alone gives the usual commons, lake included. Road names come from the map where it has them; "along the highway" means the front street when it doesn't;
  - the notes say which placements were met ("Retail at the corner of FM 362 and Bauer Road.") and which weren't, and why ("Retail isn't at the corner: no side street is marked (Side Street on the toolbar)."), what didn't fully fit ("Apartments: fits about 279 of 300 units."; a pods layout often holds fewer homes than asked, as its lake holds all the detention), when one of a use's pods didn't lay out and was left as open space, and how much of the detention the ponds hold when it's short. A pods layout where a whole use came to nothing isn't offered. When no pods layout could be made, the other layouts say which placements they don't keep;
  - not yet: retail pads aren't laid out in a pod of shops, there are no roundabouts where the collector roads meet, and the commons' parking runs along the loop road. Known rough edges: a commons at the back on the loop-road layout can be split over two or three pods, each with its own lake and amenities; a townhome pod can hold more than asked at the homes' expense; in a plain comma list ("20,000 sf retail, 200 apartments, homes at the back") the placement can also be read onto the use named before it; "apartment homes around a courtyard" is read as houses without the AI; and a pod renamed with Change With AI takes its old name back after a re-fit;
  - a part laid out in several pods is named in sections ("Homes · Section 2"); the map and the PDF sheet show the part's name over its largest pod and a smaller **Section 2**, **Section 3**… over the others. On the PDF sheet every mixed plan's part names (pods or not) are now sized to fit inside their part;
  - **Settings › Mixed sites › Layout style** chooses **Pods and bands (best first)** (the default), **Pods round a loop road** (pods are tried on any mixed site of 15 acres or more; for now they're still ranked and checked like the default, so a pods layout that holds less of the program may not be offered) or **Bands from the street back** (the zones-from-the-street layouts only). Garages, warehouses and self storage are always laid out in bands. On a 250-acre site a build with pods takes about a minute.
  - **Re-lay a pod or swap two:** select any piece in a pod and the Selected box says which pod it's in ("In Apartments · Multifamily · 18.8 ac"), with **Re-lay This Pod** (it's laid out again from scratch round anything you moved there by hand; the rest of the plan stays) and **Swap With…** (pick another pod: the two trade uses and both are laid out again; pieces you'd moved by hand in them go with the rest, and the notes say so; a pod too small for what it's given says how much fits). The commons and its lake stay put. One **Undo** takes it back; an edit made while a pod is being laid out isn't overwritten (it asks you to try again). Change With AI does the same from words: "re-lay the apartments", "redo this pod", "swap the apartments and the retail", "make the north section townhomes", "make the retail pod 80,000 sf", "make the apartments pod 4 stories", and "call the north section The Lakes" renames it. Moving or turning a whole pod isn't done from words: it says so, and you can select its pieces instead.
  - **Re-fits keep the pods:** after you move or add a piece, only the pod it's in is laid out again round it (a piece dragged into another pod belongs to that one now, and the pod it left is laid out again to fill the gap); every other pod stays exactly as it was. A new home mix lays every pod of homes out again. If a pod ends up holding noticeably less than before, the notes say how much ("Apartments: 196 units round what moved, 242 before."), and homes that were in the way of what moved are counted. If you move a collector road off the road it met, a short drive joins it back; the pods it runs into are laid out again round it where they can, and a lake it runs into is cut back from it rather than lost. If you delete a collector road, each pod it served is joined to the nearest street still reached from a way in; one that can't be is left out and the notes say so, as they do for homes left with no street.
- **Detail:** buildings are named and sized ("Apt Bldg 1 · 40 units · 42,353 sf · 3 fl", "Pad A", "Townhome Row 2"), parking has planted islands with trees, buildings have walks around them, and trees line the landscape buffer and the lake.

**Single-family homes:** choose **Single-family homes** (or say "single-family homes, mostly 2-story"). Homes go on lots along 28 ft streets, both sides of each street, with lot lines and driveways. On a site of 8 acres or more the planner lays the streets out the way real subdivisions are drawn and offers them alongside the parallel streets of before, best first:
  - **a collector first:** a loop road one lot in from the edge (the homes along it back onto the lot lines), a main street up the middle, or a loop road round a central lake and commons; the entry boulevard meets it at a T, and on a deep site a spine road crosses the loop a little along from the entry, so the boulevard keeps its median. The street in from the entry has no homes on it before its first junction. A neighbourhood of more than 30 homes gets a second way in, a big one (more than 300 homes, or over 60 acres) a third, for fire access: off the front or a side street, each meeting a street square-on and clear of the other junctions on it, or opening the end of a cul-de-sac out to the street (never a drive into a cul-de-sac). When there's no clear spot for one, the layout says so and isn't put first.
  - **local streets inside:** curving with the land or round the loop (concentric streets, loop lanes, long gently curving streets), meeting the collector at T's rather than crossroads, with cross streets where a block would run long and cul-de-sacs where land is left or a street would dead-end. Where the land left inside is too thin for homes between two streets, one street runs up the middle of it instead, with homes both sides; streets never run into each other or into themselves. A loop road usually comes two ways: streets following the loop (the most homes) and the same streets winding gently, with cul-de-sacs in the pockets of land they leave ("· winding streets", a few percent fewer homes).
  - **lots along the curves:** marched along each street's edge, each square to the street, wedge-shaped round the cul-de-sacs and on the outside of bends, each home turned to face its street with a driveway to it.
  - **the pond** on land kept for it at the back (with a walking trail round it), or as the lake in the middle; the rest of the land open space. With a commons asked for (25 acres and up), the loop goes round it: the community center, pool and lake in the middle, homes all round (on an L-shaped or wedge-shaped parcel too, wherever the loop fits; when it can't, the notes say so).
  - **street rules:** the place's own rule sheet when it has one (see Street rules for the place below), otherwise typical values (blocks up to 1,400 ft, cul-de-sacs up to 800 ft, junctions at least 125 ft apart, 300 ft on a collector); the notes, the layout cards and the PDF sheet say which rules the layout followed. The checks warn when a hand edit breaks one ("A block on Loop road runs 1,650 ft") or leaves two streets running into each other, and update as soon as a street is bent, moved, added or deleted.
  - **Re-fits keep the streets:** move a home, add a park or change the home mix and the homes are laid out again along the same streets (Re-fit Plan too: it never swaps the subdivision for parallel streets); bend a street with Edit Shape and only the lots along that stretch re-flow; the rest of the neighbourhood stays where it was. Delete a home (or have Change With AI remove one) and its lot stays empty: re-fits leave that spot vacant and don't make the count up by moving homes elsewhere; delete the empty lot too and a later re-fit can build there again. The streets themselves don't move: a park or building dropped on a street cuts it back clear of it, each end becoming a cul-de-sac (the notes say so; move the park off the street to keep it whole). Delete a street and its homes go with it, along with any cul-de-sac at its end; streets left with no way in keep no homes until they're joined up again, and the notes say so. Delete the main entry and another way in becomes the main entry. An unchanged plan re-fits exactly as it was.
  - **Editing the streets:** select a street and the Selected box says what it is ("Street · Oak Lane · 820 ft · T's at both ends") with what can be done to it, each one **Undo** away, the lots re-flowing round the change and the rest of the neighbourhood staying where it was:
    - **Make It a Cul-de-sac** ends a dead end in a cul-de-sac, or cuts a street that T's in at both ends back from the end farther from the main entry and ends it in one. A street that's the only way to somewhere says so and stays, and one that already ends in a cul-de-sac says that.
    - **Delete This Stretch** takes out the stretch between the two streets that meet it either side of where you clicked (or of the middle of the piece, when it was picked another way); its homes go, the rest of the street stays. A stretch that's the only way to the streets beyond it says so and stays. A street left ending more than 150 ft past its last junction with no cul-de-sac shows in the checks until it's made a cul-de-sac or joined up (a fire truck needs room to turn).
    - **Curve It** bows a straight street gently, its ends where they are, and the streets that T into it come along (hold Shift for the other way); the main street can be curved this way too. A bow that would run into another street is made gentler, or refused with a reason. Loops (the loop road, loop lanes), the ways in and streets meeting a roundabout don't curve this way: drag their points instead.
    - **Re-Lay This Block** lays the local streets of the block it's in out again another way, keeping the collectors, the entry and every home outside the block exactly where they were, then and at every re-fit after; each press on the same block tries another way, and the result says how many homes the block has now and had before (often fewer: the block's first layout was the best the generator found). It lays the best few other ways out in full and shows the one that holds the most homes first, the next press the next. On a collector it re-lays the block on the side you clicked; Change With AI asks for a street or home inside the block when the loop road or a collector is all it has to go on. It won't lay out a way that breaks more street rules than the plan does now, or that leaves a street with no way in. With street names on, the block's new streets are named too (the rest keep theirs).
    - **Dragging a point** of a street bends it smoothly round the point (the streets meeting it come along, so the junctions hold); double-clicking a street no longer turns it into a shape to edit.
    - Change With AI does the same from words: "make this street a cul-de-sac", "curve the main street", "bend Oak Lane 60 ft to the left", "re-lay this block" (with a street or home selected), "remove this street".
  - **Street names:** turn on **Street names** (Site parameters › Buildings) and every street is named: the main entry and collectors grander (Parkway, Boulevard), the local streets Lane, Way or Drive, cul-de-sacs Court or Circle. Give a **Theme** ("golf", "Texas rivers") and press **Name the Streets** for the AI's names; without the AI it uses a list of plain names, and says so. Names are never repeated within the plan or like a road on the map nearby (check the county's 911 list too). The names run along the streets on the map (zoomed in) and on the PDF sheet. Rename a street in the Selected box's **Street name** field (a name too like another street's here or a road nearby, "Bunker Hill" beside "Bunker", is refused, as it would be when naming); turning names off keeps them for when they're turned on again. While names are on, a new layout (New Layouts, or another option picked) gets its streets named too. A big community gets names past the plain list too (Oakwood, Cedarbrook…). Change With AI understands "name the streets after Texas rivers", "turn off street names" and "call the loop road Heritage Parkway" (naming one street switches names on and keeps the name you gave it; it never renames the others). On a plan without subdivision streets it says street names need them.
  - With a number of homes asked for ("300 homes"), they go nearest the entry and only the streets those homes need are built (the loop road and the rest wait for a later phase, kept as land for it).

  **Neighbourhood streets** under Site parameters picks the style: **Like a subdivision, and gentle curves** (the default: both kinds, best first), **Like a subdivision** (loops and cul-de-sacs only), **Parallel, gently curving** (the streets of before) or **Straight**. Set the **home mix** (the share of 1-, 2- and 3-story homes) and the lot depth under Site parameters; each home type takes its own lot width (about 60, 50 and 40 ft), or the rule sheet's smallest frontage when that's wider.

  **The homes as you describe them:** say the homes' size ("1,600–2,100 sf homes", "2,400 sf homes"), how many floor plans ("3 floorplans", "four house plans") and the smallest lot ("lots at least 6,600 sf", "60 x 120 lots"), and the neighbourhood is built from that many of the builder's house plans in that size range, spread across it (one near each end, the rest between), in turn along each street, each lot as wide as its plan needs and at least the smallest lot asked. Where the place's rules or the Waller guardrails ask for a bigger lot, the bigger one wins. The same goes for the homes in a mix, the parallel streets, subdivision streets and pods alike. **Site parameters › Homes** shows and sets the smallest and largest home, the number of floor plans and the smallest lot; leave them empty and the home mix decides as before.

**Street rules for the place:** the **Street rules** group in Site parameters reads the subdivision rules and drainage criteria that apply where the site is, so the layouts follow the local code rather than typical values.
  - **Read the Local Rules** has the AI search the place's code (the city's when the site is in a city; for a site outside one, it first works out whether it's in a city's extraterritorial jurisdiction (ETJ) and says what it decided and why, to be confirmed). It takes a minute or two and can be cancelled. It reads street widths (right-of-way and paving for local streets and collectors), the longest block and cul-de-sac, the cul-de-sac bulb, junction spacing, the narrowest lot and smallest lot area, the front and side building lines, driveway spacing and how far back from a corner, sidewalks, when a second way in is needed, and the detention rate.
  - **Every value shows its source:** a link to the page it came from (hover for the section and the words quoted). A value the AI couldn't quote is marked **Unsure**; one it couldn't find at all is filled from Waller County's rules and marked **Waller default**. Either can be checked and **Confirm**ed, or changed by typing a new value (marked **Edited**, with who did it).
  - **The layouts follow the sheet:** street widths, block and cul-de-sac lengths, junction spacing, lot widths and areas and the building lines shape the streets and lots, in the subdivision layouts and the parallel-street ones alike, homes pods included (a lot on a gently curving street is made wider where the curve narrows it, so it still holds the sheet's smallest lot; each layout says which rules it used, and any lots it still couldn't make as big as the sheet asks); the plan's own street widths, set by hand, count over the sheet's on parallel streets as on subdivision ones; the driveway spacing and corner distance shape the entrances; the detention rate sizes the pond (and shows as the Storage under Stormwater unless you set one there); sidewalks show in Numbers. Applying a sheet, or editing one of its values, lays new layouts out with it unless the plan has hand edits (then it asks first, as with any setting); confirming a value as it stands changes nothing on the plan. **Use Typical Values** goes back. A stricter sheet means fewer homes (wider lots, wider streets, deeper building lines); that's the code, not a fault.
  - **Shared with the team:** a sheet read for a place is kept for everyone on the team, so the next plan there starts with it (and anyone's confirmations and edits travel with it); a sheet over a year old offers **Refresh**. Refresh keeps what the team typed in, the confirmations on values that came back the same, and the values read before that the AI couldn't find this time; when the search fails altogether, the sheet stays as it was. A plan whose site hasn't had its rules read yet is offered the team's county sheet (**Use It**) rather than given it, since the site might be in a city's ETJ. When someone on the team updates a sheet your plan uses, it offers **Use the Update**.
  - **Without the AI** (or when the search finds nothing), **Use Waller Defaults** fills the sheet with Waller County's rules. Some of those are provisional: Waller's detention rate hasn't been read from its drainage criteria yet, so 0.55 acre-feet per developed acre (the typical Harris County figure, the same as with no sheet) stands in, marked to confirm.
  - **Never a compliance claim:** the sheet is the AI's reading of the code; the notes, the cards and the PDF say so, and say how many values are still to confirm. Confirm with the engineer and the city or county.

**Tiny home and manufactured home communities:** ask for "a tiny home community", "a mobile home park with 60 lots" or "a tiny home / mobile home community laid out like an RV park". It is homes on lots, never self storage.
- **How it's laid out:** like an RV park or a manufactured home community: long private lanes (22–24 ft) with a row of home lots on each side, the lanes joined at both ends so every lane is part of a loop, rows of lots along the property line and the end lanes, and the entry drive straight in. The pond goes across one end, down one side, or in a corner (one option always has it in a corner when it fits, the way RV park plans often do).
- **Pad sizes:** say the pad or lot size ("25 x 55 pads", "40 x 100 lots") and every lot is that size. A reference file with an RV park's site size (like "134 RV lots, 25' × 55' typ") is read the same way.
- **Homes sized to order:** with only a pad size, the homes are sized to fill the pad (a 25 × 55 pad holds a 15 × 32, 480 sf tiny home with 10 ft to the next home and its parking in front). Say a home size instead ("550 sf tiny homes", "16 x 40 homes") and the homes are that size, with the pads made as big as they need (550 sf on 25 ft pads: 15 × 37 homes on 60 ft deep pads), and the plan says so. With nothing said, the standard tiny and manufactured home models are used.
- **Out to the property line:** the lots run to the property line, as an RV park's do; say "a 10 ft buffer" for a landscape strip. The rows along the property line are made deeper where needed so the homes keep the plan's setbacks (Site parameters › Setbacks).
- **The homes:** manufactured homes (single-wides 16 × 56 and 16 × 76, double-wides 28 × 52 and 28 × 64 on lots about 40–50 ft wide and 100 ft deep), tiny homes (a 12 × 32 tiny home, a 14 × 40 park model and a 20 × 40 cottage on lots about 30–36 ft wide and 65 ft deep), or both. Each home sits near the front of its lot with a two-car parking pad beside it (one car, in front, on a lot too narrow for both). They also show in the building library as **Manufactured homes** and **Tiny homes**.
- **What you can say:** a number of homes or lots (that many, nearest the entry), home sizes, or lot sizes. The sheet and Numbers call it a manufactured, tiny, or tiny & manufactured home community and count homes and parking pads (not garages).
- **Rented out:** the pro forma values the community as rentals, the home and its pad rented together (a tiny home by its size, about $1,150 a month for 550 sf and $1,000 for 400 sf, in line with the one-bedroom tiny homes asked $925–1,195 round Magnolia, Hockley and Conroe in October 2026; $1,300 for a manufactured home), with the homes bought, delivered and set ($150 a sf for tiny homes, $80 for manufactured), a finished pad with utilities at $30,000 each, $2,400 a home a year to run, 6% management, 6% vacancy and a 6.75% cap rate. All of them can be changed in the pro forma. For a lot-rent-only park (residents own their homes), lower the rent to the lot rent and take the home cost out.
- **Not checked:** spacing between homes, lane widths and fire lanes follow typical values; confirm them against the local manufactured home or RV park rules and with the fire marshal. The detention is the app's usual estimate, not the engineer's (the King Ln exhibit shows 2.2 acres; the app sizes about 1.2).

**Sites reached by a narrow strip (flag lots):** when the only road frontage is a narrow strip running back to the main part of the land (31807 King Ln: a 60 ft strip about 250 ft long from Old Washington Rd), the main part is laid out on its own and the main entry is a drive straight up the strip to it. The plan says so and notes that with one way in, the fire marshal has to sign off on fire access. Before, the entry was put on the back edge of the main part, where it meets the neighbour's land, not a road.

**Self storage:** say it the way you would to a developer ("self storage: a 3-story, 90,000 sf climate-controlled building plus drive-up units and 30 RV spaces", "drive-up only mini storage") or choose **Self storage** under Site parameters.
- **How it's laid out:**
  - The climate-controlled building sits at the street with its leasing office and a few customer spaces in front, behind a landscaped strip.
  - Drive-up rows run behind it on 26 ft drive aisles, with doors straight onto the aisle. Rows are 40 ft deep back to back, 30 ft along the back. Cross drives cut through rows longer than about 250 ft.
  - RV and boat stalls (12 × 40 ft) go along the back when you ask for them.
  - A security fence runs from the building's corners around the yard, with a keypad gate wherever a drive goes through it. A detention pond at the front edge stays outside the fence.
- **On a small lot** (about an acre), the building goes right on the street with visitor spaces just inside the gate, so the yard still gets its rows.
- **Drive-up only:** a small leasing office replaces the climate-controlled building. **Climate-controlled only:** no drive-up rows and no fence.
- **Site parameters › Program** for self storage:
  - parking per 1,000 sf (about 1 space per 100 units);
  - climate-controlled floors and size (0 = drive-up only, blank = sized to the site);
  - drive-up size (blank = as much as fits);
  - drive-up row depth, drive aisle width and RV / boat spaces;
  - **Fence and keypad gate** on or off.
- **Unit mixes:** two tables, one for climate-controlled units and one for drive-up units. Change a size's square feet or share, remove it, or add one with **+ Add a Size**. The averages set the unit counts. When the shares don't add up to 100% the line under the table says so (they're scaled to 100%), and with every share at 0% it says what each unit is counted at.
- **The numbers:** the layout card leads with rentable square feet and about how many units. **Numbers** has a **Storage** tile (rentable sf, units split climate / drive-up, RV / boat spaces) and a table of units by size. RV and boat stalls don't count as parking.
- **Selected building:** a **Storage** field (Climate-controlled, Drive-up units or Leasing office) and a line with its rentable sf and units.
- **Pro forma:** self storage is gross rent on the units' rentable square feet (about $1.35 a sf a month climate-controlled, $0.95 drive-up, and about $125 a month for an RV or boat space) with the owner paying the operating costs, not billed back. It assumes about 12% vacancy and discounts and two to three years to fill. The rentable share of each building (about 72% climate-controlled, 92% drive-up) is editable, and so is everything else.
- If the climate-controlled building or the RV stalls don't fully fit, the layout says how much did.

**Food truck parks:** say it plainly ("a food truck park with 12 trucks, a covered pavilion, restrooms and a small bar", "food truck court with 8 truck stalls, no bar") or choose **Food truck park** under Site parameters.
- **How it's laid out:**
  - Customers park on the street side and walk straight into the park. No parking sits at the mouth of the entrance.
  - The trucks stand on concrete stalls (15 × 35 ft, each with a power and water pedestal) around a gathering area: along the back and down both sides (**Trucks round the commons**), in one row along the back (**Trucks along the back**), or, on a lot too narrow for trucks on both sides, along the back and down one long side with the seating beside them (**Trucks down one side**). A lot 150 to 180 ft wide and about 900 ft deep holds 10 to 13 trucks this way. Each truck's serving window faces the seating, with a strip of plaza in front for people to queue and order.
  - A **service lane** runs behind the stalls, up one side, across the back and down the other. Trucks pull in and out along it without crossing the seating. It joins the end of the parking aisle at both ends, so nothing has to back out.
  - On a corner lot, the lane also gets its own **Service entry** from the side street, so the trucks come and go without driving through the customer parking.
  - In the middle: a covered **pavilion** with picnic seating on a lawn (room for lawn games and shade trees), **restrooms**, a **bar** with a **beer garden** patio at the front corner, and a **playground** away from the bar.
  - A dumpster and grease enclosure sits on the lane, its gate facing the lane.
  - Detention goes on the land left over (at the back, the front or a side). When there isn't room for all of it, the layout says how much the pond holds and that the rest goes underground.
  - When you ask for a number of trucks, a layout with that many comes first. Otherwise, layouts that keep detention in a pond come first when they hold nearly as many trucks as one without a pond. There's always at least one pond layout among the choices to compare.
  - The choices are different from each other. Two layouts with the same trucks, about the same seats, and the same bar and pond count as one. Then you may see only two layouts.
- **On a small lot** (about an acre), the trucks come first and every layout is fully parked:
  - The bar shrinks, or is left out, so the parking can carry the trucks. The layout says so. The **Bar** switch stays on, so a bigger site gets the bar back.
  - Then the pavilion gets smaller, so the restrooms still fit beside it.
  - Every layout has restrooms, because the health code requires them. If they don't fit, the bar gives up its room, then a truck at a time. If they still don't fit, the site gets no food truck layout.
  - On a narrow frontage the entrance can sit toward one end, which leaves room for more parking along the street.
  - About an acre holds 4 trucks round a smaller pavilion (about 90 seats). An acre and a half holds 5 or 6. If you asked for more trucks than fit, the layout says how many it found room for.
- **Site parameters › Program** for a food truck park:
  - number of food trucks (blank = as many as fit, as many as the parking carries);
  - parking per truck (about 5) and bar parking per 1,000 sf;
  - pavilion, bar and restroom sizes (0 for the pavilion leaves it out);
  - **Bar or beer garden** and **Playground** on or off.
- **The numbers:** the layout card leads with the trucks and about how many seats. **Numbers** has a **Food trucks** tile (trucks, seats at about 15 sf of covered or patio seating each) and a **Park buildings** tile (pavilion, restrooms, bar and patio sizes). Parking needed is the trucks' share plus the bar's.
- **Selected:** a truck stall shows its size, its pedestal and the parking it brings. A pavilion, bar or restrooms building has a **Part** field and a line with its size (and seats, for the pavilion).
- **Pro forma:**
  - stall rent per truck per month (about $1,200);
  - the bar either run by the owner (sales a sf and a net margin) or leased triple net (rent a sf);
  - the park's costs per truck and a fixed amount a year (a manager, insurance, events);
  - the cost of each stall's pad and hookups.
  - It assumes about 10% vacancy and six months to fill. All of this is editable.
- If not all the trucks asked for fit, or there's no room for the bar or playground, the layout says so.

**Gas stations and corner sites:** say it the way you would to a developer ("gas station with 8 pumps and a 4,500 sf c-store on the corner", "QT-style fuel stop with a car wash", "convenience store with 12 MPDs") or choose **Gas station / c-store** under Site parameters.
- **How it's laid out:**
  - The fuel canopy runs along the street, between the street and the store, with its pumps in one or two rows down its length. Drive lanes of 24 to 30 ft go all the way round the canopy so a car can pull up to any pump from either end.
  - The store faces the canopy, with its parking along the storefront (about 4 spaces per 1,000 sf of store). A few **air / vacuum** stalls sit beside a drive lane and don't count as parking.
  - The underground **fuel tanks** get a keep-clear pad off to one side, where the tanker can pull straight alongside it on a wide drive from an entrance and drive on out. Nothing is parked over the tanks.
  - Ask for a **car wash** and a tunnel goes beside the store, with its stacking lane wrapping round the back so the waiting cars don't block the pumps. On a lot too small for a full tunnel it may fall back to a shorter in-bay wash.
  - Detention goes in the land left over, usually behind the store.
- **Entrances on a corner:** one driveway on each street, each kept at least 100 ft from the corner where the frontage allows (the layout notes it when a short frontage means one has to be closer). On an inside lot there are one or two driveways on the front street. The canopy goes along the busier street; the side street gets it only when you ask or the front is too short.
- **Odd corner lots:** wedge and triangle lots, a lot with its corner clipped off, and many-sided parcels are laid out square to one of the streets, with the canopy, store, tanks and parking fitted into the part of the lot that holds them.
- **Beside other uses:** ask for a gas station with shops, apartments, homes or any mix ("gas station with 10 pumps on the corner, 40,000 sf of retail behind it and 200 apartments at the back") and the station goes on its **own outparcel** at the front corner (on an inside lot, at one end of the frontage), as these are usually developed. The outparcel is sized for the pumps, store and car wash you asked for (about 1.5 to 2.5 acres), and the station is laid out on it just as it would be alone: canopy along the street, store behind, tank pad, its own driveways. The rest of the plan is laid out behind and beside it, with the site's detention, and a **cross-access drive** joins the station's lanes to the center's drives where a straight one fits (the layout says when none does). The outparcel shows as its own zone, the **Gas station**, in the zone list and on the map. If the rest of the plan doesn't fit beside the station, the layout says so rather than drawing the two on top of each other.
- **Site parameters › Program** for a gas station: **Pumps** (4, 6, 8, 10, 12 or 16 pumps, two fueling positions each), the **Store** size (3,000 to 6,000 sf is typical), parking per 1,000 sf of store, **Canopy along** (Auto, the front street or the side street), **Pump islands** (along the street, so cars pull through side to side, or across it, so cars pull in toward the store), and **Car wash** and **Air / vacuum stalls** on or off. Changing one lays the station out again. In a mixed plan the same settings (without the parking ratio) are in their own **Gas station** group under Site parameters.
- **The numbers:** the layout card leads with the store's square feet and the pumps and fueling positions. **Numbers** has a **Fuel** tile (fueling positions, pumps, canopy sf, store sf, air / vacuum stalls). The canopy has no floor area but counts toward site coverage.
- **In 3D:** tilt the map and the canopy stands up: a column on each pump island up to an 18 ft clearance, a dispenser on each island, the canopy deck with a coloured fascia band round its edge. The deck is see-through, so the pumps still show from above.
- **Selected:** pick the canopy for its pumps and fueling positions, with **Pumps**, **Rows** and **Pump islands** you can change (turning the islands resizes the canopy for them and re-fits the station round it); pick the store or car wash for a **Gas station** field (Convenience store or Car wash). The fuel tanks show they're a keep-clear pad.
- **Pro forma:** choose **Ground-leased to a fuel retailer** (the default: about $22,500 a pump a year, at least $100,000, with the retailer building the store, canopy, pumps, tanks and paving, and the owner delivering a graded pad and the detention) or **Built and run by the owner** (fuel sold per pump, the margin per gallon, inside sales and margin, car wash income and the store's costs, plus the canopy, pumps and tanks in the budget). Every number is editable.
- **Moving the canopy or the store:** drag the canopy along the street, turn it, or move the store, and the rest of the station is laid out again round it a moment later (with **Re-fit the site after each edit** on, or **Re-fit Plan**): the lanes all round the canopy, the store behind it (or the canopy between a moved store and the street), the parking, the tank pad and the driveways. A car wash, tank pad, driveway or pad building you placed stays where you put it and the rest keeps clear of it. If there's no room for the canopy's lanes where you dropped it, or no driveway can reach them, your move stays as it is and a message says why; **Undo** puts it back.
- **Truck stops and travel centers** are laid out as a car fueling station for now, and the layout says so.
- **Shops on a corner lot:** when you ask for retail on a lot with a side street, the planner also tries two corner shapes alongside its usual layouts and keeps whichever scores best: an **L-shaped strip** wrapped round the parking, with its storefronts facing the parking in the crook and an end cap at the street, and a **strip with a pad at the corner** (a drive-thru pad by the intersection, the strip behind). A mixed plan with shops at the front gets the same two on a corner lot: its shops' building row turned into an L with a wing running down to the front street on the far side from the corner (**L-shaped shops on the corner**), and the plan with a drive-thru pad at the corner (**pad at the corner**). The drive aisle that runs up to the L's side wing stops at its walk with its last stalls left off, so cars can turn round there instead of backing out of a dead end. Inside lots get the same layouts as before. Re-fitting an L-shaped layout keeps its L.

**Any other building type:** ask for something the planner has no setting of its own for ("a daycare for 120 children with a playground", "a pickleball club with 8 courts and a clubhouse", "an RV park with 60 sites", "an event venue for 300 with a lawn", "a charter school for 500 students") and, with the AI on, it works out how that kind of place is built instead of drawing a plain box.
- **How the AI sizes it:** it writes a short recipe for the type: its buildings (a type from the building library, or sizes it gives), its outdoor pieces (courts, a playground, a pool, a lawn, a plaza, a patio, a covered pavilion) with how many of each, the parking rule a city would use (spaces a child, a seat, a court, a site, a pad or per 1,000 sf), and the figures worth showing ("120 children", "8 courts"). Anything far outside normal sizes is brought back into range, and the plan says what was.
- **How it's laid out:** with the same planner as everything else, so the drives, parking, entrances, detention, trash and checks work just as they do for shops or apartments. The AI picks the way that suits it:
  - a building facing its parking (a car wash, a dog daycare);
  - a few freestanding buildings (a school's classrooms and gym), each sized from its own share of the floor area when there's room (a two-storey classroom building and a one-storey gym); when there isn't, they're drawn alike and the layout says to resize them by hand;
  - a building with its outdoor pieces right beside it, about 10 ft off, clear of drives and parking, with a walk from the building to them (a daycare and its playground, a club and its courts and pavilion, a venue and its lawn). Layouts with the pieces next to the building and no dead-end parking aisle come first; the land left over is plain open space;
  - rows of sites on drive aisles, the way RV and boat storage is laid out (an RV park: an office at the street, back-in sites in rows both sides of each aisle, a fence and gate).
- **AI-sized:** everything the AI sized carries an amber **AI-sized** mark: on the layout card (with the type's name), in the Selected box when you pick the building, a court or the playground, and on Numbers. These are typical sizes, not a product sheet: check them against the real building.
- **The numbers:** Numbers has a tile for the type with its figures and what was drawn (for example "8 pickleball courts"), and a box with the parking rule worked out ("0.25 a child × 120 children = 30 spaces") and the AI's other assumptions. When the building had to be drawn smaller, the rule counts what the smaller building holds ("about 105 children (the building as drawn)"). Courts and sites carry their own parking, so a smaller clubhouse doesn't shrink the courts' parking. The same rule is in each layout's notes.
- **When it doesn't all fit:** fewer courts or sites are drawn and the layout says so ("Room for 4 of the 8 pickleball courts asked for"); outdoor pieces with no room at all are left off with a note.
- **If the AI can't help:** when the AI doesn't answer, the request is read from your words as before; when its recipe makes no sense, the plan is laid out as a plain building at 3 spaces per 1,000 sf and the assumptions say so.
- **Save it for the team:** see **The building library** below. Ask for "another one like our pickleball club" and the AI reuses the saved recipe.

**Working in stages:** an option is worked on in four steps, shown as a row of four buttons under the options: **1 Site**, **2 Layout**, **3 Pro forma** and **4 Renderings & plans**. The panel shows only the step you're on, with its name, what it's for and a **Confirm** button at the top.
- **Site:** the land: its parcels, street side, side street and outline (**Change Parcels**, **Street Side**, **Side Street**, **Redraw Site**). Check them, then **Confirm Site**. Once confirmed, the site's tools are put away; **Reopen** brings them back.
- **Layout:** starts from the blank, confirmed site. Add buildings and other pieces yourself, press **Start as a Parking Lot** to fill the site (inside its paving setbacks) with parking rows and a drive along the front and up both ends, then drop buildings in: the stalls under a building give way, the way TestFit works. Or describe it under **Build** and have the AI lay it out. Change With AI, Settings, **Ways in** (Add Entrance and Add Roundabout), Numbers and Ask are here too. **Confirm Layout** needs at least one building.
- **Pro forma:** the base pro forma worked out from the layout, open and ready to edit. Change the inputs, then **Confirm Pro Forma**.
- **Renderings & plans:** renderings, the Site Plan PDF and CAD files (Export), and **Make a Plan Set**, which opens the project's Plans tab with this option picked.
- A step after one that isn't confirmed is greyed out ("Confirm the layout first"). Confirmed steps show a tick; the date it was confirmed and by whom show beside the step's name. Change something after confirming it (move a building, change a pro forma input) and that step shows **Changed since it was confirmed**, with an amber mark, and the steps after it wait until it's confirmed again; put it back as it was and the confirmation counts again. Earlier steps can always be looked at.
- The plan can be changed only in the Layout step: in the other steps Edit Layout, Add, the drawing tools, the right-click menu and the shortcuts that change pieces are off (the tool bar's buttons say "Changed in the Layout stage").
- An option opens at its first step that isn't confirmed. Options laid out before steps existed open at Layout, their site counted as confirmed. **+ New Option › Blank site** keeps the site's confirmation, so the new option starts at Layout.

**The plan panel** is a full-height column beside the map buttons. Its header stays put while the sections scroll:
- **Where you are:** **Projects › the project › the option's name**. Click **Projects** for the Projects page or the project's name for its page; click the option's name (it has a dotted underline, and a pencil when you point at it) to rename it. The option's button follows what you type, and a blank name goes back to the one it had. Under it, the address and acres (just the acres when the project is named after the address).
- **Undo** and **Redo** buttons, **Edit Layout** (once there's a layout), **Done** and **×**. Done and × close the plan; it's saved as you go. The map chip's buttons read **Stop Editing**, **Stop Measuring** or **Finish**, so **Done** here is the only Done. A small icon beside Undo says where the plan is: a laptop for **saved on this device**, a cloud with a tick for **synced with the team** (hover it for when), or a red warning when the team copy couldn't be updated; click it to try again (your work is still saved on this device).
- **The options** (Option A, B…) as buttons, each with its floor area or units, parking and coverage when you hover; **+ New Option** opens a short menu (see **Layouts and Options** below): **Blank site**, **Copy of (this option)**, and **Clear (this option) and start over**. While a build is running the options and **+ New Option** are greyed out ("Wait for the build to finish or Cancel it"), so switching can't end the build; **Cancel** on the strip under the header stops it (**Build cancelled**) and the layouts that were already there stay.
- **The four steps** (see **Working in stages** above), then **section chips** for the step you're on, in the same order as its sections, lit for the one you're looking at: **Selected** (when a piece is picked; picking a new piece brings the panel back to the top to show it), **Layouts** (the layouts the last build made), **Settings** (Site parameters), **Build** (Build with AI), **Change** (Change With AI), **Ask** (Ask about this plan), **Numbers**, **Pro forma**, **Site** (parcels, Street Side, Side Street and Redraw Site), **Add** (pieces to add, Start as a Parking Lot and the building library), **Ways in** (Add Entrance, Add Roundabout), **Render**, **Plan set** and **Export**, each in its own step. On a computer the chips wrap onto a second row so every one is visible; on a phone they scroll sideways with a fade at the edge. The chip lit is the section nearest the top, and a chip for a folded section (**Settings**, **Pro forma**) opens it. Selected, Ask, Numbers, Pro forma and Export appear once there's a layout. **Delete This Option** is at the very bottom (it's also in the **+ New Option** menu); see **Layouts and Options** below.
- Under the header, a dark overview of what's on the map: the layout's name, then Phase 1 (units or floor area), parking provided / required, coverage, and value / cost with the yield and spread (in red when negative). Its checks pill (**Passes**, or **2 problems**) opens the list of problems; click one to select that piece on the map. **Compare layouts** jumps to the Layouts. When the layout is out of date (the site, street side or entrances changed after it was laid out), a gold **update** icon appears in the card's top right corner: it updates the layout with your changes, the same as **Re-fit Around My Changes**, and turns while it works. Before anything is laid out it's left out (the step at the top says what to do); while the first build runs it reads **Building**.
- On phones the header is one row: the option's name and the buttons as icons (undo, redo, edit, done ✓, minimize, close), with the options under the overview.

**The plan on the map**
- **Plan key and scale:** while a plan is open, the map's legend card gets a **Plan key** with only what this plan uses (its building uses such as Retail or Multifamily, parking, drives, the pond, lawns, amenities), **Phase 1** and **Phase 2** when there is a Phase 2, the line styles (**Site boundary (planning)**, setback, street side, and **Parcel line (county data)** when the site has several parcels), easements, and **Problem** in red. A scale bar in feet sits at the bottom right of the open part of the map. Both go away when you close the plan. On phones the key starts folded just above the sheet; tap it to open it. Closing the plan puts the legend back as you had it. The map fits the site clear of the key.
- **Labels by zoom:** the most useful labels win when they'd overlap: the selected piece first, then anything with a problem, then buildings (bigger ones first), ponds, parks and garages, amenities, drives, and parking counts last. Zoomed out, a building shows its name only; closer in its name and size; close up, its use too. Parking counts ("38 spaces") appear only when you zoom in close. Names don't break at a hyphen.
- **Lines:** the site boundary is drawn on top of the tree ring with a thin white edge, a little heavier as you zoom in. Zone lines and house lot lines are dotted (house lots from close zoom). Orange is only ever the street side; the line you draw and the turn dot are green.
- **What's selected stands out:** a green outline on a white edge, a brighter fill, and the other buildings fade back a little; its label always shows, whatever the zoom, and the labels around it make way for it (zoomed out it's the name only, like the others, so it doesn't hide the outline). A piece with a problem keeps its red outline (red edge, green inside when it's also selected).
- **Phase 2** buildings (when **Leftover land** is set to mark them) are fainter, with dashed outlines, and their label keeps the use with **PH 2** after the name ("APT BLDG 12 · PH 2").
- **A small chip says what mode you're in,** just above the tool bar at the bottom left: **Editing** with **Stop Editing**, or for a tool with steps (Draw the site, Redraw, Draw area or line, Measure, Edit Shape, Street Side, Side Street, Add Entrance, Add Roundabout, Add drop-off loop, Add or remove parcels) one line on what to click, with **Cancel** and **Finish** (or **Stop Measuring**). While the site re-fits after an edit it says **Re-fitting the site…**. The legend, the tool bar and the scale bar sit on one line along the bottom of the map.

**Closing a plan and getting back to it**
- **Esc never closes a plan.** It steps back one thing at a time: it cancels a tool you've started (Draw, Street Side, Side Street, Add Entrance, Add Roundabout, Add or Remove Parcels), then clears the selected piece, then leaves Edit Layout (the mode pill on the map goes away), then turns off Development mix; after that it does nothing. While you draw a site that has no outline yet, Esc clears the corners so far and you carry on drawing (there's nothing to go back to); **Start Over** does the same. In a text box Esc just leaves the box, and with Layers or a menu open it closes that first. Only **Done** or **×** closes the plan.
- Closing says where the work is: **Saved to (the project)** with **Open project**. A faint dashed outline of the site stays on the map with a **Resume plan** button under the search; click it to reopen the same option. Both go away with your next click on the map or when you change pages.
- A plan started from a property card shows **← (the address)** at the top; Done, × or that link takes you back to the property card as you left it.
- Switching to another page (Projects, Market…) while a plan is open closes it cleanly and shows a banner, **Editing Option A of (the project) · Return to plan**, that reopens it. Back on the Map without the banner, the **Resume plan** button does the same. On the project page, the option you had open last is marked **Last opened**.

**What every plan includes, and what fits**
- A plan always fits the site: buildings, their parking, a drive in from the street, and dumpster enclosures for shops, restaurants and offices. On a small lot where the full planner has no room, a simple layout still parks: a surface apron with one drive in from the street, and the note counts the shortfall the same way the plan's header does ("Parking is 1 space short of 20"). Ask for more than the land holds ("100,000 sq ft on one acre") and you get the most that fits with its parking and setbacks, with a note saying so.
- Small extras asked for with one main use are laid out on the open land beside it: outdoor seating against the building, a playground, a named lawn.
- Odd-shaped lots (a wedge, a curved highway frontage) are laid out square to the street as well as to the lot's long lines.
- A "residential neighbourhood" with shops in front and a community center at the back fills the whole tract: homes take whatever land the shops and apartments leave, the community center (building, pool, parking, playground) sits by the main drive, and the land around it is a park. Land left between homes is open space; only frontage beside the shops is marked as future pad sites.
- Trees line lawn edges and the front yards of homes, with a few groves in big parks.
- Sites up to about 300 acres work; a large layout takes up to a minute while the map stays usable, and the AI may think for up to a minute first.

**The building library**
- About 240 building types: garden, breezeway, corridor, senior, L-shaped, courtyard, podium and tower apartments; townhome rows; duplexes; 24 house plans; restaurants from a coffee drive-thru to a brewpub; shop strips, pharmacies, grocery and big box; medical and office buildings; flex and warehouses; a **Self storage** group (climate-controlled buildings of 1 to 4 stories, drive-up rows 30 or 40 ft deep and 100 to 250 ft long, a leasing office); a **Food trucks** group (pavilions from 1,500 to 6,000 sf with their seats, restrooms, bars); a **Gas station** group (convenience stores of 3,000 to 6,000 sf and car washes); a **Tiny homes** group (1-story homes of 400, 480, 550, 650 and 800 sf: 16 ft wide for a 25 ft lot with a parking pad beside, or 20 ft wide for a 30 ft lot); hotels, churches and more.
- **Tiny homes** count as homes, but as the small homes they are: the plan's home table lists them as **Tiny home** at their own size, the pro forma rents them by their size (about $1,150 a month for 550 sf, editable as **Tiny home rent**) and prices them by their size when sold, and renderings paint them as tiny one-story cottages, not ordinary houses. **Build** lays out tiny-home and manufactured home communities by itself (see Tiny home and manufactured home communities); you can also place them from the library, or swap a home's **Type** to a tiny home. Their floor plans have no garage.
- **From the building library** in the Draw section adds one in the nearest clear spot to the middle of the view; **Type** in the Selected box swaps a placed building for another type in the same spot. Neighbourhood streets mix the house plans home to home.
- **The team library:** **Save to Library** keeps a building for the whole team: pick any building on a plan (one you sized by hand, a library type or an AI-sized one) and press **Save to Library** in the Selected box, or save a whole AI-crafted type, with its recipe, from its box in **Numbers**. Saved types appear first in the library list as a **Team** group, for everyone on the team. Under the list, **Team library** shows each one with its size, who saved it and when, and buttons to **Add** it to the plan, **Build One** (fills in Build with "another one like…", and the AI reuses its recipe), **Rename** it or **Delete** it (with Undo). The team library uses the same team passcode as projects. Until the team library is set up on the site, saved types stay on your device and the panel says so.

**Getting the most out of a site**
- The planner aims for the most **leasable + sellable square footage** that fits the rules: leasable floor area for shops, offices and apartments plus the homes' sellable living area. The rules come first (no failing checks, parking met, the detention met or its shortfall said, any size you asked for kept); among layouts that keep them, the one with more square footage ranks higher, and land left over gets more of the same. Shop, office and medical buildings grow along their rows until the parking is used; warehouses grow deeper; homes keep filling the blocks to the back of the site, leaving the land the detention lake needs. Each option says what was asked for (phase 1) and what the land holds in all.
- Warehouse, flex and storage in a mixed-use plan get a real warehouse with a truck court behind it (they used to come out as homes).
- **Parking garages:** ask for one ("a parking garage behind the offices") and it's built there, four levels by default, carrying most of the parking so the surface lots shrink. On shop and office sites the planner also tries a garage on its own and keeps it only when the extra floor area it allows is worth more than it costs.
- **Housing in general** ("multifamily", "rental housing", "build-to-rent") lays out garden apartments, studio and one-bed apartments, townhomes and duplexes and ranks them by value; name a product (apartments, townhomes…) to get just that one.
- **Every layout shows its money:** value and total cost, profit, yield on cost and the spread over the market cap rate (for homes for sale, cost, profit and margin), from the same land price and assumptions as the Pro forma, so the layout on the map shows exactly what its Pro forma section shows. Change an assumption under Pro forma (a rent, a cost) and every layout follows. The land is counted (the parcels' appraised value, or an estimate an acre for the use when there isn't one: about $150,000–200,000 for homes and apartments, $350,000 for commercial frontage, $650,000 for a gas station corner), so these read lower than an estimate without land would; hover the figures to see the land price used. A loss or a negative spread shows in red. They compare layouts; they aren't an appraisal.
- Dumpsters (trash enclosures) are left off unless the request asks for them or Site parameters turns them on.
- Land left over is landscape or open space; a **future pad site** is marked only on the street frontage where a real pad fits (at least about 0.6 acre), two at most.
- Ponds and amenities you drag stay inside the lot.

**Entrances**
- **Add Entrance** (in the Layout step's **Ways in** section): click the lot line where a driveway should come in. It comes in square to the street, with curb returns at the street, and on to the nearest drive (where the street slants across the layout, it curves round to meet the drive square instead of hitting it at an angle), sliding along the lot line a little if a building, pond or park is in the way; parking it crosses gives way. It never runs through a park or open space: when one is in the way it says so by name ("the driveway would cross Open space") so you can click past it. Clicking a lot line that isn't a street marks it as one. It's named Entrance 2, 3 and so on, gets a stop bar like the main entry, and says so if it's closer than about 150 ft to another entrance.
- **Driveways meet the street square.** Every entrance a layout draws leaves the street at a right angle, with its curb returns the same size on both sides. Where the street slants across the layout's rows, the driveway curves round a few car lengths in so it also meets the drive square, and its end sits inside the drive rather than poking past it. The stop bar and the double yellow centre line follow the curve.
- **How many by itself:** when the request doesn't say, a layout gets the entrances the site calls for:
  - one for an ordinary lot;
  - two for a corner lot (the second on the side street; a small pad keeps one), for more than 30 homes or more than 200 apartments (a second way in for fire access), or for a big parking field or a site over 15 acres;
  - three for a very large site.
  - The layout's notes say why. If a layout can't fit a second way in that fire access needs, it says so, and a layout that has one goes first when it's about as good otherwise. Asking for a number ("one entrance") always wins.
- Ask for them when you build: "two entrances", "a second driveway", "3 curb cuts". The extra ones go on the front or a side street, as far from the others as they can be; options with every entrance come first, and an option that can't fit one says why (mark a side street for more room).
- **A real boulevard at the main entry:** neighbourhoods, apartments, townhomes and mixed plans come in on a divided entry: a lane in and a lane out (18 ft each to start) either side of a planted median (14 ft), 50 ft across, the median running from just past the curb returns to about 45 ft short of the first drive. Where the street slants across the layout, the boulevard leaves the street square and curves round to meet the drive square like any driveway, its median on the straight stretch past the curve; where that leaves no room for the median, it comes straight in instead. The planner lays each layout out first with the old 40 ft entry (a 10 ft median), then tries the full boulevard on it and keeps it unless it costs more than 2% of the homes, units or floor area, parking that was met, detention the site was already short of, or a check. When it doesn't, the narrow entry stays and the notes say what the boulevard would have cost ("would cost 4 homes here"); **Always a boulevard** in Settings puts one in anyway. On a big mixed plan (60 acres or more) the layouts are laid out with the full boulevard from the start: there it costs nothing and saves time. Where the first drive is too near the street for a median (shops along the front, say), the entry stays the 40 ft one rather than a wide strip of paving, and the notes say how far in the first drive is.
- **A monument sign:** the community's sign stands on a stone pad in the median nose by default, 6 ft behind the tip, its face to the street. When the median is too narrow (it needs 14 ft for the 10 ft wall and 2 ft each side), too short, or there's no median, the planner puts a sign at each corner of the entry instead, in the front landscape strip just past the curb returns, slid along the street to a clear spot, and the notes say why. Settings can put them at both corners, one corner or none. The **Monument signs** choice on an entrance stays with it through re-fits, and so does a sign you delete. Two entrances side by side each keep their own signs. On the map the sign is a low stone block with the community's name on it; the PDF sheet shows it with the name beside it.
- **Where entrances may go:** the checks warn (never fail) when two driveways on the streets are closer than about 150 ft, when one is within about 100 ft of the corner where two streets meet, and when a boulevard (or a neighbourhood's main entry) meets its first drive less than about 100 ft in from the street. Adding an entrance by hand says the same; extra entrances the planner adds keep the spacing and stay clear of the corner when there's a spot that does.
- **Entrance settings** (Site parameters › Entrance, for homes, apartments and mixed plans): **Entry** (Boulevard where it fits, Always a boulevard, Driveway with a small median, Plain driveway), **Lane width**, **Median**, **Monument sign** (In the median, Both corners, One corner, None), **Corner clearance**, **Driveway spacing** and **First cross street**. They're typical starting values; the city's or county's rules set the real ones.
- **Make It a Boulevard** in the Selected box (for an entrance that isn't one): it becomes a boulevard in from where its mouth is, square to the street (curving round to meet the drive square where the median still fits past the curve, else straight in), with the median and signs. Homes in its way make way. Where the first drive is too near the street for a median (about 100 ft in is usual), it stays as it is and says so: a wide drive with nothing down its middle gains nothing. A boulevard entrance has a **Monument signs** choice there too.
- In Change With AI: "add another entrance", "add an entrance on the side street", "add an entrance at the open space" (or by any piece), or remove one by name (its median and signs go with it). "Make the main entry a boulevard", "add a boulevard entrance off the side street with monument signs on both sides", "put the signs at both corners" and "turn the main entry 15 degrees" work too. A boulevard entrance asked for where the drives sit too near the street for a median goes in as a plain driveway (with any signs asked for at its corners), and the result says why. An entrance asked for by a piece comes in from the street nearest it; a lot line that isn't a street is used only when no street runs near the piece, and the result says it's now marked as a street. When a park or open space is in the way, the driveway goes along the street past it and the result says how far ("118 ft along the street from where it was asked, clear of Open space"), or it says it couldn't fit one there. An entrance asked for off a street ("off FM 362", "off the side street") that already has a way in nearer than the driveway spacing goes to the nearest spot along that street that keeps the spacing, or the result says there's no room on that street; it's never put on top of another. Words that don't name one of the site's streets ("from the street", "on the east side") put it where it helps most.

**Roundabouts and drop-off loops**
- **Add Roundabout** (in the Layout step's **Ways in** section, beside Add Entrance): small dashed circles mark the places where streets meet and a roundabout has room; click one and a roundabout goes there: a 20 ft lane round a planted island with a tree, 100 ft across where neighbourhood streets meet and 120 ft where a boulevard or a wider street does. The streets running into it are cut back to it so the paving reads as one, the homes in its way make way (with their lots and driveways), the lots beside it are trimmed back and the parking spaces under it give way (the rest of the row stays). Where something can't move (a park, the lake, the community center, a building, or a street running alongside too close to join it) it tries a smaller one, down to 80 ft (90 ft where a boulevard or a wider street meets it), and when the one that fits would take more than two homes a smaller one that keeps two or more of them is used instead (the result says how many the bigger one would have taken); when the junction clicked has no room it goes at the nearest one that has and says so, and otherwise it says what's in the way. It's named Roundabout (then Roundabout 2…), and the result says how many homes made way. **Roundabout** in the Add palette does the same at the junction nearest the middle of the view (or where you drop it).
- **Drop-off loop (circle drive):** a one-way loop with a planted island in front of a clubhouse, community center, school, shop, hotel or office, both ends running into the drive in front of it, driven with the building on your right, with white arrows on it. Pick the building and press **Add Drop-off Loop** in the Selected box, or use **Drop-off** in the Add palette (it uses the building picked or the one nearest; with none near, click the building). A full loop needs about 44 ft between the building's front and the drive (a 10 ft walk, a 20 ft lane and the island). Buildings the app lays out usually stand about 30 ft from the drive in front, with a parking row between: there it puts a compact loop (a 16 ft lane by the building's own walk and a narrow planted island), and the parking row in front gives way. When the front is closer than about 30 ft it tries the building's other sides and says which side it used and why ("On the back facing the 24 ft drive: its front is 28 ft from the street, too close…"). Homes and lawn in its way give way too, and a park beside it gives up the strip it needs when most of the park stays. When there's no room anywhere it says how far the building would have to move back (often only a few feet: move the building, then add the loop). A loop isn't fixed to its building: move the building away and the check says the loop isn't in front of it any more. **Deleting a loop** puts back the parking spaces and the park strip it took.
- **Both move, turn and resize like any piece.** Drag a roundabout and it settles on the nearest junction and joins those streets, and the streets where it was come back as they were; hold Alt while you drag to leave it exactly where you put it. Its corner handles (or the **Across** box in the Selected box) make it bigger or smaller, from 80 to 180 ft across; the **Lane** box (14 to 30 ft) widens or narrows the lane inside the same circle, the island giving. A drop-off loop dragged or turned runs its ends into the drive again (**Edit Shape** bends it like any drive); turned or moved onto its building, its island or another drive, it says what it runs into (a compact loop has little room to turn: a few degrees can put it against its building). Duplicate and Copy don't copy either: add another with Add Roundabout or Add Drop-off Loop.
- **Deleting a roundabout** puts the streets back through the junction exactly as they were, with the parking spaces it took and an entrance's median and signs as they were before it (two roundabouts on one street: either can go first). The homes that made way come back only with a re-fit (Re-fit Plan, or the automatic re-fit after an edit).
- **Re-fits keep them:** a re-fit lays the streets out as before and joins them to the roundabout again (homes laid where it stands make way), and lays the rest round a drop-off loop, with the drive it hangs off where it was and its building in front of it (laid out again in its place, or held where it stands; on a mixed plan holding a shop building can cost a few homes, as moving it by hand does). The automatic re-fit isn't kept when it would leave a loop off its drive or away from its building. **Re-fit Plan** can sometimes choose a new street layout (when it fits more homes) whose streets miss a roundabout: the check then says "Roundabout isn't on a street". Undo, or drag the roundabout onto a junction.
- **The checks:** anything on a roundabout's or a loop's island is a problem ("Plaza 3 overlaps the island of Roundabout", "Street runs into the island of Roundabout"), as is parking on its lane and a street running across its lane without joining it ("Main drive runs across the lane of Roundabout"), and a drive across a loop's lane other than the one it hangs off; a roundabout no street meets ("Roundabout isn't on a street"), a loop that doesn't reach a drive at both ends and a loop whose building has moved away are warnings.
- Roundabouts and loops are drawn on the map, the PDF sheet and the CAD file (the island planted), and their lanes count as paving in Numbers (the island is open space).
- In Change With AI: "add a roundabout at the main intersection", "add a roundabout at the entry", "a roundabout by the community center" (the junction nearest it), "make the main intersection a 120 ft roundabout", "add a circle drive in front of the clubhouse", "make the drop-off loop 140 ft long" and "remove the roundabout". When the junction asked for has no room it goes at the nearest one that has and says how far ("add 2 roundabouts" puts the first where there's room, and when there's room for only one says what's in the way at the next); a piece the plan hasn't got ("near the pool" with no pool) is said, not guessed. The result says where it went ("Added Roundabout (100 ft across) where Main drive, a 24 ft drive and a 28 ft street meet"), or why there's no room.

**Names**
- Give the development a name ("Beacon Hill") and each part its own: the neighbourhood, the retail center ("Beacon Hill Market"), the apartment complex, the business park, the commons. Site parameters › **Names** has a box for the community and one for each part (a plan with one use has one).
- **Suggest Names** asks the AI for two or three sets that fit the place (its creeks, roads, the town, the county); click one to use it, and Undo takes it back. Without the AI it offers names from the street and town names, and says so.
- Where they show: big, faint labels over each part's zone on the map (hidden with Labels in Layers), the monument sign, the layout cards, the PDF sheet's title block and its land use and program tables, and Numbers, which breaks the parts out by name (acres, homes or units, floor area). A part without a name shows what it is (Retail center, Townhomes).
- Names stay with the plan when you build new layouts, use another layout or change the program with Build with AI.
- In Change With AI: "call the neighbourhood Beacon Hill Estates", "name the retail center Beacon Hill Market", "call it Beacon Hill" (the whole development). These name the parts, not a piece; they never re-fit the plan.

**Changing a plan with AI**
- While the AI works, its button shows a spinner and the progress strip at the top of the panel says what's happening (reading the request, laying out, reviewing).
- Once a plan is laid out, **Change With AI** (the **Change** chip at the top of the plan panel) takes changes in plain words, by name: "make Apt Bldg 7 four stories", "turn the clubhouse into a 7,500 sf one", "remove Bldg 3", "swap Bldg 2 and Bldg 5", "move the clubhouse and pool to the back", "add a quick-service restaurant beside Building A". Select a piece first and you can just say "it". **Change With AI** in the Selected box jumps there.
  - It understands places: "move the community center to the middle of the homes", "put the playground at the back", "add a pool in front of the clubhouse". The front is by the street, the back the far end, and "the middle of the homes" (or of the apartments, the shops…) is the middle of where they are.
  - Moving or adding a community center, clubhouse, pool, playground or park among houses: the houses under its new spot (with their lots and driveways) make way, and the rest of the neighbourhood stays as it was. The result says how many homes made way.
  - **Parks and open space are real places:** "add a park beside the community center" tries each side of it in turn (along its own outline, so beside a long lake means beside the water) and keeps the nearest spot clear of the streets (with a few feet of lawn to spare), the other buildings, other parks and the lot line, choosing open lawn over a spot a little closer that would take houses. If the usual size doesn't fit, a smaller park goes there and the result says so; if every side runs into something, nothing is added and the result says there's no room. A pool, playground or courts asked for beside the community center may stand on its lawn rather than take houses. If a park can't go where it was asked, a new name given in the same request is still kept. A park asked for at a spot that crosses a street is moved to the nearest clear spot, or not added (it says so, and suggests a smaller one). The streets are never moved for it. The lots of the homes that stay beside it are cut back to its edge.
  - When a change re-fits the rest of the site and the re-fit would lose homes, units or floor area (or add problems), it isn't taken: your change stays as made, the result says why, and **Re-fit Anyway** takes it if you want it.
  - **Changes across the whole site:** "make all the apartments 3 stories", "nothing over 3 stories", "remove every pool", "make all the retail one story" change every piece of that kind at once, listed on the plan or not, and the result says how many changed ("All 12 apartment buildings → 3 stories").
  - **The homes' stories:** "only 1- and 2-story homes, no 3-story", "all single-story homes" or "no 3-story houses" change the neighbourhood's home mix. Each home's lot is sized for it (a 3-story home sits on a 40 ft lot, a 2-story on 50 ft, a 1-story on 60 ft), so the lots are laid out again along the same streets with the new mix. Expect a few fewer homes on the wider lots; the result says how many there were and how many there are now ("288 → 281 homes"), and the new mix shows in Settings. The shops, lake, commons and pieces you placed stay as they are. On a plan drawn by hand (with no layout to lay the lots out again from), it says so: change the Home mix in Settings and build new layouts instead.
- The AI sees the plan as a picture with every piece's name and size, picks buildings from the library when one becomes something else, and says what it changed. More floors on apartments add units; a clubhouse keeps its pool, a restaurant its patio and drive-thru.
- Usually only the pieces named change: parking gives way under them and grows back where they leave, and everything else stays exactly as it was. When you ask for a re-fit (or tick **Re-fit the rest of the site**), or the change needs new drives (a building out in open land, several removed), the parking and lots are laid out again around what changed, along the same streets, keeping about the same amount of building. Changed pieces are pinned, so later re-fits keep them.
- **A new street pattern is a new layout.** "Make the streets curvy like a subdivision" or "give it loops and cul-de-sacs" isn't an edit: the result points to **New Layouts** (with Settings › Neighbourhood streets › Like a subdivision), since Change With AI keeps the streets the plan has. A sentence about one street or a piece by one ("add a pool next to the winding street") is still an edit.
- **Change With AI never moves the streets.** If laying the rest out again round the change leaves a problem or short parking, the street arrangement is kept anyway, the result says what's left, and a **New Layouts** button lays the whole site out afresh with new streets from the program (pieces you placed by hand, this change included, aren't carried over; Undo brings the plan back).
- If it can't tell which piece you mean it asks, and when it finds nothing on the plan to change it says so plainly rather than describing a change it didn't make. Without the AI, simple changes in the same words still work (stories, remove, swap, turn, rename, "make it a …", "no 3-story homes").
- **You see what it did:** the pieces it changed flash on the map, and anything it removed leaves a dashed outline that fades over a few seconds. Under the box a short checklist says what changed (**Apt Bldg 7 → 4 stories · Apt Bldg 13 removed**), with anything it couldn't do, **Undo this change** (Cmd+Z works too) and, when the plan is now short of parking, **Re-fit around my changes** to lay the parking out again around them. Edit Layout stays as it was; nothing is switched on for you.

**The tool bar**
While a plan is open, a small tool bar sits at the bottom left of the map (on phones at the top left, with **⋯** for the rest). Hover a button for its name and shortcut; a greyed-out button says why it can't be used yet ("Select a piece first", "Turn on Edit to draw"). Shortcuts don't fire while you type in a box.
- **Select** (V) looks and selects without moving anything; **Edit** (E) turns editing on, the same as **Edit Layout**.
- **Add** (A, the plus): the same grouped tiles as the panel's Add section (Buildings, Paving, Water & landscape, Amenities, Site). Click one and it's placed in a clear spot near the middle of the view, with editing turned on. Or **drag a tile onto the map** (from here or from the panel's Add section): its outline follows the pointer at its real size and turns red off the site, and it lands where you let go. Esc, or letting go off the map, cancels.
- **Draw area** and **Draw line**: see Drawing by hand below.
- **Measure** (M) a distance: click points; each side and the running total show in feet. **Measure area** (Shift+M): click round a shape for its square feet and acres. Enter or a double-click finishes, Backspace removes the last point, Esc stops. Measurements aren't saved.
- **Fit plan** (Shift+F) brings the whole site into view; **Fit selection** (F) zooms to what's selected. The map never moves by itself otherwise.
- **Grid** (G): a 10 ft grid (5 ft in Layers) square to the lot, shown when zoomed in close. Pieces you move and points you draw snap to it. **Snap to edges** in Layers turns off lining up with nearby edges and square turns; hold Alt to move freely either way.
- **Development mix** (U, the pie chart): shows how the site is split between uses. Each use's buildings are filled in their own colour (retail one colour, apartments another, amenity buildings such as a clubhouse or community center their own), and the parking, drives, lawns and ponds are greyed out so the uses stand out. A small card above the legend lists each use with its colour, the **acres its buildings stand on** and **what share of the site** that is, with the number of buildings and their floor area under it; **All buildings** totals them. Only the buildings' footprints count, not their parking, drives, landscape or ponds (a parking garage counts as parking). Phase 2 buildings are included and drawn lighter, with their share on a line of their own. On a mixed-use plan, **Zones** adds the land given to each use with its parking included, and each zone is outlined in its colour on the map. Point at a use in the card (or click it to keep it) to light up only that use. It stays on while you edit, so the numbers change as you move things; click it again, the card's ×, or Esc (after everything else) turns it off. Where the Plan key shows two uses in the same colour (retail and restaurants), the mix gives one its own colour so they can be told apart. On phones the card starts folded to the total; tap its title to open it.
- **Layers**: show or hide, and lock, each part of the plan: Buildings, Parking & drives, Stormwater, Landscape & amenities, Trash, Trees, Labels, the setback line, parcel lines and easements. A hidden or locked part can't be clicked or moved by accident, but the numbers and checks still count it, and exports always include everything. The Trees eye only hides them on the map; **Trees** under Draw takes them off the plan and the PDF. Layers, the grid and snapping are saved with the option.
  - **Items** (the second tab at the top of Layers) lists every piece of the plan, the way a design program's layers panel does: grouped under the same headings (buildings first), each heading with its count, a chevron to fold it, and its own show/hide and lock buttons. Each row has the piece's colour from the map, its name and one short line on what it is ("Retail · 2 floors · 13,380 sf", "28 spaces", "Dry basin · 0.60 ac"). Click a row to select that piece (the map moves to it if it's out of view); Shift-click adds it to the selection. Double-click a name, click the pencil, or press F2 to rename it: Enter or clicking away keeps the name, Esc cancels, an empty name puts the usual label back, and Undo takes a rename back. The list follows what you do on the map: pieces you select there are highlighted (and scrolled into view), and while Items is open, clicking the map doesn't close it. Hover a row to outline its piece on the map. Arrow keys move through the list, Enter selects (Enter again renames), and ← and → fold and unfold a heading. Layers opens on the tab you used last.

**Editing a plan**
- A plan opens for looking: clicking a piece shows its details and dragging pans the map, so nothing moves by accident. **Edit Layout** at the top of the plan panel turns editing on (**Stop Editing** turns it off); adding a piece from Draw turns it on too.
- While editing, drag a piece to move it, its white corner squares to resize it, a white bar on one of its sides to move **just that side** (the opposite side stays put), and the green dot to turn it. Only that piece changes. On touch screens the handles are bigger, can be grabbed from a finger's width away, and the dot sits further out.
- **Drag a face, on any building:** a selected building shows a white bar in the middle of each side long enough to grab, a rectangle or a custom outline alike (one you drew, or reshaped with Edit Shape into an L). Drag the bar and that face moves straight out or in; the sides either side keep their direction, so square corners stay square and a slanted side stays slanted. It stops before the outline would cross itself or a wing would get narrower than 8 ft. While you drag, a label along the face shows its length and how far it has moved ("90 ft face · 12 ft out"). Floor area, parking and the numbers follow, it's one step of Undo, and the rest of the site re-fits afterwards as for any other edit.
- **Hold Shift** while dragging a face or a point for whole feet, and, on a point, square or 45° corners where it nearly is one.
- **Turning:** the green dot turns freely, to the degree. Within 2° of square to the street, a site edge or another building's face it settles there exactly, with a thin guide line and the angle on the map ("105° · square to the street"); hold Alt (or turn edge snapping off) to turn right past them. **Hold Shift** to turn in 15° steps (0, 15, 30, 45, 60, 75, 90 …). The **Turn** field takes any number of degrees, 52.5 included; its arrows step 1°, or 15° with Shift. Custom outlines, parking and pads turn the same way.
- **Handles never get in the way:** a bar is caught only along the bar itself, and a corner or dot you're merely near gives way to another piece you click on, so you can always pick the building next to the selected parking. It lines up with the edges of the pieces near it and with the grid when it's on, and stays out of the setbacks (hold Alt to move it freely); parking under a building you move gives way. Anything that still clashes shows in red.
- **Right-click** a piece (long-press on a touch screen, or the menu key / Shift+F10 for what's selected) for Duplicate, Copy, Delete, Turn 90° (Turn 15° on an entrance), Edit shape, Add point here, Pin or Let it move, Fit selection and Change with AI (and, on parking, **Stall direction** and **Face the building**). Right-click several selected pieces for what works on all of them. Right-click open ground for **Add here** (the piece lands where you clicked), Measure from here, Fit plan and Paste. ⌘C / Ctrl+C copies and ⌘V / Ctrl+V pastes in the middle of the view.
- **The rest of the site re-fits after each edit:** when you finish moving, resizing, turning, adding, deleting or pasting a piece (or typing a size), the parking, drives and the other pieces the engine placed are laid out again around what you placed, a moment later. One **Undo** takes back both your edit and the re-fit. A re-fit keeps the streets where they are (entrances you added are joined to them, not built around); if the only way round your change was to lay the whole site out afresh with new streets, the message says so. If re-fitting would lose floor area or units, or add problems, your edit is kept as it is and the message offers **Re-fit anyway**. In a mixed plan each zone keeps what it has when the rest re-fits (the shops their floor area, the apartments their units), after an automatic re-fit and after **Re-fit Plan** alike. Turn it off with **Re-fit the site after each edit** in Site parameters.
- A building you drag settles in the nearest clear spot to where you let go: off the drives (a walk's width away), clear of the other buildings, ponds and amenities, inside the setbacks. If there's no clear spot nearby it stays where you let go and says so; hold Alt (Option) to place it anyway. A park, named lawn or open space does the same (it keeps a few feet off the streets and clear of the buildings and other parks, inside the lot; one already against a street can slide along it, and what stands on it, like a food truck park's pavilion, isn't in its way), and the homes under where it lands make way, with their lots and driveways; parking under it gives way and doesn't grow back beneath it later.
- What belongs to a building moves with it: a clubhouse takes its pool deck and pool, a restaurant its patio and drive-thru lane, a home its driveway. Arrow-key nudges carry them too.
- **Turn 90°** (or R; Shift+R turns the other way) works like a drag, for custom outlines too (they turn about their middle): the building stays clear of the other buildings and drives (moving to the nearest clear spot if it needs to), parking under its new footprint gives way and grows back where it was. If no clear spot is near, it says so, with Undo.
- **Any piece turns:** curved ponds, lawns, drawn outlines, custom buildings, drives you've bent, medians and entrances all turn with **Turn 90°**, R, the **Turn** box and the green dot (curved pieces have one too, above their middle). They turn about their middle and keep their shape and size. A turned pond moves to the nearest spot inside the lot and clear of the drives and buildings (homes make way; the drives don't); a long pond laid along the lot line or between drives may have none, and then Change With AI leaves it as it was and says so (a turn in the editor shows it in red, with Undo). "Turn the pond" works on a pond with no name when it's the only one.
- **Roundabouts and drop-off loops turn, move and resize too:** a roundabout joins the streets again where it lands (on the nearest junction; Alt-drag leaves it where you put it), and a loop's ends run into the drive again (see Roundabouts and drop-off loops above).
- **Turning an entrance:** it turns 15° a press (**Turn 15°**), about its middle: the drive swings and its mouth slides along the street, staying on the lot line and square to it, never more than 60° off square, and never so far its drive runs outside the lot (it says so when it stops short). Its median is drawn again along it and its signs follow the mouth. Turned, it may cross a parking row or a building until the site re-fits round it.
- **Delete**, **Undo** and **Redo** say what they did in a short message with the way back: **Deleted Building D · Undo**, **Undone · Redo**. Undo also covers renaming the option, Pro forma changes and adding or removing parcels.
- **Several pieces at once:** Shift-click adds a piece to the selection or takes it out, and Shift-drag on the map draws a box that selects every piece inside it (with a mouse or trackpad). Drag any of them, nudge with the arrow keys, **Duplicate**, **Delete**, or **Pin** / **Let them move**, and they act together as one step of Undo, with what belongs to each (a pool deck, a patio) coming along. The Selected box shows how many are selected, what kinds, and the ground they cover.
- **Duplicate** (⌘D / Ctrl+D, or the button in the Selected box) copies the selected pieces, with what belongs to them, beside the originals in the nearest clear spot, and selects the copy. It works for curved drives and the entry too (a copied entry becomes a plain drive, so the plan keeps one main entrance).
- Clicking a button in the panel doesn't stop the keys working: Delete, the arrow keys and R act on the selected piece straight after.
- A selected piece shows its width and depth along its edges when you zoom in close.
- Garages, outdoor seating and trash enclosures can be clicked and selected like everything else.
- Parking a building cut grows back when the building moves off it again, so trying a spot and moving back leaves the plan as it was. A parking row you delete or resize yourself stays as you left it.
- **Edit Shape** in the Selected box (buildings, parking, lawns, ponds, plazas, courts, pools, patios, garages) turns the outline into points: drag a point, drag the white bar on an edge to move that whole face out or in (its neighbours keep their direction, as above), drag a small blue + to add a point there, double-click a point to remove it. Points line up square with their neighbours unless Alt is held; Shift gives whole feet and square corners. Each drag is one Undo step.
- **Double-click a building's outline** while editing to add a point right there: the building turns into points (one Undo step) and Edit Shape comes on, so you can drag the new point straight away.
- **Cmd+Z / Ctrl+Z** undoes and **Shift+Cmd+Z** redoes, whatever button was clicked last; the **Undo** and **Redo** buttons in the plan's header do the same.
- **Esc** steps back: it cancels a tool, then clears the selection, then turns Edit Layout off. It never closes the plan.
- **Change what a building is, any time:** the Selected box opens with **Use** (Retail, Restaurant, Office, Medical office, Hotel, Industrial, Multifamily and the rest), **Floors** and **Type** (a building from the library). Right-click a building, or several selected, for **Change use ›**. The parking needed follows the new use and the rest of the site re-fits; one Undo takes it back. **Building shape** reads **Custom outline** for a building you've reshaped or drawn; picking Bar, L-shaped, Courtyard or Tower turns it back into that shape at the same size.
- **Re-fit Plan** in the Selected box lays out everything else again around the pieces you placed (marked 📌; **Let it move** releases one), whether or not re-fitting after each edit is on, and Undo takes it back. A pond you enlarge counts toward the detention.
- Curved pieces (ponds, the pool, lawns, curved drives) resize from their corner handles; curved outlines turn from the green dot too. Ponds, pools, lawns, plazas and courts can switch between **natural curves**, a **rounded rectangle** and a **rectangle**, keeping their area.

**Parking: any shape, either way, facing the building**
- **A custom parking outline:** select a parking piece and press **Edit Shape**. Its outline becomes points like a building's: add points with the blue +, drag points and faces, double-click a point to remove it. The stalls fill the new shape as you drag: whole 9 × 18 ft stalls (or your own sizes from Site parameters) in rows facing an aisle, double-loaded where there's room for a row each side of the aisle, a single row along an edge where there's room only for one, and never a stall cut by the outline or one without its aisle. The count feeds everything: the Selected box, the map, Numbers, the checks, the pro forma, the PDF sheet, the CAD file, the 3D and rendering brief and the Waller guardrails. Accessible stalls stay in the rows nearest the building. Parking rows the layout engine made work the same way (a custom outline drops their planted islands).
- **Stall Direction** (Selected box, or right-click) turns the stalls 90° inside the same outline, so the rows run the other way. Press it again to turn them back.
- **Face the Building** turns the stalls to face the nearest building face: the cars pull in toward it, the aisle runs along it, and the parking turns the least it needs to line up with that face (a rectangle keeps its size; a custom outline turns about its middle). A row of stalls sits on the building's side. If the parking was over the building, closer than a walk, or far off, it moves to sit a walk's width (8 ft) off the face. On an L-shaped building it faces the inner face it sits beside. The message says how many spaces it holds now, with Undo.
- The **Turn** field and the green dot turn parking like any other piece; Shift gives 15° steps.

**Drawing by hand**
- Add a **Building, Restaurant pad (drive-thru)**, Parking, **Parking garage**, Drive, Truck court, **Roundabout, Drop-off loop**, Detention, Landscape, **Playground, Courts, Pool, Outdoor seating**, Trash enclosure or **Monument sign** piece. A restaurant pad comes with its patio and drive-thru lane; a parking garage counts about one space per 330 sq ft on each level (set the levels in the Selected box). An enclosure you place stays where you put it, and the plan adds any others the rules still call for. A new piece lands in the nearest clear spot to the middle of the open map (off the buildings, drives, ponds and amenities and inside the setbacks, on open ground before parking), turned like the buildings near it, and is selected so the Selected box is at the top. When the site is full it says so (a building may then sit over a drive, in red, until you move it). Drag a piece to move it, its white corners or side bars to resize it, and the green dot to turn it (freely, settling square to the lot's lines and the buildings within 2°; hold Shift for 15° steps).
- Curved pieces from Build with AI (ponds, the pool, lawns) move as a whole: drag them or nudge them with the arrow keys, resize them from their corners, or turn them with the green dot.
- **Bend a drive or an entrance:** select a drive, a drive aisle or an entrance and press **Edit Shape** (or right-click › **Bend it**). It turns into a line of points along its middle:
  - drag a point, or drag the small blue + on a stretch to add a bend there, and double-click a point to remove it;
  - double-clicking a drive while editing adds a bend right where you clicked;
  - **Width** and **Curve** in the Selected box set how wide it is and how round its bends are.
  - An entrance shows its bend points and the blue + markers as soon as it's selected, without Edit Shape. It keeps its mouth on the street: drag its first point, or drag the whole entrance, and the mouth slides along the lot line (hold Alt to move it freely). It comes in square to the street, with the curb flaring out each side (**Mouth flare** sets how wide). Its median and monument signs move with it, and after a bend the median is drawn again along it.
  - When the site re-fits, a main entrance you've bent stays the way in: the drives are laid out to meet it, and no second entrance is added.
- **The paving is one surface:** drives, aisles, parking and entrances pour together with one curb round the outside, and the curb is rounded where a row ends and where an aisle meets a drive. Curves are smooth at any zoom.
- **A parking row with no aisle** (moved away from the one it faced, or left facing a lawn) shows a warning: “Parking 9 has no aisle to drive in from”.
- **Draw area** (in the tool bar) draws a **Building outline** (any shape, corner by corner, with the plan's number of floors), a **Landscape** area, **Plaza**, **Pond** or **Easement / no-build area** point by point; **Draw line** draws a **Drive** (26 ft) or a **Walk / trail** (6 ft). Click to place each point; click the first point (or press Enter) to finish, Backspace removes the last point, Esc stops. A shape with fewer than three points, one that crosses itself, or one outside the lot is refused with a message (an easement may run along the lot line). What you draw is pinned and selected.
- **Easements and no-build areas** are hatched in red with a dashed edge. Anything built or paved on one (a building, garage, parking, drive, truck court, pond or pool) is flagged as a problem ("Building A is on Easement 1"). **Build Layouts** and **Re-fit Plan** lay everything out around them, and a piece you place on one yourself stays where you put it, flagged. They aren't counted as building, paving or open space. They appear in the Plan key ("drawn, not surveyed"), on the PDF sheet (hatched, with a legend line and a row in the land use table) and in CAD on their own EASEMENT layer. You draw them from what you know; the app doesn't look up recorded easements.
- Keyboard: arrow keys nudge (Shift for 10 ft), R turns 90° (an entrance 15°; Shift+R the other way; Shift while turning with the dot or the Turn field's arrows: 15° steps), ⌘D / Ctrl+D duplicates, ⌘C / ⌘V copy and paste, A opens Add, Delete removes, ⌘Z / Ctrl+Z undoes. They work with a panel button focused, not only after a click on the map; in a text box the keys stay the box's.
- **Dragging a piece off the site** puts it back at the last clear spot on the site (undo still works) and says **That's outside the site**; hold Alt to place it anyway. A piece dropped on a drive or another building still gets the "No clear spot there" message.
- **A Parking garage next to a Parking lot** goes beside it, leaving the lot as it is. If the site has no room for both, the lot gives way and the message says so: **Replaced Parking with Parking garage**, with **Undo**.
- Parking counts its spaces as you size it, using the stall and aisle sizes under Site parameters (9 × 18 ft spaces on 24 ft aisles to start); parking you add by hand uses them too. Each pair of accessible stalls shares an aisle the width of a stall, so it costs one space; the count shown already allows for that.
- Type exact sizes, the turn, the number of floors, the building's use (which sets its parking and colour) and its name in the **Selected** box. Townhomes and duplexes also take the number of homes.
- The site tools are in the **Site** section: **Add or Remove Parcels**, **Street Side**, **Side Street**, **Add Entrance**, **Add Roundabout** and **Redraw Site**. While one is on, the pill on the map says what to click, with **Cancel** (or **Stop Editing**); Esc cancels it too.
- **Street Side:** the orange line marks the side along the street. The app finds it from the map's roads; when it can't, it says the line is a guess. Click **Street Side**, then the lot line along the street, to set it; the lot line under the pointer lights up orange before you click. Front setbacks and drives follow it.
- **Side Street:** on a corner lot, a second street along another lot line (a thinner orange line). The app finds it from the map's roads, and only where a road actually runs along that lot line, close to it, for most of its length: driveways, parking lot lanes and alleys don't count, a road that only passes a corner or runs off at an angle doesn't count, and short jogs in the lot line (around an out-parcel, say) are never streets. When two roads border the site, the front goes to the bigger one (a highway or farm-to-market road over a local street). Click **Side Street**, then a lot line, to add or remove one (the line under the pointer lights up); it stays on for the next one until you press **Stop Editing** on the pill. Clicking the street side itself moves the front to the other street, so a wrong street side can be taken off too (a site needs one street, so the last one stays). Plans whose side streets were found before October 15 are checked against the map again once when opened; if you had set them by hand, a note says how many sides the map supports and they're left for you to change. Buildings keep the street setback from it, and a pull-through drive can reach it.
- **Add or Remove Parcels:** click a parcel next to the site to add it (up to 20), or one in the site to take it out; the outline is merged again and the pieces stay where they are.
- **Redraw Site** replaces the outline (for part of a parcel, or a site that doesn't follow parcel lines). An outline that crosses itself (corners clicked out of order) or has almost no area (corners in a line) isn't kept: the app says what's wrong and the drawing stays open, so Backspace can take points back.
- **Only the parcels that make the site are counted.** The Site heading says how many there are and the acres of the site itself ("Site · 2 parcels · 8.90 ac"). Gaps between parcels are filled in as site, and a mapped road between them is a no-build **Road between parcels**, also when you add or take out parcels with **Change Parcels**. A parcel more than 200 ft from the rest, or with no outline from the county, is left out: its chip is greyed (hover: "Not part of the site: more than 200 ft from the rest" or "no outline"), and a note in the section says so, the same one the Start card gave.
- Building heights show in 3D when you tilt the map.

**Numbers and checks**
- Site area, building floor area (and about how many apartments), **Leasable + sellable** (the square feet to lease plus the homes' square feet to sell: what the layouts are ranked by; each layout card shows it too, and **Sort › Leasable + sellable sf** orders them by it), coverage, FAR, parking provided against what's needed (with the accessible stalls), detention (with the pond depth it assumes), paved area and open space.
- Red outlines and a list flag a building inside a setback, anything outside the lot, buildings overlapping other pieces, a park, named lawn or open space under a drive or a building ("Park overlaps Street"), a parking shortfall, and coverage, FAR or height over the zoning limits when Regrid has them. A plain unnamed lawn under paving, a boulevard's median and a pavilion standing in a park aren't problems. A monument sign standing on paving, a building or a lot is (one wholly in a median isn't), and a median that sticks out of its drive is a warning. The entrance warnings (spacing, the corner, the first cross street) are warnings only. Anything standing on a roundabout's or a drop-off loop's island, parking on its lane, or a street across a roundabout's lane is a problem; a roundabout no street meets, a loop that doesn't reach a drive at both ends and a loop whose building has moved away are warnings.
- **Deal summaries**, the way TestFit tabulates a scheme: acreage, FAR, building coverage, impervious coverage, units, units per acre, beds, net rentable and gross square feet and the parking ratio, plus the unit mix (units, share and net square feet of each type) or the home mix.
- **Site parameters** (changing any of them lays out new layouts, keeping the same arrangement on the map; a layout you've changed is never replaced without asking, see "New settings and your edits" below):
  - **Program:** the use, parking per unit, per home or per 1,000 sf, floors, building depth and efficiency (net ÷ gross).
  - **Stormwater:** detention pond, retention (wet) pond, underground vaults or none; the storage per acre (the rule sheet's detention rate when the plan has one, unless typed here) and the pond depth; and an optional pond size as a share of the site. A line underneath says how much storage this plan needs for its developed acres, how big the pond drawn is, and how much would go underground.
  - **Trash:** enclosures placed by the rules or none, and how many apartments or how many square feet of commercial space each one serves (each only when the plan has apartments, or shops and the like; a plan of homes only has no Trash settings, since homes roll carts to the curb).
  - **Unit mix** for apartments: each unit type's size, share and beds (studio, 1, 2 and 3 bedroom to start); add or remove types. The mix sets the average unit, the unit count and the beds.
  - **Setbacks:** building setbacks front, side and rear, and the landscape buffer front, side and rear, each set turned on or off.
  - **Parking:** stall width and depth and the drive aisle width (not for a plan of single-family homes, which park in their driveways).
  - **Buildings:** only what the plan's uses read, its zones included: the building shape (bars, L-shaped, courtyard U, or towers on a podium for 4 floors and up) for shops, offices and apartments; apartment depth, longest building and the space between buildings for apartments; **Neighbourhood streets** (like a subdivision, parallel and gently curving, or straight) and **Street names** (on or off, with a theme) for single-family homes. A gas station, self storage, a food truck park, a warehouse or restaurants alone are drawn their own way, so the section isn't shown for them.
  - **Mixed sites** (a plan of 15 acres or more with homes or apartments among two or more uses, and no garage, warehouse, self storage or food trucks: where pods can be laid out; a gas station doesn't count, as it goes on its own outparcel): **Layout style**: pods and bands (best first), pods round a loop road, or bands from the street back (see Mixed sites in pods).
  - **Street rules:** the place's rule sheet (see Street rules for the place): read it, check and confirm its values, or go back to typical values. On a plan without homes it shows the detention rate only.
  - **Fill the whole site:** on by default (see Build with AI).
  - **Look:** trees on or off; ponds and pools as rectangles (the default), rounded rectangles or natural curves; parking rows ending square (the default) or in rounded islands; drive corners curved or square.
  - Defaults: 25 ft front, 10 ft sides, 20 ft rear, 10 ft buffer, 9 × 18 ft stalls on 24 ft aisles, and a detention pond holding 0.55 acre-feet per developed acre, 5 ft deep, on sites of an acre or more.

**Waller guardrails**

Sites in or near the City of Waller are planned under the Finishes Solutions site-planning standard for Waller (the AI Site-Planning Guardrails, Waller / Harris County basis, version 1.0). It applies when the site is inside Waller's city limits, or within half a mile of them, which is treated as Waller's extraterritorial jurisdiction (ETJ). The half-mile reach comes from the Texas rule for a city of Waller's size; no ETJ map was checked, so the ETJ is marked **Unverified**. Every other site is planned exactly as before.

- **How it knows:** the project's **Jurisdiction & site** lookup (city limits from the Census) decides it, and the project page says when the Waller standard applies. **Site parameters** has **Waller guardrails: Auto / On / Off**: Auto follows the lookup; On tries the standard on any site; Off turns it off when the lookup is wrong.
- **The label:** a Waller plan carries the concept label ("AI-assisted conceptual site plan · for feasibility and planning only · not for permit or construction") on the drawing and title block of the PDF, and on the exports. The plan panel doesn't show it; when the layout on the map was made for a different scenario, a line at the top of the panel says so, with **Lay out B** (or the scenario picked) to redo it.
- **Three scenarios:** **A, maximum code envelope** (the published minimums with no extra margin, detention at the rate on file for the outfall); **B, Finishes base case** (the default, drawn on the map: 5% more than each minimum dimension, 3–5% more parking than the minimum count, maximums held 5% below the cap, and at least 1.00 acre-foot of detention per developed acre); and **C, conservative** (B plus reserves for what isn't known yet: a right-of-way strip along the street, land held back for flooding when the flood map is missing or shows a floodplain, a buffer round each easement, and 10% more detention while the outfall is unknown). The other two are laid out in the background and shown beside it in Numbers; **Lay out A** or **Lay out C** puts one on the map in one click. Each column is the best layout found that passes that scenario's rules, so A is never less than B and B never less than C. The yield is shown as a range across them, never as one exact number, while survey, title, flood, outfall, utilities and access are unresolved.
- **The Waller numbers:** single-family lots at least 57 × 114 ft (59 × 118 in the base case), so at least 6,498 sf (6,962 sf in the base case) even where a curve makes a lot a wedge, in a mix's neighbourhood as well as on a homes-only plan, with homes 26.25 ft back from the street and 5.25 ft from the side lot lines, along curved subdivision streets as well as parallel ones (where a place's street rules ask for more, the stricter wins); apartments parked at 2 spaces a unit (2.1 in the base case: 420 for 200 units); shops, offices, industrial, storage and mixed use keep the app's parking ratios, marked **Unverified** until the City's ratio is confirmed, with the extra 3–5% on top; drives and parking aisles 26 ft wide as fire lanes, with every building within 150 ft of one.
- **Detention as a real pond:** acre-feet are a volume, so the pond's footprint is worked out from it: about 4 ft deep, 4:1 side slopes, a foot of freeboard, a 15 ft maintenance strip round the top and room for the outfall structure and overflow. Pond land is never counted as buildable. **Site parameters** sets the **Outfall** (unknown, roadside ditch, public underground storm sewer or channel): 1.00 acre-foot per acre unless it's a public storm sewer you've confirmed (0.75). It also sets the owner's margin (0–5%), the right-of-way reserve, the storage depth and the side slopes. A higher storage figure from a drainage study always wins.
- **Deeper ponds, shown as options:** the pond's depth is an engineering assumption, not a rule, so **Build Layouts** (and **New Layouts From These Settings**) also lays the site out with a **6 ft** and an **8 ft** deep pond: the same storage (the 1.00 acre-foot per acre is never lowered) in less land, given back to homes and shops. Each is a layout of its own, named "· 6 ft deep pond" or "· 8 ft deep pond", marked **6 ft pond · assumed** on its card, and its notes say what it assumes and what it buys against the usual 4 ft one ("Assumes an 8 ft deep pond …: confirm with your civil engineer. A dry pond needs an outfall deep enough to drain it; a wet-bottom pond holds the storage above its normal pool. Against the 4 ft layout: 6.8 → 4.0 acres of pond for the same 20.6 acre-feet, +12 homes, +22,150 sq ft leasable + sellable"). The usual depth stays the first layout. Using a deeper-pond layout sets that depth under Site parameters, and Numbers says it's assumed and to confirm it. A deeper pond that gains nothing on a site isn't offered (the first layout's notes say so). Off the Waller guardrails it works the same from the plan's own pond depth (5 ft unless set). Only where the pond is a real share of the site (4% or more) and on sites under 60 acres.
- **Classification tags:** every material number in Numbers carries a small tag: **Verified** (a current ordinance or written direction), **Owner guardrail** (the Finishes margin), **Eng. assumption** (to be confirmed by the engineer) or **Unverified** (unknown or awaiting confirmation). Hover a tag for its source.
- **Land-use reconciliation:** Numbers has the table the standard asks for, from the gross site through right-of-way, flood reserve, detention, easements, buffers and setbacks, roads and parking to net usable land, then the lots or units, building square feet, impervious cover, and the detention volume and footprint, each with its basis and tag. It always adds back up to the gross site.
- **Unresolved risks:** a list of what isn't known and why it matters: survey and title (always), flood data missing or a mapped floodplain (missing flood data is a stop condition), an unknown outfall, utility capacity not documented, who controls the roads (TxDOT, the county or the City), a fire-access check failing, the ETJ, and parking ratios still to be confirmed.
- **Required plan content:** the standard's 18-item checklist, each item marked shown, unresolved or not applicable; none is left out.
- **What the AI may and may not say:** with the guardrails on, the AI gets the standard's instruction and the plan's values, and is asked to classify its assumptions. Its answers and review notes on a Waller plan never call the plan permit-ready, construction-ready, code-compliant, approved, engineered, a guaranteed yield, or say utility capacity is confirmed: any such phrase is rewritten. The layout card's check reads **Passes the planning checks**, not just "Passes".
- **Exports:** the PDF adds a second sheet (SP-1A) with the label, the land-use table, the scenarios, the risks and the checklist; **Land Use (CSV)** under Export and a **Waller land use** sheet in the Pro Forma (Excel) carry the same; Copy Summary and the CAD file carry the label.
- The standard itself is kept with the app as a reference copy.

**Layouts and Options**
- **Options** (Option A, B…) are the plans you keep in a project. **Layouts** (Layout 1, 2, 3…) are what a build draws for the option you're in. Layouts are numbered, never lettered, so they can't be mixed up with options.
- The layouts a build makes are listed in the **Layouts** section right under the overview, headed **Layouts · 3** with the sort and **More layouts…**. The one on the map is marked **On map**, or **On map · edited** once you've changed it by hand or with Change With AI. Two layouts with the same arrangement are told apart by the number that differs between them ("· 114 spaces").
- **Each layout is a card to compare:** a small drawing of it, its name, and the same four figures on every card: **Phase 1** units or floor area (with "as asked", or "Fits 160 of 200 units"), **Parking** provided / required with the ratio, **Value / cost** with the profit, and **Yield on cost** with the spread. Then whether it passes the checks (**Passes**, **Parking short 79** or **2 problems**, the same as the overview; on the **On map** card it's the plan as it is on the map now, so a setting change shows there too), the AI's score when it reviewed them, and badges across the set: **Recommended**, **Most floor area** (or **Most units**) and **Best yield**.
- **Recommended** is the AI's first choice when it reviewed the layouts, otherwise the planner's first layout that passes every check, with one line saying why. Every other card says what it has compared with it, e.g. **vs Recommended: +7 spaces · −$60k cost**. Layouts with exactly the same numbers fold into one card (**2 similar layouts · show**).
- **The planner's notes by how much they matter:** shortfalls and things that didn't fit in red, access and parking warnings in amber, the rest (stormwater, phases) behind **Details**. Notes every layout shares are said once, above the cards.
- **When none passes,** a banner at the top says so and why, for example **This asks for about 12.9 ac; the site is 4.7 ac**, with **Edit request** to change it.
- **Sort** by **Recommended** (the default: the AI's order, or the planner's), **Profit**, **Yield on cost**, **AI score**, **Units / Floor area**, **Parking ratio** or **Open space**. Layouts that fail a check always come after the ones that pass, the figure you sorted by is highlighted on each card, and money shows a second decimal when two layouts would otherwise read the same. The layout on the map stays marked whatever the order.
- **Layouts stay with the option:** switch to another option, close the plan or reload the page and they're still listed, with your request and the AI's note. Up to six are kept: the first three and the one on the map in full, the rest as numbers only (**Numbers only: click to lay it out again**). If one comes out differently when it's laid out again, it says so. Teammates see the same layouts as numbers only (the one on the map comes with the option), and a browser that runs out of room keeps the layouts of options you aren't working on as numbers only, so the save always goes through.
- **Save as new option** on any layout keeps it as a new option in the same project, named after it (**Option B · Rows facing the street…**). You stay in the option you're in; the message offers **Open Option B**.
- **More layouts…** lists every usable layout the planner found (up to 12), keeping the AI's scores on the ones it saw. When the layouts were reviewed by the AI, the new ones are reviewed too before they're shown (up to ten, scored on the same scale as its choice), and only those it rated join the list; while it looks, the list stays as it was. If that review doesn't come back, the new layouts are shown anyway with a note that the AI didn't review them. If the layout on the map isn't among them (the request changed since it was laid out), it stays at the top of the list, still marked **On map**. The drawings on the cards appear as you scroll to them, so a long list opens at once.
- **+ New Option**, after the options in the header, opens a short menu. It works with a mouse, a finger or the keyboard (arrows to move, Enter to pick, Esc to close).
  - **Blank site** (the first choice) makes a new option (Option B, C…) on the same site with nothing laid out: the same lot outline, front, street sides, setbacks, parcels, zoning and easements, but no buildings, parking, layouts, request or renderings, and the program back to the start. It opens with the Build box ready, so you can type what to try. The land price you set in the Pro forma comes along; the rest of the Pro forma starts from the usual numbers.
  - **Copy of (this option)** copies the option you're in, its layouts included, with an empty Build box (its renderings stay with the original).
  - **Clear (this option) and start over**, set apart under the other two, takes everything laid out off the option you're in and keeps the site, leaving the Build box empty and ready. Nothing asks first: the message **Cleared Option A · Undo** (or Undo in the header) brings it all back, layouts included. The option's renderings stay; they say **plan changed since**.
  - **Delete (this option)**, under Clear, deletes the option you're in (as does **Delete This Option** at the bottom of the panel). Nothing asks first: the next option opens (after the last one, the project page) and the message **Deleted Option A from (the project) · Undo** brings it back exactly where it was for about 8 seconds. For a project shared with the team it says **(for the team too)**. Its renderings on this device are removed once Undo has passed. While a build is running it waits, like the other choices.
  - Each project keeps all its options. On the Projects page, **New Option** makes the same blank site, even after its last option was deleted.
- **Profit after hold & sale** is the one profit figure, on the layout cards and in the Pro forma: what the equity investors net, with the rent collected through the hold, the loan payments and the sale after sale costs counted, less the equity put in. Hover it for the same words. It isn't the stabilized value minus the cost, so the two won't subtract to it.

**Asking about a plan**
- **Ask about this plan** (the **Ask** chip, right after Change) answers questions about the plan that's open, in plain words: "why do we need so much parking?", "why is there a detention pond (or not)?", "how does the pro forma work out?", "why did you put the clubhouse there?". Before you've asked anything, four questions to try are offered; click one to ask it. Type your own and press **Enter** (Shift+Enter for a new line) or **Ask**.
- The AI answers from this plan's own numbers, the ones the panel already shows: the request and how it was read, the setbacks and zoning if known, parking needed and provided and the rule each use is counted by, how much stormwater storage the site needs and what the pond holds, coverage and open space, the checks, the Pro forma's numbers and assumptions, where each piece sits, and the notes and AI review from the last build. On a commercial site it also has the AI's plan from the last build, with its reason for each decision and what the layout engine changed, so "why did you put the pond there?" or "why two pads?" gets the AI's own answer, and it says whether the layout on the map is the AI's plan or one of the engine's. It doesn't make up numbers: when the plan doesn't record something (why a piece is exactly where it is, a city's own rule), it says so and what would settle it. It also says when a number is a typical starting assumption rather than a city requirement, and where to change it.
- Answers are a few sentences or a short list. While it works a quiet **Thinking** line shows under your question. After an answer, a couple of follow-up questions are offered. The last few questions and answers stay while the plan is open (they aren't saved); **Clear** forgets them. It uses the effort level picked under Build.

**New settings and your edits**
- **Nothing you've edited is replaced without asking.** When the layout on the map has your changes, **Build Layouts**, **Use this layout**, **New layouts from these settings** and the AI's **Use it** first ask, at the top of the panel: "Your edits to Layout 2 will be replaced", with **Replace my edits**, **Save my edits as a new option first** or **Cancel** (Esc). Undo brings the edits back after replacing.
- Changing a site parameter lays out new layouts with the new settings and puts the same arrangement back on the map. If that arrangement no longer fits, the nearest one is shown and the message says so ("Layout 2 no longer fits; showing the nearest"). The message says what changed for the layout on the map, for example **Front setback 35 ft: 22,800 sf (−2,200), 104/92 spaces**, with **Undo**, which takes back both the new layout and the setting you changed.
- **Your edits are never replaced silently.** When the layout on the map has your changes, nothing is redrawn; a bar under Site parameters says **Settings changed** and offers **Re-fit around my changes** (the pieces you placed stay, the rest is laid out again with the new settings) or **New layouts (replaces your edits)**. Undo brings the edits back either way.
- **New layouts from these settings** (at the bottom of Site parameters) does it on demand; it replaces the layout on the map, edits included. Untick **New layouts as I change settings** to change several settings first.
- When new settings replace layouts the AI had scored, the list says **Settings changed: AI review cleared** with **Review again**.
- **Changing the site or its street side makes the layouts out of date.** After **Redraw Site**, adding or removing parcels, **Street Side**, **Side Street** or **Add Entrance**, a bar at the top of the panel says **Layouts out of date** with **Re-fit Around My Changes** (everything else is laid out again for the new site, keeping the pieces you placed) and **New Layouts**. The same update is one click on the gold icon in the top right corner of the dark layout card; the icon and the bar go away once the layout is up to date. Every layout card says **Out of date · site changed** instead of **Passes**, and **Use this layout** on one fits it to the new site instead of putting the old drawing back. The zone lines of the old site are cleared from the map. Undo takes the change, and the bar, away.

**Pro forma**
- **Numbers** shows the plan as one set of tiles (site, gross building sf, leasable / rentable sf, hotel keys, units, coverage, FAR, parking, detention, paving, open space, height), each zoning limit beside its figure (**FAR 0.12 / max 0.5**, in red when over). With a Phase 2, floor area, units, coverage and FAR show **Phase 1** and **Full site** side by side.
- **The three returns the team underwrites on come first:** **Levered IRR**, **Equity multiple** (with the equity put in) and **Cash-on-cash** (the first stabilized year, with the average over the hold), over a **10-year hold** after the project is stabilized. Under them, **Cash-on-cash by year** shows each year of the hold: that year's net operating income less the permanent loan's payments, divided by the equity put in. Rents grow each year, so it usually climbs. Change the hold under Assumptions (**Hold after stabilizing**).
- Opens under the plan and reads its buildings, units by type, homes, parking, paving, detention and site area. It shows the total cost (and per unit), stabilized NOI, yield on cost and spread over the market cap rate, stabilized value, profit, unlevered and levered IRR, equity multiple, peak equity and debt coverage, with the budget and the stabilized operating statement.
- **Gross and leasable apart.** An **Area** table shows each use's **gross building sf** and its **leasable / rentable sf**, with how the second is counted, and the rent and the yield are worked out on the leasable figure, never the gross:
  - shops and restaurants: about 97% of the building, leased triple net (NNN);
  - offices and medical offices: the usable share of the building (about 85%) times a load factor for the shared lobbies, halls and restrooms (about 1.12 for offices, 1.15 for medical);
  - apartments: the units' net square feet from the unit mix;
  - self storage: the units' rentable square feet;
  - a hotel: its keys (rooms), with the rate, occupancy and the hotel's own costs;
  - a church: usually built for its own congregation, so it shows a budget and no yield until you enter a rent.
  The shares are editable under Assumptions, and Numbers follows them.
- **Triple net means triple net:** shop, office and warehouse tenants pay back their share of the property taxes, insurance and common-area costs (95% of it by default), so those don't come out of the owner's income twice.
- **Site work follows what's built,** not the size of the lot: site prep for the buildings, paving and ponds (plus a margin for their slopes and buffers), drives priced by the square foot, parking by the space, a detention pond by the acre-foot it holds, underground storage by the acre-foot, a parking garage by the space, and courts, pools and playgrounds by their size. Land you're not using doesn't add site work; it only adds to the land price.
- **Every number is a market assumption, not verified.** The defaults are typical 2026 Houston, Katy and Waller numbers with their basis, and the section says so (**Market assumption, not verified**). Hover a box to see whether it's the default or your own number. Nothing in the pro forma is an appraisal, a quote or a promise of a yield.
- **Notes when a number looks off.** A number outside the usual range here keeps working but gets a calm note under its box, for example **Retail rent $48/sf NNN is above the usual $18–35 here; check it.** The results get a note too when the yield on cost is under 5% or over 12%, a cap rate is outside 4.5–9%, the development spread is negative (worth less than it costs to build) or the permanent loan's debt coverage is under 1.2×.
- **The schedule** in months: design and permits, construction, lease-up (or sales for homes) and the hold, drawn as a bar. The months are estimated from the size of the job (for example 9 months of design and permits, about 12 months plus one month per 30 apartments of construction, and lease-up at about 20 apartments a month) and can be changed.
- **Two methods.** *Month by month* (the default): land at closing, soft and hard costs spread through design and construction, equity first and then a construction loan with its interest, lease-up from empty to stabilized, refinancing into a permanent loan, and a sale at the end of the hold. *TestFit method*: the one-page annual sheet TestFit's custom pro forma uses, reproduced exactly; it puts all the money in at year 0 and starts full rents in year 1, so it reads higher, and the panel shows both.
- Assumptions to change: land price (the parcels' appraised value when the county has it, otherwise an estimate an acre for the use), hard cost per gross square foot for each use (climate-controlled and drive-up storage apart), the leasable shares and load factors, site prep, paving, parking a space, a parking garage a space, the detention pond and underground storage (about $30,000 and $420,000 an acre-foot), lot development for homes, tenant improvements and leasing commissions for leased space, soft costs, contingency and developer fee, rents by unit type (or per home, per leasable square foot NNN for commercial, per square foot a month for storage, or a hotel's rate and occupancy), other income, vacancy and credit loss, operating costs, the share billed back to NNN tenants, the management fee, the property tax rate and taxable share, loan-to-cost and rates, cap rates, rent and expense growth, the hold, and the schedule. Homes can be **for sale** instead of for rent (price per square foot and sales pace).
- Assumption boxes only take sensible numbers: the exit and market cap rates 0.5–20, vacancy, soft costs, contingency, developer fee and loan to cost 0–100, the land price 0 or more. Anything else isn't applied and the box says **Enter 0–100** (or the range). The land price shows with commas, and its "appraised value" note goes once you set your own price. A return that can't be worked out reads **n/a** (never "Infinityx"), and money over a billion reads **$1.35B**.
- **Pro Forma (Excel):** a workbook that opens on **Returns** (levered IRR, equity multiple, cash-on-cash in the first year and on average, then the hold year by year: NOI, debt service, cash flow and cash-on-cash), then the assumptions, the one-page pro forma with live formulas, the month-by-month cash flows with IRR formulas, and the budget with the gross and leasable areas.
- The default numbers are typical 2026 Houston, Katy and Waller starting points, not quotes. With them, new garden apartments, rented homes, suburban offices and hotels often show a thin yield or a negative spread: that's the market today, not a fault in the numbers.

**Renderings**
- Once an option is laid out, the **Render** section of the plan panel makes a photoreal picture of it. Pick:
  - **View:** **From the corner** (the usual marketing aerial), **From the street**, **Low, at the entry**, **From the back**, **From the left side**, **From the right side**, **High overhead** (a steep aerial that reads almost like the plan), **Top down** (straight down, like a drone survey photo), **All site views** (corner, street, entry and back, one after another), or **Building close-up** (below). High overhead and Top down turn the picture so a long or angled site fills it.
  - **Materials:** to suit each building's use, or brick and stone, stucco and stone, modern glass and metal, tilt-wall concrete.
  - **Light:** midday, early morning, overcast, late afternoon, sunset, dusk or night.
  - **Surroundings:** **Real surroundings** (the roads, lots and buildings really there, from aerial imagery, whatever base map the page shows), **Typical surroundings** (the AI draws believable suburban surroundings from the street map) or **The site only** (the site alone on a plain ground, like a presentation model).
  - **Resolution:** **HD** (2048 × 1152), **2K** (2560 × 1440) or **4K** (3840 × 2160, true 4K). 2K and 4K need GPT Image 2 or newer as the engine (an older one sends 1536 × 1024); 4K takes two to four minutes, costs about twice as much as HD, and OpenAI calls it experimental.
  - **Detail:** **Best** (the default, the most faithful to the massing), **Standard** (good detail for about a quarter of the price) or **Draft** (a quick, cheap look).
  - **Engine:** which OpenAI image model paints it, from the ones the site's key offers (for example GPT Image 2 for 4K). **Default** uses the site's choice. It's the same pick as Settings › AI model › Renderings.
  Then **Render 3D View**.
- **Preview Massing** (beside the button) takes the massing of every view, with the surroundings picked, exactly as the AI would get it, free (no AI), and shows them as small pictures as they come. Click one to pick that view; its corner button shows it large. Change the plan or the surroundings and press it again for fresh ones.
- How it works: the map points its 3D model of the plan (buildings at their heights, the paving, pond and trees) at the site from that view, takes a picture of it with the whole parcel in the frame and room around it, and the AI (GPT Image 2.5 Sunburst where the key has it, else the newest GPT Image model it can use) paints that picture as a photograph. The buildings, drives, parking rows, pond and trees stay where the plan puts them, at their heights and shapes; the materials, windows, landscaping, cars, people, sky and the surroundings are the AI's. A picture takes about a minute (more at 2K and 4K) and comes out at the resolution picked, or the nearest size the engine offers (it used to ask for 1920 × 1080, a size the image models don't take, so pictures came back at 1536 × 1024); the panel shows which step it's on, and **Cancel** stops it (a picture the AI had already started is dropped, not kept). Nothing else on the map moves: the view goes back to where it was. If you close the plan, open another option or go to another page while a picture is being painted, it still arrives and is kept with the option it was taken of ("Rendering of Option A ready"); with **All site views**, the views not yet started are skipped.
- **Building close-up** is a hero shot of one building: a closer aerial from the street corner with the whole building in the picture and some of its parking and landscaping around it (neighbouring buildings may be cut off at the edges). The choice names the building it will frame, for example **Building close-up · Building A**. To pick the building, select it on the plan first (the choice then says **(selected)**); with nothing selected it frames the building nearest the street. **All site views** doesn't include it. Its pictures are titled with the building's name in the list, the full-size view and on the PDF.
- The pictures appear under the button, newest first. Click one to see it full size (its top line says **plan changed since this was made** when the plan has moved on), **Show the massing** to compare it with the 3D model it was painted from, **Download** it (JPEG) or **Delete** it. When the plan changes after a picture was made, it says **plan changed since** and goes grey.
- **They are concept illustrations, not designs.** Placement, counts and heights follow the plan; everything else is made up, and two renderings of one plan will differ. Check a picture against the massing before showing it to anyone; if a building came out wrong, render again.
- **They open on any computer.** Each rendering, and the massing it was painted from, is kept in the team's storage as it's made, so it shows on every computer and for every teammate, not only on the one that made it. Renderings made before Oct 24 were kept only on the computer that made them: open the project once on that computer and they're shared too. Until then they say **Only on the computer that made it** instead of the old "Not on this device". Deleting a rendering removes the team's copy as well. The newest sixteen are kept per option.
- Renderings count toward the AI's use in Settings › AI model › **Usage** under **Renderings**; the price of the image model can be set there when the app doesn't know it.
- **A monthly budget for renderings.** The administrators set how much renderings may cost each calendar month ($100 a month to start), or turn renderings off (the line then says so). A line under **Render 3D View** shows how much of it has been used and how many pictures were made (it says "about" when the image model's price is only estimated). When the budget is spent, the button is paused with a note, and rendering starts again on the 1st of the next month. Ask AI and site plan builds are not affected.

**Exports**
- **Site Plan PDF:** a conceptual site plan sheet on **11 × 17** or **24 × 36** paper:
  - the plan to a standard scale (1" = 50', 100', 150'…), turned so the street is at the bottom, with a north arrow and scale bar;
  - a vicinity map, the project and sheet title, the program at a glance, and the basis of design;
  - tables for site data and land use (by zone), development program and parking, and the parcels (address, owner, acres);

## Where the data comes from

The **?** button at the top right (Data & help; on phones, More → Data sources & freshness) lists every source, what it's used for, and how fresh it is. It reads the market and census data itself, so the rows say **Checking…** for a moment and then show their real status, even if you haven't opened Market yet. A line at the top says whether sources show on cards, with a link to change it in Settings and **Save voice log**. **Getting started** at the top right shows the first-visit tip again on the Map. The main ones are:

- **Parcels and owners:** county appraisal districts through the Texas GIO parcel service (free), and Regrid (paid, capped).
- **People, homes and jobs:** US Census American Community Survey (5-year averages) and Census jobs data (about two years behind).
- **Flood:** FEMA. **Traffic counts:** TxDOT. **Crime:** Houston Police (street level) and the FBI (yearly, by police department, nationwide). **Buildings:** OpenStreetMap.
- **Environment:** Texas Commission on Environmental Quality, US EPA, Railroad Commission of Texas (wells and pipelines), US Fish & Wildlife Service (wetlands) and USDA (soils).
- **Utilities:** OpenStreetMap (power lines and substations), the Public Utility Commission of Texas service-area (CCN) maps through Harris County and the Texas Water Development Board, and the City of Houston (public sewer and water mains). The federal transmission-line map (HIFLD) was taken offline in 2025, so it isn't used. Exact pipe locations, sizes and capacity come from the utility or district engineer.
- **Company owners:** Texas Comptroller franchise-tax records and the Secretary of State.
- **Google (paid, capped each month):** Google base maps, 3D city and place search, when the site's Google keys are set. A monthly allowance keeps place searches inside Google's free monthly use; past it, search uses the free sources until the next month.
- **Businesses and places:** Overture Maps and Foursquare's free open datasets, reloaded monthly (about 300,000 in the seven counties), plus OpenStreetMap.
- **Construction filings:** TDLR TABS, refreshed nightly.
- **Development pipeline (nightly):** Harris Central Appraisal District (building permits, read when its weekly files change), City of Houston Plat Tracker, Fort Bend County and the Montgomery, Waller, Austin and Washington appraisal districts (subdivisions), TxDOT Project Tracker, TCEQ water districts and the Texas Bond Review Board (MUDs, tax rates and bonds), and the Texas Education Agency (schools).
- **Markets:** Yahoo Finance (stocks, funds, indexes and crypto history; delayed), Coinbase (live crypto) and the St. Louis Fed's FRED (oil, gas, building material prices, rates and Houston metro statistics).
- **Weather, radar and storms:** NOAA and Open-Meteo. **Planes:** adsb.lol and airplanes.live. **Airports:** OurAirports and the FAA.
- **County and state lines:** US Census.

## Good to know

- **No sale prices.** Texas doesn't publish what properties sell for. Regrid has some recorded sales, and rent and sales comps would need a paid data provider.
- **Filings are filer estimates.** Costs and dates come from whoever filed. About four in ten filings are missing a start or end date, and the app estimates those (shown hatched). AI-read fields such as use, tenant and developer can be wrong.
- **Coverage gaps:**
  - Harris and Waller aren't in the free state parcel data; their owner and value come from each county's appraisal district instead. Harris records have no year built or acquisition date (Regrid details can fill in more).
  - Building permits are Harris County only. Fort Bend, Montgomery, Waller, Austin, Washington, Grimes and their cities don't publish permits as open data. The Harris permits are the ones the appraisal district records, so they lag the city by a few weeks and don't list values or contractors.
  - Plat dates exist for the City of Houston (city and ETJ) and Fort Bend only. Grimes publishes no subdivision names. Water district creation dates are read a few hundred at a time, so for the first few nights some districts say "Checking".
  - School enrollment is TEA's latest yearly count; enrollment forecasts aren't public.
  - Street-level crime covers the City of Houston only. Elsewhere crime is yearly and department-wide (FBI), so it can't show which blocks are worse.
  - FBI crime figures come out once the year is over; the newest full year is shown. Some departments report only part of a year or have stopped reporting, and the card says so. Rates count residents only, so places with many visitors (downtowns, shopping areas) read high.
  - Business lists come from open data: most businesses are there, but some are missing, closed or slightly misplaced. They are refreshed monthly.
- **Numbers lag.** Census averages cover five years, jobs data is about two years old, and permits arrive about six weeks after the month.
- **Shared notes have no sign-in.** Anyone with the site address can edit them, unless a team passcode is set.
- **Performance.** With many layers, live planes and 3D on at once, older phones and laptops can still slow down. Selecting large areas, following planes, and toggling layers were made much faster in October 2026.
